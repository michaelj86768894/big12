DRAFT NIGHT: Winners, Losers & The Moves That Changed the GLFL

By Ethan Calloway | GLFL National/League Correspondent • September 3, 2026

The 2026 GLFL Draft is in the books. Some teams got better. Some got weird. And at least one GM appears to have mistaken the draft for a defensive coordinator's meeting.

The 2026 Great Lakes Football League draft is officially in the books.

Three hundred selections.

Twelve franchises.

A whole lot of linebackers.

And, as always, several decisions that will have fantasy football people arguing until the opening kickoff.

The first eight rounds belonged to the draft. After that, the familiar names began reappearing as teams protected the stars that carried them through 2025. But while the keeper portion of the draft provided plenty of established firepower, the opening rounds told us something potentially more important:

What each general manager thinks his team needs to become a championship team.

And that's where things got interesting.

🏆 DRAFT WINNER: MIAMI INFERNO

Grade: A

Richard Head came into this draft with a simple problem.

Miami went 3–11 in 2025.

The solution?

Apparently, attack everything.

The Inferno opened with Jordyn Brooks, followed by Nick Emmanwori, Will Anderson Jr., Jonathon Brooks and Tank Dell.

That's an impressive combination of immediate upside and positional aggression.

The first three selections give Miami a potentially nasty defensive foundation, while Jonathon Brooks and Tank Dell address two skill-position groups that needed more juice.

Then there's the keeper core.

Drake Maye. Chase Brown. Deebo Samuel. Jerry Jeudy. Bucky Irving. Mark Andrews. Quinshon Judkins. Tyler Warren.

Miami didn't need to completely rebuild.

It needed to stop being a 3–11 team.

This draft looks like an attempt to do exactly that.

GLFN Analysis: Miami may not have drafted the flashiest collection of names, but this is one of the clearest examples of a team drafting with a specific objective: become significantly harder to beat.

For a franchise with three championships in its history, "significantly harder to beat" feels like a reasonable starting point.

🐴 DRAFT WINNER: MILWAUKEE MUSTANGS

Grade: A-

Michael Bautch may have caused several fantasy analysts to briefly question whether the GLFL had changed its scoring system to award points for linebackers.

Milwaukee opened:

Jadarian Price
Makai Lemon
Alex Singleton
Tremaine Edmunds
DeMarvion Overshown
Azeez Al-Shaair
Michael Mayer
Luke Schoonmaker

Six of the first eight selections were linebackers or tight ends.

That is... certainly a strategy.

But there's method behind the madness.

Milwaukee's keeper foundation was already loaded with offensive firepower:

CeeDee Lamb. Saquon Barkley. A.J. Brown. Breece Hall. T.J. Hockenson. Jared Goff.

The Mustangs didn't need to spend their early draft capital chasing another superstar offensive name.

Instead, they attacked depth and defensive production.

The question is whether Milwaukee drafted too aggressively toward IDP depth.

If Singleton, Edmunds, Overshown and Al-Shaair all hit, this could look brilliant by October.

If not?

Well...

Tommy Gallagher is going to have some explaining to do.

⚡ DRAFT WINNER: BROOKLYN BOLTS

Grade: A-

Two-time champion Cory Colvin wasn't interested in subtlety.

He got Malik Nabers with the second overall pick.

Then:

Kyle Hamilton. Nick Bosa. Dallas Turner. Kenyon Sadiq. Malik Willis. Robert Spillane.

That's a very Brooklyn draft.

After a disappointing 4–10 season in 2025, the Bolts needed impact.

They got it.

Nabers immediately gives the offense another explosive weapon alongside Davante Adams, Brian Thomas Jr. and Xavier Worthy.

Hamilton and Bosa add elite defensive upside.

And suddenly Brooklyn's keeper foundation looks a lot more interesting than the 2025 record suggested.

The biggest question is quarterback.

Brooklyn kept Joe Burrow, but also drafted Malik Willis before eventually returning to the keeper portion with Jaxson Dart and Burrow.

That tells you everything you need to know.

Cory Colvin isn't comfortable standing still.

And that's probably exactly what Brooklyn needed.

🟣 DRAFT WINNER: CLEVELAND GLADIATORS

Grade: A-

Cleveland's draft was almost aggressively sensible.

Fred Warner. Nick Bolton. Danielle Hunter. Kyler Murray. Calvin Ridley. Jalen Nailor. Talanoa Hufanga.

No panic.

No bizarre reaches.

Just a steady attempt to add proven production around an already exciting young core.

The keeper list tells the rest of the story:

Ashton Jeanty. Puka Nacua. De'Von Achane. Rome Odunze. Bo Nix. Tucker Kraft.

Josh Thurmer clearly believes the foundation is already there.

Now he needs to turn potential into wins.

The 2025 record was only 6–8, and Cleveland has never won a GLFL championship.

At some point, "young and talented" stops being an excuse.

This draft suggests Cleveland is getting closer to that point.

📈 BIGGEST RISER: PHILADELPHIA SOUL

Grade: B+

Derek Thompson is going to love this one.

Philadelphia opened with Jeremiyah Love, followed by Keaton Mitchell, Tykee Smith, Micah Parsons, Kamari Lassiter, Dontayvion Wicks and Quentin Lake.

That's a lot of athleticism.

More importantly, the Soul added help on both sides of the ball without abandoning their existing offensive foundation.

Bijan Robinson. Garrett Wilson. Terry McLaurin. Jameson Williams. Chris Olave. Emeka Egbuka. Sam LaPorta. C.J. Stroud.

There is talent everywhere.

The problem is that talent hasn't translated into postseason advancement.

Philadelphia has made the playoffs five times and has only three playoff victories.

At some point, the story has to change from:

"They have a really good roster."

to:

"They finally won something."

This draft gives Brian Simmons another opportunity to make that happen.

🔥 BEST VALUE: TUCSON SUGAR SKULLS

Grade: B+

Tucson didn't have the luxury of drafting near the top.

They didn't need it.

Mason Bautch opened with Carnell Tate, then added Nate Landman, Ernest Jones IV, Caleb Downs, Ty Johnson, Sonny Styles and DaRon Bland.

Meanwhile, the keeper portion brought back a terrifying collection of established talent.

Jahmyr Gibbs. Justin Jefferson. Brock Bowers. Patrick Mahomes. Jaxon Smith-Njigba. James Cook. Brian Thomas Jr.

That's a championship roster.

Which is why Alex Ramirez will probably tell you this draft wasn't good enough.

That's the Tucson standard now.

The Sugar Skulls went 12–2 in 2025 and still didn't win the championship.

A lesser franchise might call that a great season.

Tucson calls it unfinished business.

🐾 LOS ANGELES: QUIETLY VERY GOOD

Grade: B+

Sarah Bennett probably doesn't want the word "quiet" anywhere near this story.

Los Angeles opened with:

Bhayshul Tuten. MarShawn Lloyd. Quay Walker. Roquan Smith. T.J. Watt. Edgerrin Cooper. T.J. Edwards.

That's a lot of defensive production.

And then you remember the keeper list.

Josh Allen. Amon-Ra St. Brown. Trey McBride. Josh Jacobs. Travis Hunter. Jayden Reed.

Suddenly this looks like one of the deeper rosters in the league.

The Wildcats went 9–5 in 2025, won a playoff game and then fell to Tucson.

The roster isn't the question.

The postseason finish is.

Again.

🟠 NORTHERN ARIZONA: THE "IS THIS FINALLY THE YEAR?" DRAFT

Grade: B

Jake Callahan has officially entered his favorite portion of the calendar.

Northern Arizona drafted:

Jack Campbell. Nakobe Dean. Tuli Tuipulotu. KC Concepcion. Terrance Ferguson. Mike Washington Jr.

Then came the familiar stars:

Christian McCaffrey. Tee Higgins. Jaxon Smith-Njigba. Breece Hall. Jordan Love.

This roster has enough upside to make people believe.

Which is precisely what Northern Arizona has been doing for years.

The Wranglers have five playoff appearances.

Three playoff wins.

No championship.

At some point, the question has to be answered.

Is this finally the year?

🐻 CHICAGO: DON'T CALL IT A REBUILD

Grade: B

Chicago took Cam Skattebo fifth overall, then went heavily into defense with Cedric Gray, Omar Speights and Jamien Sherwood.

And then they basically said:

"We're keeping everything else."

Because Chicago's keeper list is ridiculous.

Lamar Jackson. Alvin Kamara. George Kittle. TreVeyon Henderson. DK Metcalf. Jaylen Waddle. George Pickens. Christian Watson. Rhamondre Stevenson. Dak Prescott. Mike Evans. Travis Kelce.

The problem?

Chicago went 7–7 in 2025 and missed the playoffs.

Marcus Reed is therefore permitted to ask the obvious question:

If this team is this talented...

why doesn't it have a championship?

🐾 BOSTON: DEFENSE, DEFENSE AND MORE DEFENSE

Grade: B

The defending champions made one thing abundantly clear.

They're not planning on winning pretty.

Boston opened with:

Myles Garrett. Brian Burns. Alex Highsmith. Jalen McMillan. Denzel Boston. Derwin James Jr.

Three defensive linemen in the first three rounds.

Then Derwin James.

That is a rather aggressive way of telling the league:

"Good luck scoring."

And then Boston gets to bring back:

Jonathan Taylor. Tyreek Hill. Jayden Daniels. Kyren Williams. Omarion Hampton. Zay Flowers. Marvin Harrison Jr.

So, yes.

The defending champions are still pretty good.

Jack Callahan's job just became considerably easier.

🟡 VEGAS: THE MOST CONFUSING DRAFT?

Grade: B-

Let's review.

Vegas has:

Ja'Marr Chase. Malik Nabers. Derrick Henry. Marvin Harrison Jr. Tua Tagovailoa.

Those are five names that should make a fantasy roster terrifying.

Vegas went 4–10 in 2025.

So what did Dave Nguyen do?

He opened the draft with:

Blake Cashman. Jeffery Simmons. Andrew Van Ginkel. J.K. Dobbins. Keenan Allen. Jordyn Tyson. Chase Young. Devin Bush.

There is plenty to like.

But the central Vegas question remains unanswered:

How did a team with Ja'Marr Chase, Malik Nabers and Derrick Henry go 4–10?

The draft didn't erase that question.

It merely gave Cole Maddox another eight months to ask it.

🟢 CEDAR RAPIDS: BUILDING THE DEPTH

Grade: B

Cedar Rapids went after proven defensive production early:

Maxx Crosby. Carson Schwesinger. Aidan Hutchinson.

Then added De'Zhaun Stribling, Jaylin Noel, Jonah Coleman, Zack Baun and Najee Harris.

The Titans already have a strong keeper foundation:

Nico Collins. Ladd McConkey. Jalen Hurts. James Conner. DeVonta Smith. Dalton Kincaid.

The issue isn't talent.

It's what happens when the calendar flips to the postseason.

Cedar Rapids made the playoffs in 2025 and lost to Los Angeles.

That continues the larger question around this franchise:

Can Mitch Smith turn regular-season competence into postseason success?

📉 DRAFT LOSER: NOBODY REALLY LOST — BUT...

There wasn't a catastrophic draft.

That's important.

Nobody left the draft with a roster that suddenly looks hopeless.

But if we're handing out the "What exactly was the plan here?" award, Milwaukee and Vegas are going to have a conversation.

Milwaukee's linebacker collection is either brilliant or completely insane.

Vegas spent an enormous amount of early capital on defense while already possessing arguably the most explosive offensive trio in the league.

And Boston's first three selections were all defensive linemen.

The beautiful thing?

We don't have to decide whether these strategies worked yet.

That's what the season is for.

🏆 WAY-TOO-EARLY CHAMPIONSHIP OUTLOOK

If the season started tomorrow, the defending champion Boston Brawlers would still have to be considered a favorite.

But Tucson isn't going anywhere.

Neither is Milwaukee.

Los Angeles remains dangerous.

And Miami may be the team nobody wants to play if this draft produces the kind of turnaround Richard Head is clearly hoping for.

Then there's Brooklyn.

A two-time champion coming off a 4–10 season just added Malik Nabers, Kyle Hamilton and Nick Bosa.

That doesn't look like a franchise interested in rebuilding.

It looks like a franchise interested in reminding everybody who used to run this league.

🗣️ THE EARLY GLFL STORYLINES

  • Can Boston repeat?
    • The defending champs kept the core and loaded up on defense.
  • Can Tucson finish the job?
    • A 12–2 season followed by another championship-less offseason isn't going to satisfy anybody in the desert.
  • Is Miami back?
    • Three championships are sitting in the franchise history book. A 3–11 season is sitting in the recent one. This draft could be the beginning of the comeback.
  • What the hell is Milwaukee doing?
    • Seriously. Six linebackers/tight ends among the first eight selections is either genius or the most elaborate practical joke of the 2026 draft.
  • Can Brooklyn return to the top?
    • Cory Colvin has two championships. He didn't draft like a 4–10 team.
  • Is this finally the year for Northern Arizona?
    • Jake Callahan has already started typing the headline.
  • Why can't Vegas win?
    • Cole Maddox has approximately 4,000 words prepared on this subject.

THE BOTTOM LINE

The 2026 GLFL Draft didn't produce one overwhelming winner.

It produced something more interesting.

It produced questions.

Boston wants to prove 2025 wasn't a one-year miracle.

Tucson wants revenge.

Milwaukee wants another championship.

Brooklyn wants its old identity back.

Miami wants to become dangerous again.

Cleveland wants its young stars to grow up.

Northern Arizona wants its breakthrough.

Philadelphia wants its optimism to finally become reality.

Vegas wants its stars to actually win games.

And Chicago, Cedar Rapids and Los Angeles are all trying to prove that talent alone isn't the problem.

The draft is over.

The grades are filed.

The arguments have begun.

Now we find out which general managers actually knew what they were doing.
