GLFL WEEK 8 PREVIEW: Milwaukee Seeks Playoff Revenge, Vegas Eyes Four Straight and Tucson Faces a Defining Test

By Julian Mercer | GLFL National • October 29, 2026

The GLFL trade market has exploded just as the season reaches its midpoint. Vegas has acquired wide receiver Josh Downs from Miami, while Cleveland and Boston have completed a four-player deal that could reshape both rosters. Meanwhile, Milwaukee prepares for a rematch against the defending champions who ended its playoff run last season, Tucson desperately needs to stop its slide, and Los Angeles GM Elizabeth Bautch is still chasing career victory No. 100. With seven weeks remaining in the regular season, Week 8 is shaping up to be one of the most intriguing weekends of the year.

Seven weeks are complete.

Seven remain in the regular season.

And the race for seven playoff spots is becoming increasingly intense.

Milwaukee has separated itself from the field at 6-1, riding six consecutive victories and sitting comfortably atop the GLFL Power Rankings.

But the Mustangs haven't forgotten what happened the last time they faced Boston.

Meanwhile, Vegas has transformed from an 0-4 disaster into one of the league's hottest teams. Northern Arizona is surging, Miami is back among the elite, and Cleveland has quietly won three straight.

Then there's Tucson.

The Sugar Skulls remain No. 2 in the Power Rankings, but they've lost three of four games, including consecutive blowouts against Vegas and Milwaukee.

With reports of a possible Jahmyr Gibbs trade request circulating, the pressure on GM Mason Bautch is growing.

And apparently, nobody in the GLFL plans on standing still.

With Week 8 approaching, two more trades have been completed, sending six players to new teams and giving several playoff hopefuls a different look heading into the second half of the season.

Vegas Acquires Josh Downs From Miami

The Vegas Knight Hawks have acquired wide receiver Josh Downs from the Miami Inferno in exchange for running back J.K. Dobbins.

For Vegas GM Dave Nguyen, the move addresses a need at wide receiver as the Knight Hawks attempt to extend their three-game winning streak.

Vegas has already benefited tremendously from its recent trade with Tucson, acquiring Jaxon Smith-Njigba and Trey McBride.

Now Nguyen has added another receiving option in Downs, giving the Knight Hawks additional flexibility as they continue their remarkable turnaround.

The move also suggests Vegas isn't satisfied with simply getting back into the playoff race.

After an 0-4 start, the Knight Hawks have won three straight and climbed to sixth in the Power Rankings.

They're making moves like a team that expects to keep climbing.

Miami, meanwhile, adds running back J.K. Dobbins to a roster already ranked third in the league.

Richard Head's Inferno are 5-2 and coming off a 199.20-point victory over Philadelphia.

The addition of Dobbins gives Miami another backfield option as the team attempts to keep pace with Milwaukee.

It's an intriguing deal between two teams with legitimate postseason ambitions.

Cleveland and Boston Complete Four-Player Deal

The second trade sends wide receiver Cooper Kupp and tight end Brenton Strange from Cleveland to Boston.

In return, the Gladiators acquire running back Kyle Monangai and tight end Dallas Goedert.

For Cleveland GM Josh Thurmer, the motivation is clear.

The Gladiators needed additional running back help, and Monangai provides another option for a team that has won three consecutive games.

Cleveland also adds Goedert at tight end while parting with Strange.

Boston GM Chris Cholka, meanwhile, brings in veteran receiver Cooper Kupp and tight end Brenton Strange as the defending champions prepare for one of their biggest matchups of the season.

The timing is particularly interesting.

Boston faces the league-leading Milwaukee Mustangs in Week 8, a rematch of last year's divisional playoff showdown.

Whether the newly acquired players make an immediate impact remains to be seen.

But the message from both front offices is unmistakable.

Neither team intends to stand still while the playoff race intensifies.

And with the GLFL trade market showing no signs of slowing down, these may not be the last roster changes before the weekend.

Week 8 offers six games and plenty of opportunities to change the narrative.

MILWAUKEE MUSTANGS (6-1) vs. BOSTON BRAWLERS (4-3)

Projected Score: Milwaukee 199.3 | Boston 184.8

Power Rankings: Milwaukee No. 1 | Boston No. 5

All-Time Series: Milwaukee leads 8-4

Revenge Is on the Schedule

If there is one game that deserves the spotlight this week, it's Milwaukee against Boston.

The Mustangs are the league's hottest team.

The Brawlers are the defending champions.

And the last time these franchises met, Boston ended Milwaukee's season.

Milwaukee has dominated the recent regular-season rivalry, winning five of the last six overall meetings.

But the one loss is the one that matters most.

Last year's divisional playoff round.

Boston advanced.

Milwaukee went home.

And the Brawlers eventually lifted the Lakefront Bowl trophy.

Now Michael Bautch gets another shot at Chris Cholka's defending champions, this time with the league's No. 1 team.

Milwaukee enters at 6-1 following a convincing 202.40-143.20 victory over Tucson.

The Mustangs have won six consecutive games and lead the league with a Power Index of 5706.24.

Their offense has demonstrated remarkable depth throughout the season, with Jared Goff, Javonte Williams, CeeDee Lamb and others providing consistent production.

Boston, meanwhile, is searching for answers after a disappointing 182.30-176.95 loss to Chicago.

The Brawlers had opportunities to win that game but left valuable points on the bench.

A lineup decision involving Adonai Mitchell and Marvin Harrison Jr. ultimately proved costly.

Now Boston faces a Milwaukee team that has been far less forgiving than Chicago.

Boston will also enter the matchup with a new-look roster following a trade with Cleveland. The Brawlers acquired Cooper Kupp and Brenton Strange in exchange for Kyle Monangai and Dallas Goedert. Whether either newcomer contributes immediately remains uncertain, but Chris Cholka clearly isn't content to rely solely on the roster that delivered last year's championship.

The projections favor Milwaukee 199.3-184.8, but the Brawlers have already demonstrated that they know how to beat the Mustangs when the stakes are highest.

This isn't just a regular-season matchup.

It's a measuring stick for both franchises.

Mercer's Take: Milwaukee has earned its No. 1 ranking, but Boston remains one of the few teams with recent postseason success against the Mustangs. If Michael Bautch wants to make a statement about this year's championship ambitions, beating the defending champions would be a powerful way to do it.

LOS ANGELES WILDCATS (2-5) vs. TUCSON SUGAR SKULLS (4-3)

Projected Score: Los Angeles 177.9 | Tucson 201.5

Power Rankings: Los Angeles No. 10 | Tucson No. 2

Can Tucson Stop the Bleeding?

The Sugar Skulls need a win.

Badly.

Tucson has lost three of its last four games, including a 216.20-157.60 defeat to Vegas and a 202.40-143.20 loss to Milwaukee.

Those aren't competitive defeats.

Those are warning signs.

The Sugar Skulls remain second in the Power Rankings thanks to their tremendous early-season scoring production, but recent results suggest this team is heading in the wrong direction.

Mason Bautch has been aggressive on the trade market, bringing in players such as Amon-Ra St. Brown, Derrick Henry and Ja'Marr Chase.

But the roster changes have yet to produce the consistent results Tucson expected.

And now the team is dealing with another distraction.

The Jahmyr Gibbs Situation

Earlier this week, GLFL Network reporter Alex Ramirez reported that star running back Jahmyr Gibbs had requested a trade from Tucson, reportedly telling GM Mason Bautch that he wanted to play for a contender.

The report remains unconfirmed, and Tucson has not announced a trade involving Gibbs.

But the timing is particularly uncomfortable.

Gibbs scored 24.30 points against Milwaukee and remains one of Tucson's most productive players.

If the Sugar Skulls continue losing, questions about the team's direction will only intensify.

Los Angeles presents an opportunity to regain momentum.

But the Wildcats have their own motivation.

Liz Bautch's Chase for 100

Los Angeles GM Elizabeth Bautch remains one victory away from her 100th career GLFL win.

She would become just the second GM in league history to reach that milestone, joining Milwaukee's Michael Bautch.

The Wildcats are 2-5 and coming off a 225.25-171.75 defeat to Northern Arizona.

But this roster has shown explosive potential.

Los Angeles scored 210.90 points in Week 6 against Philadelphia.

And with Tucson struggling, an upset isn't out of the question.

The projections favor Tucson 201.5-177.9.

But projections haven't meant much for the Sugar Skulls lately.

Mercer's Take: This is a defining game for Tucson. A victory would calm some of the criticism surrounding Mason Bautch. Another loss, especially against a 2-5 opponent, would raise serious questions about the Sugar Skulls' championship prospects. Meanwhile, Liz Bautch has a chance to make history and potentially derail Tucson's season in the process.

VEGAS KNIGHT HAWKS (3-4) vs. CHICAGO SLAUGHTER (2-5)

Projected Score: Vegas 183.1 | Chicago 170.9

Power Rankings: Vegas No. 6 | Chicago No. 11

All-Time Series: Chicago leads 11-4

The Hottest Team in the League?

Four weeks ago, Vegas looked finished.

The Knight Hawks were 0-4.

The offense was inconsistent.

And another disappointing season appeared inevitable.

Then Dave Nguyen made a blockbuster trade with Tucson.

And everything changed.

Vegas has won three consecutive games, including back-to-back victories over Tucson and Cedar Rapids.

The Knight Hawks scored 216.20 points against Tucson in Week 6 and 204.35 against Cedar Rapids in Week 7.

Jaxon Smith-Njigba and Trey McBride have been spectacular since arriving from Tucson.

Against Cedar Rapids, the duo combined for 70.40 points.

Through two games with Vegas, they've produced 121.60 combined fantasy points.

The trade has transformed the Knight Hawks' offense.

And Dave Nguyen isn't finished making moves. Vegas has now acquired Josh Downs from Miami in exchange for J.K. Dobbins, adding another receiver to an offense that has scored more than 200 points in consecutive weeks. With Smith-Njigba and McBride already producing at an elite level, Downs gives the Knight Hawks another potential weapon as they chase a fourth straight victory.

Vegas has climbed from ninth to sixth in the Power Rankings and now sits just one game below .500.

But Chicago isn't going to be an easy opponent.

The Slaughter are coming off an impressive 182.30-176.95 victory over defending champion Boston.

Dak Prescott and George Pickens combined for 63.10 points on Monday night to complete the comeback.

Chicago also holds a commanding 11-4 advantage in the all-time series.

However, eight of those victories came before 2018.

The rivalry has been much more competitive in recent years.

Vegas is projected to win 183.1-170.9.

And given the Knight Hawks' recent performances, that projection may actually be conservative.

Mercer's Take: Vegas is playing like a completely different team since acquiring Smith-Njigba and McBride. Chicago has the talent to make this competitive, but the Knight Hawks have momentum, confidence and an offense that suddenly looks capable of beating anyone. A fourth consecutive victory would officially put Vegas back in the playoff conversation.

MIAMI INFERNO (5-2) vs. BROOKLYN BOLTS (0-7)

Projected Score: Miami 184.2 | Brooklyn 141.7

Power Rankings: Miami No. 3 | Brooklyn No. 12

How Much More Can Brooklyn Take?

At some point, Brooklyn has to win a game.

Right?

The Bolts are 0-7.

They're the only winless team remaining in the GLFL.

And last week's loss to Cleveland may have been their most painful yet.

Brooklyn entered Sunday night leading 140.70-136.70 with Xavier Worthy remaining against Cleveland defensive player Nick Bolton.

Worthy scored 5.10.

Bolton scored 12.75.

Cleveland won 149.45-145.80.

Another opportunity disappeared.

Another week without a victory.

Now Cory Colvin's team faces Miami.

And the Inferno are not exactly an ideal opponent for a team desperately searching for its first win.

Miami is 5-2, ranked third in the Power Index and coming off a 199.20-point performance against Philadelphia.

Drake Maye scored 50.65 points last week, while Quinshon Judkins, Luther Burden III and Jaylen Warren all delivered strong performances.

Richard Head's team has emerged as one of the league's legitimate championship contenders.

Miami has also strengthened its running back options, acquiring J.K. Dobbins from Vegas in exchange for Josh Downs. For a 5-2 team already ranked third in the Power Index, the move suggests Richard Head is focused on building the depth necessary for a championship run.

Brooklyn, meanwhile, has struggled to generate consistent production outside of occasional individual explosions.

The projections favor Miami by 42.5 points.

That's the largest projected margin of any Week 8 matchup.

Brooklyn has already shown that it can remain competitive when unexpected performances break its way.

But against Miami, the Bolts will likely need their best game of the season.

Mercer's Take: Miami should win this game. The Inferno have the stronger record, deeper roster and significantly better scoring profile. But Brooklyn's desperation makes this matchup interesting. An 0-8 start would leave Cory Colvin with increasingly little reason to focus on anything beyond next season.

NORTHERN ARIZONA WRANGLERS (4-3) vs. CEDAR RAPIDS TITANS (5-2)

Projected Score: Northern Arizona 168.3 | Cedar Rapids 155.8

Power Rankings: Northern Arizona No. 4 | Cedar Rapids No. 7

Two Teams Heading in Opposite Directions

On paper, Cedar Rapids has the better record.

But recent results tell a very different story.

Northern Arizona enters Week 8 ranked fourth after producing the league's highest score last week.

The Wranglers erupted for 225.25 points against Los Angeles, winning by 53.50.

Jordan Love, Tee Higgins, KC Concepcion, Christian McCaffrey and Ollie Gordon II all delivered strong performances.

It was exactly the kind of response Jason Richmond needed after losing to Cleveland by just 0.30 points in Week 6.

Cedar Rapids, meanwhile, is trying to recover from one of the most disappointing performances of its season.

The Titans scored only 131.35 points against Vegas, losing by 73.00.

Their receiving corps combined for just 9.00 points.

And the defeat sent Cedar Rapids tumbling from third to seventh in the Power Rankings.

The Titans remain 5-2, tied with Miami for the second-best record in the league.

But the Power Index paints a more concerning picture.

Cedar Rapids' score of 3484.50 is more than 1,400 points behind Northern Arizona's 4916.73.

That's a significant difference between two teams separated by only one game in the standings.

The projections favor Northern Arizona 168.3-155.8.

But Cedar Rapids has demonstrated an ability to win games even when its scoring production isn't elite.

The question is whether that approach can survive against a Wranglers team capable of putting up 225 points.

Mercer's Take: Northern Arizona has the momentum and the stronger scoring profile. Cedar Rapids has the better record but needs to prove its Week 7 collapse was an isolated incident. This matchup could tell us whether the Titans are legitimate contenders or simply a team that benefited from a strong early-season start.

CLEVELAND GLADIATORS (4-3) vs. PHILADELPHIA SOUL (3-4)

Projected Score: Cleveland 174.7 | Philadelphia 167.4

Power Rankings: Cleveland No. 8 | Philadelphia No. 9

All-Time Series: Cleveland leads 10-4

Can Philadelphia Finally Break the Streak?

Cleveland has quietly become one of the league's most resilient teams.

The Gladiators have won three consecutive games.

They've improved from 1-3 to 4-3.

And they've done it with some of the most dramatic finishes of the season.

A Monday-night comeback against Cedar Rapids.

A 0.30-point victory over Northern Arizona.

And a 3.65-point escape against Brooklyn.

Josh Thurmer's team has developed a remarkable ability to survive close games.

Cleveland has also made a move to address its running back depth. GM Josh Thurmer acquired Kyle Monangai and Dallas Goedert from Boston, sending Cooper Kupp and Brenton Strange to the Brawlers. With the Gladiators riding a three-game winning streak, the trade could help stabilize an offense that has relied heavily on close finishes.

Now Cleveland faces a Philadelphia franchise it has historically dominated.

The Gladiators lead the all-time series 10-4.

Philadelphia hasn't beaten Cleveland since 2023.

And the Soul enter Week 8 struggling to maintain consistency.

Philadelphia is 3-4 after consecutive losses to Los Angeles and Miami.

Brian Simmons has received strong individual performances from players such as Tony Pollard, C.J. Stroud and Drake London.

But the Soul have struggled to put together complete team performances.

Cleveland's own scoring has been uneven, despite the winning streak.

The Gladiators scored just 149.45 points against Brooklyn last week.

And their Power Index of 3270.44 keeps them eighth despite a winning record.

Philadelphia sits immediately behind Cleveland at No. 9 with a Power Index of 3006.25.

The projections favor Cleveland 174.7-167.4.

That's a narrow enough margin for either team to win.

For Cleveland, a fourth consecutive victory would strengthen its playoff position.

For Philadelphia, a victory would end its losing streak, bring the Soul back to .500 and finally snap a lengthy drought against the Gladiators.

Mercer's Take: Cleveland has momentum and history on its side. Philadelphia has enough offensive talent to win, but the Soul must avoid another inconsistent performance. With both teams fighting for playoff positioning, this is one of Week 8's most important games.

Five Things to Watch in Week 8

1. Can Milwaukee Get Revenge Against Boston?

The Mustangs have won six straight and sit atop the Power Rankings. But Boston ended their season in last year's divisional playoffs. A victory over the defending champions would be another statement for Michael Bautch's 6-1 team.

2. Will Vegas Win Its Fourth Straight?

The Knight Hawks have transformed their season since acquiring Jaxon Smith-Njigba and Trey McBride. After starting 0-4, Vegas has won three consecutive games and scored more than 200 points in back-to-back weeks. Chicago is next.

3. Is Tucson's Championship Window Closing?

The Sugar Skulls have lost three of four, including consecutive blowouts. Mason Bautch is facing growing criticism over recent trades, and an unconfirmed report involving Jahmyr Gibbs has added another distraction. Tucson needs to respond against Los Angeles.

4. Will Liz Bautch Finally Reach 100 Career Wins?

Los Angeles GM Elizabeth Bautch remains one victory away from joining Michael Bautch as the only GMs in GLFL history with 100 career wins. A victory over Tucson would make history while giving the Wildcats a much-needed third win.

5. Can Brooklyn Avoid 0-8?

The Bolts have lost seven consecutive games to open the season, including a heartbreaking Sunday-night defeat to Cleveland. Now they face a Miami team ranked third in the league. Cory Colvin's search for victory is becoming increasingly desperate.

The Final Word: The Season Is About to Get Serious

Seven weeks into the season, the GLFL has a clear leader.

Milwaukee.

But behind the Mustangs, almost everything remains unsettled.

Tucson is struggling.

Miami is surging.

Northern Arizona is climbing.

Boston is trying to defend its championship.

Vegas has completely reinvented itself.

And Cleveland keeps finding ways to win.

Week 8 offers an opportunity for those teams to strengthen their positions—or expose weaknesses that could become costly as the playoffs approach.

Milwaukee and Boston will renew a rivalry shaped by last year's postseason.

Tucson will try to stop a season-threatening slide.

Vegas will attempt to extend one of the league's most surprising winning streaks.

And Brooklyn will once again search for a victory that has remained painfully out of reach.

The first half of the season established the contenders.

The second half will determine which of them are for real.

Week 8 starts now.
