GLFL WEEK 5 PREVIEW: A Championship Rematch, an Undefeated Streak and a Date With History

By Julian Mercer | GLFL National • October 8, 2026

Tucson wants revenge against the defending champions. Cedar Rapids wants to stay perfect. Cleveland wants its 100th regular-season victory. And Chicago and Brooklyn are about to play a game where somebody finally has to win. Welcome to Week 5.

Four weeks into the 2026 GLFL season, the standings are beginning to take shape. But if this week's schedule tells us anything, it's that the league isn't particularly interested in letting those standings settle.

Cedar Rapids stands alone at 4-0, the last undefeated team in the league. Milwaukee has climbed to No. 1 in the Power Rankings after producing the highest single-team score in GLFL history. And Tucson, fresh off its first defeat, has responded by making one of the biggest trades of the season.

Meanwhile, three teams remain winless.

That number will be down to at most two by the end of Week 5.

Chicago and Brooklyn meet in a matchup that guarantees one franchise its first victory of 2026. The alternative for either team involves an uncomfortable conversation with the GLFL record book.

Cleveland is also approaching a milestone, although the Gladiators would very much prefer to celebrate theirs.

And hanging over everything is a rematch of last season's championship, featuring two teams that have spent the opening month looking like legitimate contenders.

There are six games on the schedule. Every one of them has something at stake.

Let's get into Week 5.

GAME OF THE WEEK: Tucson Sugar Skulls (3-1) at Boston Brawlers (3-1)

Projected Score: Tucson 203.8 | Boston 180.4

Some games need an introduction.

This isn't one of them.

Tucson. Boston.

Mason Bautch. Chris Cholka.

A rematch of last season's Lakefront Bowl championship.

The last time these franchises met with a title on the line, Boston walked away with the championship and Tucson walked away wondering what might have been.

Now the Sugar Skulls get another opportunity.

And the timing couldn't be more interesting.

Tucson suffered its first defeat of the season last week, falling 220.80-196.20 to Miami. It was the Sugar Skulls' first loss after a 3-0 start and their first defeat against the Inferno since 2019.

Mason Bautch's response?

Make a blockbuster trade.

Tucson acquired Amon-Ra St. Brown, Trey McBride and Bhayshul Tuten from division rival Los Angeles, sending Brock Bowers, James Cook III and Carnell Tate to the Wildcats.

It's a massive exchange of offensive talent between two franchises competing in the Superior Division.

And now the Sugar Skulls prepare to face the team that ended their championship hopes last season.

The recent history actually favors Tucson.

Since Mason Bautch took over the franchise in 2021, the Sugar Skulls have won five of their last seven meetings against Boston.

The two losses?

One in 2023.

The other in last season's championship.

That second defeat carries considerably more weight.

Boston, meanwhile, has its own historical opportunity.

The defending champions are 3-1 after defeating Cleveland 222.85-185.65 in Week 4.

A victory over Tucson would give the Brawlers their first 4-1 start in franchise history.

Think about that.

Boston has been part of the GLFL since 2012. The franchise is the defending champion. And a 4-1 opening would represent its best start ever.

Kyren Williams led Boston with 47.2 points last week, while Zay Flowers, Trevor Lawrence and Jonathan Taylor helped deliver one of the team's strongest offensive performances of the season.

The Brawlers have the talent to make this competitive.

But the projections favor Tucson by 23.4 points, making the Sugar Skulls the clear statistical favorite.

There is also the question of how quickly Tucson's newly acquired players can reshape the offense.

The Sugar Skulls didn't make that trade to look better on paper.

They made it to win games like this.

Boston already has the championship.

Tucson wants a measure of revenge.

And with both teams sitting at 3-1, the winner will take another significant step toward establishing itself among the league's elite.

What to Watch: Can Tucson's revamped offense deliver immediately, or will Boston remind the Sugar Skulls why the Brawlers are the defending champions?

Cleveland Gladiators (1-3) at Cedar Rapids Titans (4-0)

Projected Score: Cleveland 166.8 | Cedar Rapids 166.1

The only undefeated team in the league is an underdog.

Not by much.

But technically, an underdog.

Cleveland enters Week 5 projected to defeat Cedar Rapids by seven-tenths of a point.

That's right.

The Gladiators are 1-3. The Titans are 4-0. And the projections have Cleveland winning.

Welcome to fantasy football.

Cedar Rapids has already established the best start in franchise history.

Mitch Smith's Titans have won four consecutive games, including last week's 189.85-151.95 victory over Los Angeles.

They are the only remaining undefeated team in the GLFL.

And despite that perfect record, they sit third in the latest Power Rankings.

The reason is simple: Milwaukee and Tucson have produced more offensive points.

But Cedar Rapids has something neither of those teams possesses.

Four wins. Zero losses.

The Titans have also won three consecutive meetings against Cleveland.

The Gladiators still lead the all-time series 12-8, but recent history belongs to Cedar Rapids.

Cleveland has a different number on its mind.

One hundred.

The Gladiators entered the season with 99 regular-season victories. Four weeks later, they're still waiting for No. 100.

Josh Thurmer's team has now missed four opportunities to reach the milestone.

And the front office hasn't been sitting quietly.

Cleveland completed a trade with Northern Arizona this week, acquiring Matthew Golden and Kyle Pitts in exchange for Rome Odunze and Hunter Henry.

Whether the move provides an immediate spark remains to be seen, but the message is clear: Cleveland isn't satisfied with its 1-3 start.

Bryce Young scored 46.75 points last week, with Puka Nacua adding 33.7.

The Gladiators have offensive talent.

What they haven't had is enough production at the right time to consistently win games.

Now they face a Cedar Rapids team that hasn't lost at all.

Something has to give.

Either Cleveland finally reaches 100 regular-season victories, or Cedar Rapids improves to 5-0 and extends the best start in franchise history.

And with a projected margin of just 0.7 points, this might be the closest matchup on the schedule.

What to Watch: Does Cleveland finally reach 100 wins, or can Cedar Rapids keep its perfect season alive?

Chicago Slaughter (0-4) at Brooklyn Bolts (0-4)

Projected Score: Chicago 172.6 | Brooklyn 165.2

Somebody is going to win.

I know.

It's a bold prediction.

But barring a tie, one of these two franchises will leave Week 5 with its first victory of 2026.

Chicago is 0-4.

Brooklyn is 0-4.

And both teams are approaching some extremely uncomfortable franchise milestones.

Let's start with Chicago.

A loss would drop the Slaughter to 0-5, the worst start in franchise history.

It would also mark Chicago's 100th regular-season defeat.

That would make the Slaughter the fourth franchise to reach 100 regular-season losses, joining Cedar Rapids (103), Philadelphia (102) and Vegas (102).

But there's another layer.

Darrien Clay has been Chicago's only general manager since the league began in 2012.

That means the franchise's 100th regular-season defeat would also be Clay's 100th as a GM.

Only Philadelphia's Brian Simmons, with 102, has already surpassed that milestone.

It's not exactly the kind of exclusive club anyone is rushing to join.

Brooklyn has its own historical concerns.

A loss would drop the Bolts to 0-5, matching their worst start in franchise history, previously established in 2022.

And yet, if Cory Colvin needs a reason for optimism, the head-to-head history provides one.

Brooklyn has won six consecutive meetings against Chicago.

The Bolts have also won nine of the franchises' 13 all-time matchups.

That's a significant historical advantage.

Of course, history won't set Brooklyn's lineup.

That responsibility belongs to Colvin, whose Week 4 lineup included an injured Rico Dowdle who scored zero points.

The mistake didn't cost Brooklyn the game, as Northern Arizona won by 42.80 points and no available replacement would have changed the outcome.

But it certainly didn't help.

The Bolts did receive extraordinary individual performances from Joe Burrow and Tetairoa McMillan, who combined for 104.2 points.

The rest of the lineup contributed just 51.05.

That's the problem Brooklyn needs to solve.

Chicago, meanwhile, is taking an unconventional approach to its own roster problems.

Darrien Clay spent $5 in free-agent bidding to acquire Tyreek Hill, who had not yet officially signed with an NFL team at the time of the move.

It's a gamble.

And at 0-4, perhaps Clay believes the Slaughter have reached the point where taking chances is necessary.

Chicago is favored by 7.4 points.

Brooklyn has the historical advantage.

One team will finally have something to celebrate.

The other will have a new entry in the franchise record book that nobody particularly wants.

What to Watch: Can Brooklyn extend its six-game winning streak against Chicago, or will the Slaughter avoid the worst start in franchise history and regular-season loss No. 100?

Milwaukee Mustangs (3-1) at Philadelphia Soul (3-1)

Projected Score: Milwaukee 188.1 | Philadelphia 183.0

How do you follow the greatest single-team scoring performance in GLFL history?

Apparently, by entering the next week with a running back problem.

Milwaukee is coming off a staggering 278.60-point performance against Chicago.

It was the highest single-team score in league history, surpassing Los Angeles' 263-point playoff performance from last season.

The Mustangs also broke their own regular-season scoring record of 259.70, which they had established just two weeks earlier.

Over their last three games, Milwaukee has scored 743.45 points.

Three games. Three victories. An average of 247.82 points.

That's how a team climbs to No. 1 in the Power Rankings despite having a loss on its record.

But Week 5 presents a different kind of challenge.

Kenneth Walker III is on a bye. Saquon Barkley is doubtful. Jadarian Price is on injured reserve.

That leaves Javonte Williams and Samaje Perine as the likely running back options for Milwaukee.

It's a significant change from the lineup that just overwhelmed Chicago.

And Philadelphia isn't exactly an easy opponent.

The Soul are also 3-1 after defeating Vegas 195.80-167.30 in Week 4.

C.J. Stroud scored 45.55 points, Bijan Robinson added 33.7, and Sam LaPorta contributed 27.4.

Brian Simmons has Philadelphia playing winning football, and the Soul already own a victory over defending champion Boston.

That's not a team Milwaukee can afford to overlook.

The projections reflect the uncertainty.

Milwaukee is favored by just 5.1 points.

For a team that won by more than 100 last week, that's a remarkably narrow margin.

The Mustangs have established themselves as the league's most explosive offense.

Now they need to prove they can keep winning when the roster isn't operating at full strength.

Philadelphia has an opportunity to make a major statement.

Milwaukee has an opportunity to strengthen its claim as the GLFL's best team.

And with both franchises sitting at 3-1, the winner will move to 4-1 and remain firmly in the early championship conversation.

What to Watch: Can Milwaukee overcome its running back shortage, or will Philadelphia become the latest team to prove that record-setting offense doesn't guarantee victory?

Northern Arizona Wranglers (3-1) at Miami Inferno (3-1)

Projected Score: Northern Arizona 158.4 | Miami 177.4

If there's one team that deserves more attention after the first four weeks, it might be Miami.

The Inferno went 3-11 last season.

They're already 3-1 in 2026.

And their latest victory came against previously undefeated Tucson.

Miami scored 220.80 points in Week 4, handing the Sugar Skulls their first loss and recording its first victory against Tucson since 2019.

Drake Maye led the way with 52.85 points, while Quinshon Judkins, Jaylen Warren and Deebo Samuel Sr. helped power the offense.

The victory sent Miami climbing three spots to No. 6 in the Power Rankings.

Now Richard Head's team has another opportunity to prove the turnaround is real.

Northern Arizona is also 3-1.

The Wranglers defeated Brooklyn 198.05-155.25 last week and currently sit fourth in the Power Rankings, just 7.68 points ahead of Boston.

Jason Richmond has built one of the more consistent teams in the league's opening month.

But he isn't standing still.

Northern Arizona completed a trade with Cleveland this week, sending Matthew Golden and Kyle Pitts to the Gladiators for Rome Odunze and Hunter Henry.

The move reshapes the Wranglers' receiving and tight end options as they prepare for an important stretch of the schedule.

And this week's opponent provides a serious test.

Miami is favored by 19.0 points.

That's a substantial projection gap between two teams with identical records.

The Inferno have momentum.

Northern Arizona has consistency.

Both teams have legitimate reasons to believe they belong among the league's contenders.

Only one will leave Week 5 at 4-1.

What to Watch: Was Miami's victory over Tucson the beginning of something bigger, or can Northern Arizona slow the Inferno and strengthen its own contender credentials?

Vegas Knight Hawks (0-4) at Los Angeles Wildcats (1-3)

Projected Score: Vegas 170.6 | Los Angeles 179.5

Los Angeles needs a win.

Vegas needs a win even more.

And both franchises enter Week 5 with plenty of questions about what comes next.

The Wildcats are 1-3 after losing 189.85-151.95 to Cedar Rapids in Week 4.

It was another frustrating result for a team that entered the season with significant expectations.

Liz Bautch responded with a blockbuster.

Los Angeles traded Amon-Ra St. Brown, Trey McBride and Bhayshul Tuten to Tucson, acquiring James Cook III, Brock Bowers and Carnell Tate in return.

It's a fascinating transaction.

The Wildcats surrendered established star power, but received a package that could provide better overall production across multiple positions.

Based on the team's evaluation of current-season averages, the three incoming players had collectively produced more points per game than the three outgoing players.

That doesn't guarantee anything.

Four weeks is a small sample. And trading Amon-Ra St. Brown is never a minor decision.

But Los Angeles clearly believes the move gives the team a better chance to win now.

Vegas, meanwhile, is running out of time to turn promising individual performances into actual victories.

The Knight Hawks are 0-4 after losing 195.80-167.30 to Philadelphia.

Kirk Cousins scored 48.25 points last week, and Chuba Hubbard added 34.9.

But the receiving corps struggled, with Ja'Marr Chase, Mack Hollins and Kalif Raymond combining for just 19.6 points.

It's the kind of inconsistency that has defined Vegas' opening month.

Two weeks ago, the Knight Hawks scored 239.20 points against Tucson and still lost.

Last week, they managed just 167.30.

The result was a four-position fall in the Power Rankings, from sixth to tenth.

Dave Nguyen's team has talent.

What it doesn't have is a victory.

Los Angeles is favored by 8.9 points, but this matchup could be closer than the records suggest.

For the Wildcats, it's an opportunity to begin a new chapter following a franchise-altering trade.

For Vegas, it's another chance to avoid falling deeper into an increasingly dangerous hole.

What to Watch: Will Los Angeles' new acquisitions provide an immediate boost, or can Vegas finally turn its offensive potential into its first victory?

Five Things That Could Define Week 5

1. The Championship Rematch

Tucson and Boston meet for the first time since last season's championship. The Sugar Skulls have won five of the last seven meetings under Mason Bautch, but Boston won the one that mattered most. Now Tucson brings a reshaped offense into the rematch.

2. The Last Undefeated Team

Cedar Rapids is 4-0 for the best start in franchise history. The Titans are also slight underdogs against Cleveland. A fifth consecutive victory would keep their perfect season alive and extend a start that has already entered franchise history.

3. Somebody Finally Wins

Chicago and Brooklyn are both 0-4. Brooklyn has won six straight meetings against the Slaughter, but Chicago enters as the projected favorite. One franchise will finally celebrate a victory. The other will fall to 0-5.

4. The Century Marks

Cleveland is seeking its 100th regular-season victory. Chicago is trying to avoid its 100th regular-season defeat. And because Darrien Clay has managed the Slaughter since 2012, a Chicago loss would also give him 100 regular-season losses as a GM.

5. The Trade Fallout Begins

Northern Arizona and Cleveland have exchanged four players. Tucson and Los Angeles have exchanged six. Those transactions have the potential to reshape multiple rosters and influence both the Michigan and Superior Division races.

Week 5 will provide the first opportunity to begin evaluating whether those moves deliver the results their general managers expect.

The Final Word

The first four weeks established the contenders.

Week 5 could begin separating them.

Milwaukee has the league's most explosive offense, but its running back depth is suddenly being tested.

Cedar Rapids has the only perfect record, but the Titans are projected to face a razor-thin contest against a Cleveland team desperate for a milestone victory.

Miami and Northern Arizona are both 3-1, and one will emerge with an important fourth win.

Chicago and Brooklyn are both searching for their first.

And in Boston, the defending champions will welcome the team they defeated for last season's title.

Tucson has made its move.

Boston has its championship.

Cedar Rapids has its perfect record.

Everybody else has something to prove.

Week 5 is where the next chapter begins.
