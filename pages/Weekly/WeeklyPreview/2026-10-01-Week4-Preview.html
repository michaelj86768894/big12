GLFL WEEK 4 PREVIEW: The Cushion Is Disappearing

By Julian Mercer | GLFL National • October 1, 2026

Two teams can reach 4-0. Three winless teams are trying to avoid 0-4. Cleveland gets another shot at No. 100. And suddenly, "it's still early" isn't quite as comforting as it used to be.

Remember when it was only Week 2?

Everybody was fine.

0-2? Plenty of time.

1-1? Basically meaningless.

2-0? Nice start, but don't get carried away.

We're entering Week 4 now.

Things are beginning to matter.

Tucson and Cedar Rapids can leave this weekend 4-0. Vegas, Chicago and Brooklyn can leave it 0-4. And for everybody sitting somewhere in between, the difference between a win and a loss is beginning to dramatically change the path toward the postseason.

Welcome to Week 4.

Why Week 4 Matters

Historical fantasy-football results show just how quickly the playoff outlook begins changing once teams reach their fourth game.

Entering Week 4 With a Win Playoff Rate With a Loss Playoff Rate
3-0 4-0 91.5% 3-1 76.5%
2-1 3-1 77.0% 2-2 53.9%

0-3 1-3 28.5% 0-4 11.7%

Those aren't GLFL-specific percentages, and they aren't guarantees. But the trend isn't difficult to understand.

Winning this week gives you options.

Losing starts taking them away.

GAME OF THE WEEK: Boston Brawlers (2-1) at Cleveland Gladiators (1-2)

Projection: Cleveland 176.4, Boston 158.6

Boston has a Cleveland problem.

A very real Cleveland problem.

The Brawlers have played the Gladiators 14 times in GLFL history.

They've won three.

Boston's victories came in 2015, 2021 and 2024. That's it.

Cleveland owns an astonishing 11-3 all-time advantage over a franchise that has spent much of the last several years beating just about everybody else.

And this particular meeting comes at an interesting moment.

Boston just had its undefeated start ripped away by Philadelphia in a 227.00-167.05 loss. The defending champions now enter Week 4 as a projected underdog against one of the few franchises that has consistently had their number.

Cleveland has problems of its own. Injuries to De'Von Achane and Jalen Coker helped derail last week's attempt to secure the franchise's 100th regular-season victory.

So, for the third consecutive week, we'll say it again:

Cleveland is one win away from 100.

For Boston, a victory means 3-1 and a historical playoff rate of 77.0 percent. A loss sends the champs to 2-2.

For Cleveland, the difference is even more dramatic: win and get back to 2-2. Lose and fall to 1-3, a record that historically reached the postseason only 29.6 percent of the time in the outside data we're tracking.

What we're watching: Can Boston solve one of the strangest matchup problems in franchise history, or will Cleveland finally get No. 100 at the defending champions' expense?

TOSS-UP OF THE WEEK: Vegas Knight Hawks (0-3) at Philadelphia Soul (2-1)

Projection: Philadelphia 187.9, Vegas 180.0

Welcome to the weirdest 0-3 team in the league.

Vegas scored 239.20 points last week.

It was the 10th-highest regular-season score in GLFL history.

They lost.

Now the Knight Hawks enter Week 4 ranked sixth in the GLFL Power Rankings despite being winless, and the projections say they're separated from Philadelphia by fewer than eight points.

The problem isn't necessarily how Vegas is playing anymore.

It's the hole they've already dug.

A victory gets Vegas to 1-3. Historically, teams reaching that mark from 0-3 have still made the playoffs 28.5 percent of the time.

At 0-4, that number drops to 11.7 percent.

And Philadelphia probably isn't feeling particularly charitable.

The Soul just hung 227 points on the defending champions and have won five straight against Vegas. The Knight Hawks haven't beaten Philadelphia since 2022, and that streak has swung the all-time series to 12-9 in favor of the Soul.

Philadelphia can move to 3-1.

Vegas is trying to prove that its 239-point explosion was the beginning of a turnaround rather than the cruelest loss of its season.

What we're watching: Eventually, Vegas' underlying performance has to turn into an actual win. If it doesn't happen soon, the Power Rankings aren't going to matter.

Miami Inferno (2-1) at Tucson Sugar Skulls (3-0)

Projection: Tucson 208.3, Miami 178.5

Everybody keeps waiting for Tucson to blink.

They nearly did last week.

Vegas pushed the Sugar Skulls to the absolute limit before Tucson escaped 243.65-239.20 in the highest-scoring regular-season matchup in league history.

The reward?

Another 200-point projection.

Tucson enters Week 4 at 3-0, No. 1 in the Power Rankings and projected for a league-high 208.3 points. A victory would produce the Sugar Skulls' first 4-0 start since 2014.

History isn't particularly encouraging for Miami, either.

Tucson owns a 10-5 advantage in the all-time series, and Miami's last victory came in 2019. That means the Inferno have not beaten Tucson once since Mason Bautch took over the franchise in 2021.

Miami shouldn't be dismissed. The Inferno are 2-1, just scored 211.70 and celebrated the franchise's 100th overall victory last week.

But this is a different test.

What we're watching: Tucson has survived everything thrown at it through three weeks. Can Miami become the first team to finish the job?

Cedar Rapids Titans (3-0) at Los Angeles Wildcats (1-2)

Projection: Los Angeles 185.8, Cedar Rapids 173.8

Here's something we haven't been able to say since 2015:

Cedar Rapids is 3-0.

Here's something considerably less encouraging for the Titans:

Los Angeles is waiting.

The Wildcats own an 11-3 all-time advantage over Cedar Rapids.

The Titans did manage to string together three consecutive victories in the series from 2016 through 2018. Outside of that stretch, this matchup has belonged almost entirely to Los Angeles.

And despite the difference in records, the projections favor LA by 12 points.

That makes this one of Week 4's more fascinating tests.

Cedar Rapids has spent three weeks proving its start deserves to be taken seriously. A win here would move the Titans to 4-0 and give them a victory over a franchise that has historically dominated them.

Los Angeles is fighting a different battle.

At 1-2, a victory gets the Wildcats back to .500 and corresponds with a 53.9 percent historical playoff rate. A loss drops them to 1-3, where that rate falls to 29.6 percent.

What we're watching: Cedar Rapids has already earned respect. Beating LA would add something else: legitimacy against one of its historical nightmares.

Brooklyn Bolts (0-3) at Northern Arizona Wranglers (2-1)

Projection: Northern Arizona 167.7, Brooklyn 151.5

Jason Richmond asked for respect after Week 1.

Three weeks later, Northern Arizona is No. 4 in the Power Rankings after dropping 226.25 on Los Angeles.

Request granted.

Now comes a game the Wranglers are supposed to win.

Brooklyn still holds an 8-7 advantage in the all-time series, but Richmond is 2-0 against the Bolts since taking over Northern Arizona.

Brooklyn, meanwhile, is running out of room for another bad Sunday.

The Bolts started Joe Burrow last week, watched him score 46.4 and still lost 211.70-143.00. They're now 0-3 for the first time since 2022.

Win this week and Brooklyn gets to 1-3 with at least a path forward.

Lose, and 0-4 comes with that ugly 11.7 percent historical playoff rate.

What we're watching: Northern Arizona proved it can win as an underdog. Now can the Wranglers handle being the team everyone expects to win?

Milwaukee Mustangs (2-1) at Chicago Slaughter (0-3)

Projection: Milwaukee 189.4, Chicago 174.2

Didn't we just do this?

Yes.

Milwaukee and Chicago meet for the second consecutive week after the Mustangs won 205.15-170.20 in Week 3.

There isn't much new to say about the history.

Milwaukee owns the all-time series. The Mustangs have won five of the last six. And after starting the season 0-1, Milwaukee has responded by scoring 259.70 and 205.15 in consecutive weeks.

Chicago is now 0-3 for only the second time in franchise history.

The first was 2021.

That team started 0-4.

That's exactly what Darrien Clay is trying to avoid repeating.

Chicago has already been connected to potential star additions as the front office looks for ways to change the trajectory of the season. But unless one of those stars is walking through the door before kickoff, the Slaughter have to solve the same team that beat them five days ago.

Milwaukee can move to 3-1.

Chicago can either breathe life into its season at 1-3 or fall to 0-4.

What we're watching: Back-to-back matchups are rare opportunities for immediate revenge. Chicago gets one. Milwaukee gets an opportunity to make sure nothing changes.

One More Thing...

Week 4 isn't an elimination game.

Nobody's season officially ends Sunday.

But look at the numbers again.

4-0: 91.5 percent.

0-4: 11.7 percent.

That's the difference between spending October thinking about playoff positioning and spending October trying to dig out of a hole.

Tucson and Cedar Rapids can put themselves in the first group.

Vegas, Chicago and Brooklyn are desperately trying to stay out of the second.

And somewhere in Cleveland, somebody has probably already printed another "WIN 100" banner and is hoping they finally get to use the thing.

Welcome to Week 4.
