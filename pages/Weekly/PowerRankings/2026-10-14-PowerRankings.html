GLFL WEEK 5 POWER RANKINGS: Tucson Takes Back No. 1, Miami Crashes the Top Three

By Marcus Vance | GLFL Senior Analyst • October 14, 2026

Milwaukee won its fourth consecutive game. Tucson scored 254.70 points against the defending champions. Miami continued its remarkable turnaround. And when the Week 5 numbers settled, the GLFL Power Index had a new leader — or rather, a familiar one returning to the throne.

Milwaukee didn't lose.

The Mustangs won their fourth consecutive game, improved to 4-1 and remained one of the most explosive offenses in the league.

They also lost the No. 1 ranking.

Welcome to the Power Index.

While Milwaukee was surviving Philadelphia by 3.80 points, Tucson was busy dropping 254.70 on the defending champions.

The Sugar Skulls didn't just avenge last season's championship defeat. They reclaimed the throne.

And if Milwaukee general manager Michael Bautch has a complaint, he can take it up with the numbers.

I don't make the formula.

I just report what it says.

Of course, Tucson's return to No. 1 is only part of the story.

Miami has climbed into the top three after another historic offensive performance. Cedar Rapids suffered its first defeat but remains among the league's strongest teams. Cleveland finally secured its 100th regular-season victory.

And at the bottom of the rankings, three teams finally have a victory while one remains winless.

Five weeks down. Nine regular-season weeks remaining.

Let's see where everybody stands.

Week 5 Power Index

The GLFL Power Index combines season-long scoring production, winning percentage and performance against the league's weekly scoring median.

The formula rewards teams that consistently score well, not simply those fortunate enough to face a struggling opponent.

It also means that winning doesn't guarantee a rise in the rankings, and losing doesn't automatically send a team tumbling.

Just ask Milwaukee.

1. Tucson Sugar Skulls (4-1) ▲ 1

Power Index: 4344.54 | Previous Rank: 2

Well, that didn't take long.

One week after losing the top spot to Milwaukee, Tucson is back at No. 1.

And if there was ever a week to make a statement, this was it.

The Sugar Skulls traveled to Boston for a rematch of last season's Lakefront Bowl championship and absolutely dismantled the defending champions.

Final score: Tucson 254.70, Boston 176.90.

That's a 77.80-point victory.

It's also the fourth-highest regular-season single-team score in GLFL history.

Jahmyr Gibbs delivered 48.70 points. Aaron Rodgers added 39.35. Jaxon Smith-Njigba contributed 28.30. DeMario Douglas scored 26.50.

And Trey McBride, acquired from Los Angeles just days earlier, immediately contributed 25.60 points.

That brings us to the other reason Tucson deserves serious attention.

Mason Bautch didn't stand pat with a 3-1 team. He completed a blockbuster trade with Los Angeles, bringing in Amon-Ra St. Brown, Trey McBride and Bhayshul Tuten.

Then his team scored 254.70 points.

The Sugar Skulls didn't need every new acquisition to have a career day.

Their existing roster was already capable of producing historic numbers.

Now they've added more weapons.

Tucson leads the league in scoring average, owns the highest Power Index and has one of the strongest projected playoff outlooks in the GLFL.

And after what happened in last season's championship, I suspect Mason enjoyed this victory a little more than the others.

The Sugar Skulls are back on top.

Nobody can argue they didn't earn it.

2. Milwaukee Mustangs (4-1) ▼ 1

Power Index: 4206.22 | Previous Rank: 1

Before anybody in Milwaukee starts sending angry messages, let me explain something.

The Mustangs are very good.

They might be championship good.

They've won four consecutive games, broken the league's single-team scoring record and remain one of only three franchises averaging more than 200 points per game.

They're also No. 2.

That's how the Power Index works.

Milwaukee escaped Philadelphia 173.70-169.90 in Week 5, improving to 4-1.

Jared Goff scored 50.65 points. Justin Jefferson contributed 21.80.

And Samaje Perine, pressed into service because of Milwaukee's running back situation, delivered a surprising 23.55 points.

It was a gritty victory.

It was also a considerable step down from the 278.60-point explosion that propelled Milwaukee to No. 1 a week earlier.

Meanwhile, Tucson scored 254.70.

That difference matters.

Last week, Milwaukee led Tucson by 167.25 Power Index points.

This week, Tucson leads Milwaukee by 138.32.

That's a 305.57-point swing in the gap between the league's two highest-ranked teams.

And Milwaukee didn't even lose.

General manager Michael Bautch probably won't appreciate that distinction.

But here's the good news for Mustangs fans.

Milwaukee's Power Index actually increased from 3499.50 to 4206.22.

The Mustangs didn't get worse.

Tucson simply had a better week.

There's a difference.

And if Milwaukee keeps winning, it will have plenty of opportunities to reclaim the top spot.

For now, though, second place is exactly where the numbers put them.

Sorry, Milwaukee.

Don't shoot the messenger.

3. Miami Inferno (4-1) ▲ 3

Power Index: 3814.82 | Previous Rank: 6

The biggest climb in this week's rankings belongs to the Miami Inferno.

And frankly, it's overdue that we start treating this team like a legitimate contender.

Miami finished 3-11 last season.

Five weeks into 2026, Richard Head's team is 4-1.

The Inferno have already surpassed last season's win total.

And they're doing it with an offense that is threatening the league's historical scoring benchmarks.

Miami defeated Northern Arizona 243.65-166.60 in Week 5.

The 243.65 points rank seventh among regular-season performances in GLFL history.

Drake Maye was magnificent, producing 69.20 fantasy points.

Jaylen Warren added 41.80. Parker Washington scored 33.40. Tyler Warren contributed 26.40.

Four players accounted for 170.80 points.

That's more than Northern Arizona scored with its entire starting lineup.

Consider Miami's last two weeks.

Week 4: 220.80 points against Tucson.

Week 5: 243.65 points against Northern Arizona.

That's 464.45 points in two games against two of the Superior Division's strongest teams.

The Inferno have climbed from ninth to sixth to third in consecutive Power Index releases.

That's not a hot streak anymore.

That's a team forcing the league to reconsider what it thought it knew.

Tucson and Milwaukee remain the two highest-ranked teams.

But Miami is officially part of the championship conversation.

And if this offense continues producing at its current pace, the Inferno might not be finished climbing.

4. Cedar Rapids Titans (4-1) ▼ 1

Power Index: 3740.53 | Previous Rank: 3

The last undefeated team has fallen.

But Cedar Rapids remains one of the GLFL's strongest franchises.

The Titans suffered their first loss of the season in dramatic fashion, falling to Cleveland 206.80-188.55.

Cedar Rapids entered Monday night with a substantial lead.

Then Puka Nacua delivered 47.70 points for the Gladiators.

Dalton Kincaid managed just 3.70 for the Titans.

And suddenly, the perfect season was no longer perfect.

Still, let's not overreact.

Cedar Rapids scored 188.55 points.

That was the fifth-highest total in the league during Week 5.

Against seven of the other 11 teams, the Titans would have won.

Instead, they happened to face the third-highest-scoring team.

That's unfortunate scheduling.

Not evidence of a collapse.

Bucky Irving scored 38.50. Brock Purdy contributed 31.05.

The Titans remain 4-1, and their Power Index has increased despite the loss.

They're now just 74.29 points behind third-ranked Miami.

Mitch Smith's team has plenty of reasons to remain confident.

The undefeated streak is over.

The championship aspirations should not be.

5. Boston Brawlers (3-2) —

That was ugly.

There isn't a more diplomatic way to describe Boston's Week 5.

The defending champions hosted Tucson in a rematch of last season's Lakefront Bowl championship.

Boston scored 176.90.

A 77.80-point defeat.

Jayden Daniels did everything he could to keep the Brawlers competitive, producing 49.25 points.

The defeat also denied the Brawlers their first-ever 4-1 start.

And while Chris Cholka's team remains fifth in the Power Index, the gap between Boston and the league's elite is growing.

Tucson leads Boston by 861.18 points.

Milwaukee leads Boston by 722.86.

Those are substantial differences.

But before anybody starts writing off the defending champions, remember what matters most.

Boston is 3-2.

The Brawlers remain above 90 percent in projected playoff probability.

And last season's championship is a reminder that this franchise knows how to win when it matters.

Still, Tucson delivered a message.

Boston needs to answer.

6. Northern Arizona Wranglers (3-2) ▼ 2

Power Index: 3453.66 | Previous Rank: 4

Two weeks ago, Northern Arizona looked like one of the league's most dangerous teams.

Now the Wranglers are staring at a two-game losing streak.

Miami defeated Northern Arizona 243.65-166.60 in Week 5, sending Jason Richmond's team from fourth to sixth in the Power Index.

Jordan Love scored 43.95 points.

Christian McCaffrey added 20.30, and Ollie Gordon II contributed 20.80.

Hunter Henry, acquired from Cleveland earlier in the week, scored 16.40 in his Wranglers debut.

But the overall offensive production wasn't enough against Miami's historic performance.

Northern Arizona now sits just 29.70 Power Index points behind Boston.

That's essentially a dead heat for fifth place.

The Wranglers also remain firmly in the playoff race at 3-2.

But the schedule hasn't been kind.

After losing to Brooklyn's division rivals in consecutive weeks, Northern Arizona needs to rediscover the consistency that carried it into the top four.

The talent is there.

The record is still respectable.

But the Power Index is beginning to ask whether Northern Arizona belongs with the championship favorites or the teams chasing them.

7. Philadelphia Soul (3-2) —

Power Index: 2405.91 | Previous Rank: 7

Philadelphia lost.

Philadelphia remains seventh.

And honestly, that feels about right.

The Soul pushed Milwaukee to the absolute limit in Week 5, falling 173.70-169.90.

A 3.80-point defeat against the team that entered the week ranked No. 1.

C.J. Stroud scored 40.55. Garrett Wilson contributed 27.40. Drake London added 27.30. Sam LaPorta scored 22.80.

There were plenty of positives.

But the Soul still lost.

At 3-2, Brian Simmons' team remains in a respectable position.

Northern Arizona, ranked sixth, leads Philadelphia by 1047.75 Power Index points.

That's not a small difference.

That's an entirely different neighborhood.

Philadelphia is winning enough games to remain competitive.

But the Power Index suggests the Soul still have considerable work to do before they can be considered a serious threat to the league's elite.

The next few weeks will determine whether Philadelphia can close that gap.

Or whether seventh place is simply where this team belongs.

8. Cleveland Gladiators (2-3) ▲ 1

Power Index: 2222.52 | Previous Rank: 9

Cleveland finally did it.

One hundred regular-season victories.

And the Gladiators couldn't have chosen a more dramatic way to reach the milestone.

Trailing previously undefeated Cedar Rapids by approximately 26 points entering Monday night, Cleveland needed something extraordinary.

Puka Nacua delivered.

His 47.70-point performance powered Cleveland to a 206.80-188.55 victory.

It was Cleveland's 100th regular-season win.

It ended Cedar Rapids' undefeated season.

And it gave Josh Thurmer's team its second victory of 2026.

Cleveland also received 35.55 points from Kyler Murray, 27.00 from Quincy Williams and 25.30 from Matthew Golden.

Golden had arrived earlier in the week as part of a trade with Northern Arizona.

Speaking of trades, Thurmer spent the week actively shopping players.

One reported proposal would have sent Nacua to Tucson in exchange for a package featuring Jahmyr Gibbs and Trey McBride.

The deal wasn't completed.

And after Monday night, Cleveland fans are probably thankful it wasn't.

The Gladiators' Power Index increased by 604.21 points this week.

That's a substantial improvement.

Cleveland moves up to eighth, and suddenly the season looks considerably more promising.

There's still work to do.

But for one week, the Gladiators had everything they could have wanted.

A milestone.

A comeback.

And a victory over the league's last undefeated team.

9. Vegas Knight Hawks (1-4) ▲ 1

Power Index: 1999.69 | Previous Rank: 10

Vegas finally has a win.

It wasn't pretty.

It wasn't dominant.

But it counts.

The Knight Hawks defeated Los Angeles 155.50-153.05 in Week 5, escaping with a 2.45-point victory.

Michael Penix Jr. scored 31.80. Ja'Marr Chase contributed 28.20. Derrick Henry added 25.00. D'Andre Swift scored 21.30.

Those performances were enough to deliver Vegas its first victory.

And after four consecutive losses, Dave Nguyen probably isn't particularly concerned with style points.

The Knight Hawks move up one spot to ninth in the Power Index.

Their 1999.69 score places them just ahead of Los Angeles and Chicago.

But let's keep things in perspective.

Vegas is still 1-4.

Its projected playoff probability is just nine percent.

And the Knight Hawks have yet to establish the consistent scoring needed to compete with the league's strongest franchises.

Still, every comeback begins somewhere.

Vegas finally has something to build on.

Now comes the difficult part.

Doing it again.

10. Los Angeles Wildcats (1-4) ▼ 2

Power Index: 1935.34 | Previous Rank: 8

Los Angeles made one of the biggest roster moves of the season.

And still couldn't find a way to win.

The Wildcats completed a blockbuster trade with Tucson, acquiring James Cook III, Brock Bowers and Carnell Tate.

In exchange, Los Angeles sent Amon-Ra St. Brown, Trey McBride and Bhayshul Tuten to the Sugar Skulls.

The immediate results were surprisingly balanced.

Tucson's three new players combined for 60.10 points.

Los Angeles' three new players combined for 59.40.

A difference of just 0.70 points.

Unfortunately for the Wildcats, the team results were considerably less encouraging.

Tucson scored 254.70 and reclaimed the No. 1 ranking.

Los Angeles scored 153.05 and lost to previously winless Vegas.

James Cook scored 29.80 in his Wildcats debut.

Brock Bowers contributed 28.00.

Josh Allen added 47.60.

Those were strong performances.

But the Wildcats received almost nothing from several other positions.

Carnell Tate scored 1.60. Romeo Doubs managed 3.60. Roman Wilson finished with zero.

And Los Angeles lost by 2.45 points.

The Wildcats fall two positions to No. 10.

Their Power Index of 1935.34 is just 3.85 points ahead of Chicago.

That's an extraordinarily narrow margin.

At 1-4, Los Angeles now has a projected playoff probability of just five percent.

The trade may ultimately prove beneficial for Liz Bautch's team.

One week isn't enough to judge a blockbuster.

But the Wildcats need results.

And they need them quickly.

11. Chicago Slaughter (1-4) —

Power Index: 1931.49 | Previous Rank: 11

Chicago finally won.

Chicago scored 202.65 points.

Chicago avoided the first 0-5 start in franchise history.

And Chicago remains No. 11.

If that sounds harsh, welcome to the Power Index.

The Slaughter defeated Brooklyn 202.65-143.00, delivering their strongest performance of the season.

Christian Watson scored 35.30. George Pickens added 34.00. Mike Evans contributed 32.30.

Chicago trails Los Angeles by just 3.85 Power Index points.

A strong Week 6 performance could send the Slaughter climbing several positions.

The Slaughter finally have something positive to talk about.

Now they need to prove Week 5 wasn't a one-time event.

12. Brooklyn Bolts (0-5) —

Power Index: 1487.20 | Previous Rank: 12

Brooklyn is the last winless team in the GLFL.

And its general manager might already be thinking about next season.

Before Week 5, Cory Colvin made a revealing comment in the league's group chat.

"No need to send me any, I'm going down with this ship. If this stays a keeper league and I'm already 0-4 I'm thinking more about next season than this one."

Then Brooklyn lost again.

The Bolts fell to Chicago 202.65-143.00, dropping to 0-5.

The record matches Brooklyn's worst start in franchise history, previously established in 2022.

Joe Burrow scored 48.50 points. Malik Nabers added 36.10.

But the rest of the lineup offered little support.

D.J. Moore finished with zero. Austin Ekeler scored 3.40. Kenyon Sadiq contributed 0.30.

And Chicago, a team that entered the week without a victory, scored nearly 60 more points than Brooklyn.

The Bolts now sit more than 400 Power Index points behind 11th-ranked Chicago.

Their projected playoff probability has fallen to zero.

That doesn't mean Brooklyn is mathematically eliminated.

But it does mean the league's current projections see virtually no realistic path to the postseason.

Colvin hasn't formally announced a rebuilding effort.

But his comments suggest the future is already entering the conversation.

And at 0-5, it's difficult to blame him.

Brooklyn has nine regular-season games remaining.

Nine opportunities to avoid making this one of the most disappointing seasons in franchise history.

The first step?

Win one.

The Power Index: A Two-Team Race at the Top?

After five weeks, Tucson and Milwaukee have established themselves as the two highest-ranked franchises in the GLFL.

Tucson leads Milwaukee by 138.32 Power Index points.

Milwaukee leads Miami by 391.40.

That's a meaningful separation between second and third.

But it would be a mistake to dismiss Miami or Cedar Rapids.

The Inferno have climbed three positions this week and are scoring at a historic pace.

Cedar Rapids sits just 74.29 points behind Miami, despite suffering its first loss.

And Boston and Northern Arizona are separated by only 29.70 points for fifth place.

The rankings are beginning to form distinct groups.

Tucson and Milwaukee lead the championship conversation.

Miami and Cedar Rapids are chasing closely behind.

Boston and Northern Arizona remain dangerous, but have work to do.

Philadelphia occupies a significant gap between the contenders and the rest of the league.

And Cleveland, Vegas, Los Angeles, Chicago and Brooklyn are trying to change the direction of their seasons.

Of course, this is Week 5.

Nobody receives a trophy for leading the Power Index in October.

But if you're looking for the league's strongest teams through five weeks, the numbers are beginning to tell a compelling story.

Vance's Final Word

Last week, Milwaukee broke the all-time scoring record and claimed the No. 1 ranking.

This week, Tucson answered with 254.70 points against the defending champions.

That's how a rivalry develops.

Not through press conferences.

Not through group-chat arguments.

Through production.

And right now, the Sugar Skulls have the better numbers.

Milwaukee can complain.

Miami can keep climbing.

Cedar Rapids can regroup.

Boston can remind everyone who won last year's championship.

But until somebody changes the numbers, Tucson is No. 1.

The Power Index doesn't care how you feel about it.

It cares what you score.
