GLFL WEEK 3 POWER RANKINGS: The Standings Say 0-3. The Numbers Say Something Else.

By Marcus Vance | Senior GLFL Analyst • September 30, 2026

Tucson strengthens its grip on No. 1, Cedar Rapids remains unbeaten, Northern Arizona surges into the top four and an 0-3 team is about to make everybody angry.

Let's get this out of the way immediately.

Vegas is 0-3.

Vegas is also No. 6 in the GLFL Power Rankings.

If that sentence bothers you, I recommend directing your complaints to the numbers. They don't have a Twitter account and therefore cannot argue back.

Week 3 was exactly why these rankings exist. Wins matter. Losses matter. But we're also trying to measure how teams are actually performing underneath the standings.

Sometimes those two things agree.

Tucson is 3-0 and No. 1. Cedar Rapids is 3-0 and No. 2.

Sometimes they absolutely do not.

Vegas just scored 239.20 points, lost by 4.45 and moved up four spots.

Welcome to the Week 3 Power Rankings.

1. Tucson Sugar Skulls —

Record: 3-0 | Power Score: 2769.60

For a few hours Sunday, Tucson finally looked vulnerable.

Vegas had the Sugar Skulls on the ropes. The Knight Hawks briefly became the favorite. The No. 1 team in these rankings was staring at its first loss of the season.

Then Jahmyr Gibbs, Patrick Mahomes and Jaxon Smith-Njigba started scoring touchdowns.

Tucson finished with 243.65 points, the fifth-highest regular-season score in GLFL history, and survived 243.65-239.20 in the highest-scoring regular-season matchup the league has ever seen.

Three weeks. Three wins. The highest Power Score in the league.

And remember what GM Mason Bautch said before Week 2:

"Just wait for Week 5 Boston, I'm coming."

At the time, that sounded like somebody looking a little too far ahead.

Two weeks later, Tucson is still undefeated.

2. Cedar Rapids Titans —

Record: 3-0 | Power Score: 2423.80

It's time to stop treating Cedar Rapids like a cute early-season story.

The Titans are 3-0.

That's their best start since 2015, and Sam Darnold's 59.45-point performance helped Cedar Rapids put away Cleveland 189.20-162.65.

They've now beaten all three division opponents to start teh season and sit alone atop the Erie Division.

There are flashier teams below them. There are teams with bigger individual scoring explosions. There are teams with more recent postseason success.

None of those teams have a better Power Score than Cedar Rapids outside of Tucson.

Three weeks ago, nobody was talking about the Titans as one of the GLFL's top teams.

They are now.

3. Milwaukee Mustangs ▲ 1

Record: 2-1 | Power Score: 2400.42

Milwaukee has apparently decided that scoring 200 points every week is a reasonable strategy.

After setting the regular-season scoring record with 259.70 in Week 2, the Mustangs followed it with 205.15 against Chicago.

That's 464.85 points in two weeks.

And this time they did it despite Justin Jefferson leaving early and contributing only 6.2 points.

The opening-week loss still exists, which is why Milwaukee isn't sitting beside the two undefeated teams above it. But the Power Index doesn't care about aesthetics.

Over the last two weeks, nobody has produced more offense.

GM Michael Bautch got some grief after leaving a Week 1 victory on his bench.

Since then, his team has scored 259.70 and 205.15.

We'll call the lesson learned.

4. Northern Arizona Wranglers ▲ 4

Record: 2-1 | Power Score: 2186.27

Jason Richmond wanted respect.

Jason Richmond has received respect.

After Week 1, the Northern Arizona GM wasn't particularly thrilled about seeing his 1-0 team ranked behind an 0-1 Milwaukee team.

"Mike's team 0-1 ahead of my team who's 1-0...yeah he got these reporters on the payroll."

I would like the record to reflect that Northern Arizona is now No. 4.

I would also like the record to reflect that the check has apparently stopped clearing.

The Wranglers earned this one.

Los Angeles had won nine of the previous 10 meetings between these teams. Northern Arizona responded by dropping 226.25 points on the Wildcats and winning by more than 41.

Two wins, a top-four Power Score and a statement victory over a team that has historically owned this matchup.

Northern Arizona wanted the rankings to notice.

Consider them noticed.

5. Los Angeles Wildcats —

Record: 1-2 | Power Score: 1915.48

This is where things get uncomfortable.

Los Angeles is 1-2, just lost by 41.55 points and remains in the top five.

Why?

Because three weeks of accumulated performance still matter more than one result.

The Wildcats scored 207.95 in Week 2, and that performance hasn't disappeared from the formula because Northern Arizona punched them in the mouth a week later.

That said, 184.70 wasn't enough in Week 3 and LA's position is becoming considerably less comfortable.

The Wildcats aren't being punished severely for one loss.

But another one will start changing the conversation.

6. Vegas Knight Hawks ▲ 4

Record: 0-3 | Power Score: 1758.45

Here we go.

I can already hear you.

"HOW IS AN 0-3 TEAM SIXTH?"

Because the Power Rankings are not the standings.

Vegas scored 239.20 points against Tucson.

That is the 10th-highest regular-season score in GLFL history.

It would have beaten Milwaukee. It would have beaten Cedar Rapids. It would have beaten Northern Arizona. It would have beaten Los Angeles, Boston, Philadelphia, Miami, Cleveland, Chicago and Brooklyn.

It did not beat Tucson.

That's fantasy football.

The Knight Hawks are 0-3 because wins are determined by who happens to be standing on the other side of the matchup. The Power Index is telling us something different: this does not currently look like one of the worst six teams in the league.

Even the GLFL playoff model agrees to an extent, giving Vegas a 46.05 percent postseason probability despite the winless start.

Vegas still needs to actually win a football game.

But if you're looking at 0-3 and assuming this team is dead, you haven't been paying attention.

7. Boston Brawlers ▼ 4

Record: 2-1 | Power Score: 1518.46

The defending champions just took the biggest fall of the week.

Boston entered Week 3 ranked third and left it seventh after Philadelphia handed the Brawlers a 227.00-167.05 beating.

This doesn't erase the championship. It doesn't erase the 2-0 start. It doesn't suddenly make Boston a bad team.

It does expose the first real crack we've seen this season.

And there's another interesting wrinkle developing here.

Philadelphia has now beaten Boston in consecutive meetings after Boston won six straight in the series from 2019 through 2024.

One loss isn't a crisis.

But 60-point losses tend to get the computer's attention.

8. Philadelphia Soul ▲ 1

Record: 2-1 | Power Score: 1492.59

And here's where Philadelphia fans are going to yell at me.

Yes, the Soul just beat Boston by 59.95.

Yes, they scored 227.

Yes, Boston is directly above them.

No, I'm not manually changing the formula because one result looks weird.

Philadelphia's 124.70-point Week 2 performance still counts just as much as its 227-point explosion in Week 3. The Power Index is cumulative, and right now Boston still holds a narrow 1518.46-1492.59 advantage.

But we're talking about a difference of fewer than 26 points in Power Score.

The gap is basically gone.

Philadelphia wanted a statement win.

They got one.

#FlySoulFly.

9. Miami Inferno ▼ 2

Record: 2-1 | Power Score: 1438.71

Only in the GLFL can you score 211.70 points, win by nearly 69 and fall two spots.

That's not an indictment of Miami's Week 3 performance.

It's what happens when Northern Arizona scores 226, Vegas scores 239 and the entire middle of the league decides to lose its mind at the same time.

Miami handled Brooklyn 211.70-143.00 and picked up the franchise's 100th all-time victory, including regular season and playoffs.

The Inferno are 2-1.

Their Power Score improved.

Their ranking didn't.

Sometimes everybody else just has a better week.

10. Cleveland Gladiators ▼ 4

Record: 1-2 | Power Score: 1246.64

This one needs context.

Cleveland lost De'Von Achane and Jalen Coker to injuries during its loss to Cedar Rapids. The pair entered the week projected for a combined 42.4 points and produced only 7.5.

That's brutal.

It's also not something the Power Index knows how to feel sorry about.

The formula sees 162.65 points, a loss and a 1-2 record.

So Cleveland falls four spots.

There is still enough talent here to climb back quickly, but the Gladiators have now missed two opportunities to secure the franchise's 100th regular-season victory.

They'll try again in Week 4.

11. Chicago Slaughter —

Record: 0-3 | Power Score: 995.50

Chicago scored exactly what it was projected to score in Week 3.

170.00 projected.

170.20 actual.

Normally, you'd take that.

Unfortunately, Milwaukee scored 205.15.

The Slaughter are now 0-3 for only the second time in franchise history, joining the 2021 team that eventually started 0-4.

There have already been whispers that GM Darrien Clay isn't interested in quietly riding this out and has been looking into adding star-level talent.

At 0-3, that makes sense.

The problem is that Chicago's next opponent is Milwaukee.

Again.

12. Brooklyn Bolts —

Record: 0-3 | Power Score: 890.70

Joe Burrow scored 46.4 points.

So at least we can officially retire the "Cory should've started Burrow" storyline from last week.

Brooklyn still scored only 143.00.

The Bolts are 0-3 for the first time since 2022, when they opened 0-5, and the current GLFL playoff model has effectively run out of optimism.

That doesn't mean Brooklyn is mathematically eliminated.

It means the turnaround needs to start immediately.

There is no No. 13 spot in these rankings.

Brooklyn should probably be thankful for that.

One Last Thing

If you take nothing else from this week's rankings, take this:

The standings tell you what has happened.

The Power Index is trying to tell you what kind of team produced it.

That's why 3-0 Cedar Rapids is No. 2.

That's why 2-1 Milwaukee is No. 3.

That's why 0-3 Vegas is No. 6.

And that's why we're not manually moving teams around because somebody's GM starts yelling in the group chat.

Although based on recent history, I'm sure that's coming anyway.

See you after Week 4.
