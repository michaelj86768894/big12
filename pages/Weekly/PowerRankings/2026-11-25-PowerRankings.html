GLFL WEEK 11 POWER RANKINGS: The Numbers Say Milwaukee, but the Momentum Says Otherwise

By Marcus Vance | GLFL Senior Analyst • November 25, 2026

There comes a point in every season when the numbers and the eye test begin telling two very different stories. Welcome to Week 11 of the Great Lakes Fantasy League.

Milwaukee has lost consecutive games. Tucson has a losing record. Northern Arizona just surrendered more than 220 points to Chicago. Yet those three franchises remain atop our Power Rankings with just three weeks remaining in the regular season.

Meanwhile, Miami owns the best record in football, Cedar Rapids has reclaimed the Erie Division lead, Cleveland just knocked off the Mustangs, and a Chicago team we practically buried six weeks ago is suddenly knocking on the postseason door.

Remember, the Power Index measures cumulative performance, not simply who won last Sunday. But if you asked me which teams I would least want to face in a playoff elimination game right now, my answer might look considerably different from the rankings below.

1. Milwaukee Mustangs (8-3)

Power Score: 7,416.1 | Last Week: 1 | —

Week 11: Lost to Cleveland, 152.05-145.85

For the first time since Milwaukee established itself as the team to beat this season, I have legitimate concerns about the Mustangs.

After winning eight consecutive games, Milwaukee has dropped two straight while averaging just 152.35 points during that stretch. This is the same franchise that spent the first half of the season rewriting the GLFL record book.

CeeDee Lamb delivered 39.2 points, J.J. McCarthy contributed 30.45 and Javonte Williams added 29.1. Unfortunately, Milwaukee also received a combined 4.4 points from Makai Lemon and Dalton Schultz while leaving Christian Elliss in the lineup on a bye week.

In a game decided by 6.2 points, that empty production is particularly difficult to overlook.

The Mustangs have officially secured their 11th postseason appearance, but they have surrendered control of the Erie Division to Cedar Rapids. Fortunately, they won't have to wait long for an opportunity to take it back.

Vance's Verdict: Still No. 1 on the strength of an outstanding season, but the Mustangs are no longer intimidating opponents the way they did a month ago.

2. Tucson Sugar Skulls (5-6)

Power Score: 7,060.6 | Last Week: 2 | —

Week 11: Lost to Cedar Rapids, 201.30-185.45

How can a team with a losing record remain the second-most powerful franchise in the GLFL?

Because Tucson continues to possess an offensive ceiling that nobody else in this league has demonstrated consistently. Just one week removed from scoring an all-time record 289.4 points, the Sugar Skulls received 54.2 from Patrick Mahomes, 35.8 from Jahmyr Gibbs and 25.1 from Derrick Henry.

And they still lost.

Ja'Marr Chase's disappointing 8.1-point performance on Monday night was the final blow. Tucson has invested heavily in assembling a championship-caliber roster, but that investment has not translated into enough victories.

Since opening the season 3-0, the Sugar Skulls have gone 2-6. That is no longer a temporary slump. That is two months of inconsistent results.

Vance's Verdict: Perhaps the most dangerous potential playoff team in the league. The problem is that Tucson still has to actually make the playoffs.

3. Northern Arizona Wranglers (6-5)

Power Score: 6,419.6 | Last Week: 3 | —

Week 11: Lost to Chicago, 221.75-168.65

Just when Northern Arizona appeared ready to establish some separation in the Superior Division, Chicago arrived with a rather emphatic reminder that nothing is guaranteed.

Tyler Shough scored 33.5 points and Breece Hall added 26.0, but the Wranglers had no answer for Dak Prescott's spectacular performance. Northern Arizona lost by 53.1 points and missed an opportunity to extend its division lead.

The good news? Tucson and Vegas both lost, leaving the Wranglers alone atop the Superior Division at 6-5.

The bad news? Chicago has now won seven of the last eight meetings between these franchises.

Vance's Verdict: Northern Arizona controls its own division race, but a one-game advantage is hardly comfortable with Tucson and Vegas chasing from behind.

4. Cedar Rapids Titans (8-3)

Power Score: 6,024.4 | Last Week: 4 | —

Week 11: Defeated Tucson, 201.30-185.45

At what point do we stop treating Cedar Rapids as a pleasant surprise and start treating them as a legitimate championship contender?

The Titans secured their sixth postseason appearance, their fifth winning season in franchise history and, for the first time ever, consecutive winning seasons.

Brock Purdy scored 45.75 points, Jonah Coleman exploded for 42.9 and Bucky Irving contributed 27.0. Ladd McConkey added another 25.7 after his costly zero-point outing against Miami the previous week.

Cedar Rapids also defeated Tucson for the first time since 2022 and reclaimed first place in the Erie Division.

Mitchell Smith has an opportunity to turn an already historic season into something considerably bigger when the Titans meet Milwaukee in Week 12.

Vance's Verdict: Fourth in the formula, but arguably playing better football than every team ranked above them.

5. Miami Inferno (9-2)

Power Score: 5,942.6 | Last Week: 6 | ▲ 1

Week 11: Defeated Los Angeles, 143.00-138.95

Miami has the best record in the GLFL and somehow continues to fly under the radar.

Without Drake Maye in the starting lineup, Cam Ward stepped in and delivered 40.7 points. Parker Washington added 24.3 and Devaughn Vele contributed an unexpected 19.6 as the Inferno snapped a four-game losing streak against Los Angeles.

It wasn't pretty. Miami's running backs combined for just 15.5 points, Tyler Warren scored 4.2 and the Inferno barely escaped against a three-win opponent.

But consider this: Miami has won its last four games by a combined 18 points. Richard Head's team keeps finding ways to survive games that could easily have gone the other direction.

Miami has officially clinched its ninth postseason appearance, and after finishing 3-11 last season, the Inferno now sit alone atop the league standings.

Vance's Verdict: Winning ugly is still winning. However, Miami's recent scoring decline is worth monitoring as the postseason approaches.

6. Vegas Knight Hawks (5-6)

Power Score: 5,809.5 | Last Week: 5 | ▼ 1

Week 11: Lost to Boston, 161.80-154.55

Vegas has become one of the most unpredictable franchises in the GLFL.

In Week 10, the Knight Hawks scored 246.35 points and delivered the largest margin of victory in league history. One week later, they managed just 154.55 in a crucial game against a direct postseason competitor.

Chuba Hubbard continued his excellent season with 34 points, while Antonio Williams added 26.2 and Trey McBride scored 16.8. However, Daniel Jones contributed just 10.5 at quarterback and Jordyn Tyson finished with three points.

Vegas has now lost two of its last three games after winning four consecutive contests to recover from an 0-4 start.

Dave Nguyen deserves considerable credit for rebuilding this roster during the season, but the Knight Hawks are running out of opportunities to turn that improvement into a playoff berth.

Vance's Verdict: Capable of beating anyone by 100 points or losing a game they should win. That's a dangerous combination this late in the season.

7. Cleveland Gladiators (7-4)

Power Score: 5,445.0 | Last Week: 7 | —

Week 11: Defeated Milwaukee, 152.05-145.85

One week after suffering the most lopsided defeat in GLFL history, Cleveland responded by knocking off the No. 1 team in the Power Rankings.

That is precisely the kind of response you want to see from a postseason contender.

Bryce Young delivered 33.5 points, Ashton Jeanty added 29.9 and Justice Hill contributed 21.6. Calvin Ridley also exceeded expectations with 21.8 points.

Josh Thurmer's team has won six of its last seven games after starting the season 1-3. Yet because of the strength of the Erie Division, the Gladiators remain in third place behind Cedar Rapids and Milwaukee.

Now comes a matchup against a resurgent Chicago team that has scored 484.15 points over the last two weeks.

Vance's Verdict: Seventh in the Power Rankings, but Cleveland's recent record suggests this team deserves considerably more respect.

8. Boston Brawlers (6-5)

Power Score: 5,369.4 | Last Week: 8 | —

Week 11: Defeated Vegas, 161.80-154.55

The defending champions are quietly beginning to stabilize.

Boston has won consecutive games after falling to 4-5, and its victory over Vegas could prove particularly important when the final postseason standings are determined.

Jayden Daniels scored 34.95 points, Zay Flowers added 21.4 and Khalil Shakir contributed 18.3. Boston's defensive starters combined for 45.25 points, providing valuable production on a week when Jonathan Taylor managed just 7.7.

The Brawlers have now won three consecutive meetings against Vegas.

Chris Cholka's team has not consistently looked like the championship roster we watched last December, but the Brawlers are once again positioning themselves for an opportunity to defend their title.

Vance's Verdict: Nobody should confuse Boston's inconsistency with an inability to win meaningful games. We've seen how that story ends before.

9. Chicago Slaughter (5-6)

Power Score: 5,023.9 | Last Week: 9 | —

Week 11: Defeated Northern Arizona, 221.75-168.65

Well, well, well. Look who's still alive.

At 1-5, Chicago appeared destined for another disappointing season. Five weeks later, the Slaughter have won four of their last five games and ESPN gives them a 42 percent chance of reaching the postseason.

Dak Prescott followed his 65.85-point Week 10 performance with an incredible 71.15 points against Northern Arizona. That's 137 fantasy points in two games.

Emmett Johnson added 33.3, George Pickens scored 28.8 and Jacob Rodriguez contributed 22.25 as Chicago recorded its second consecutive 220-point performance.

The problem is the schedule. Cleveland, Tucson and Miami await over the final three weeks. Those opponents have a combined record of 21-12 and include some of the most explosive rosters in the league.

But after watching Chicago dismantle Los Angeles and Northern Arizona in consecutive weeks, perhaps those teams should be worried about facing the Slaughter, too.

Vance's Verdict: Forget the ninth-place ranking for a moment. Chicago might be the hottest team in the GLFL right now.

10. Los Angeles Wildcats (3-8)

Power Score: 4,642.5 | Last Week: 10 | —

Week 11: Lost to Miami, 143.00-138.95

Los Angeles officially will not finish with a winning record for the first time since 2017.

For a franchise that has enjoyed years of sustained regular-season success, that is a remarkable development.

Josh Allen scored 31.2 points and James Cook III added 23.2, but the Wildcats received nothing from Oronde Gadsden at tight end and fell just 4.05 points short of upsetting the league's best team.

ESPN gives Los Angeles a one percent chance of reaching the postseason. Technically, the Wildcats remain alive, but their path requires an extraordinary combination of results over the final three weeks.

Vance's Verdict: One of the league's most consistently successful franchises is approaching an unusually early offseason.

11. Philadelphia Soul (3-8)

Power Score: 4,463.1 | Last Week: 11 | —

Week 11: Lost to Brooklyn, 201.10-148.95

There are disappointing losses, and then there is becoming the first team to lose to an opponent that entered the game 0-10.

Philadelphia has now lost seven consecutive games after opening the season 3-1. What once looked like a promising campaign has turned into one of the most dramatic collapses of the 2026 season.

C.J. Stroud scored 40.4 points and Chris Olave added 27.7, but Philadelphia received just 13.7 combined points from Garrett Wilson and Emeka Egbuka.

Like Los Angeles, the Soul have just a one percent chance of reaching the postseason according to ESPN.

Vance's Verdict: Philadelphia went from playoff contender to the answer to a Brooklyn trivia question in seven weeks.

12. Brooklyn Bolts (1-10)

Power Score: 2,993.3 | Last Week: 12 | —

Week 11: Defeated Philadelphia, 201.10-148.95

BREAK UP THE BOLTS!

After ten consecutive losses, Brooklyn finally entered the win column and did so in convincing fashion, scoring more than 200 points and defeating Philadelphia by 52.15.

Joe Burrow contributed 41.9 points, while Brooklyn's receiving trio of Malik Nabers, Tetairoa McMillan and DJ Moore combined for 75.7. David Montgomery added 21.4 and David Bailey chipped in another 18.75 on defense.

The 2013 Boston Brawlers can rest easy knowing their infamous 0-14 season remains unique in GLFL history.

Brooklyn remains mathematically eliminated from postseason contention, but Cory Colvin's team has finally demonstrated that it can play spoiler over the final three weeks.

Vance's Verdict: Still No. 12, but for the first time all season, writing this section was actually enjoyable.

The most fascinating development is Chicago's emergence as a legitimate threat to Tucson and Vegas. Both franchises spent significant portions of the season looking like potential championship contenders, and now both are tied with a team that started 1-5.

At least two of the six primary contenders for those remaining four spots will be watching the postseason from home. Considering the talent assembled on these rosters, that is going to make for a particularly painful December somewhere in the GLFL.

The Final Word

For all the attention paid to Milwaukee's record-breaking performances, Tucson's blockbuster trades and Miami's remarkable turnaround, the defining characteristic of this season might ultimately be its unpredictability.

Milwaukee is 8-3 but hasn't won in two weeks. Tucson is the second-ranked team in the league but currently owns a losing record. Miami has won four consecutive games without winning any of them by more than seven points. Chicago was practically eliminated from the conversation a month ago and is now one game below .500.

And Brooklyn finally won a football game.

With three weeks remaining, the Power Rankings are becoming less of a championship forecast and more of a reminder that past performance guarantees absolutely nothing.

Next week, Milwaukee and Cedar Rapids meet with first place in the Erie Division on the line. Chicago gets another opportunity to prove its resurgence is legitimate against Cleveland. And the Superior Division remains entirely up for grabs.

We've spent eleven weeks trying to figure out who the best team in the GLFL is.

I'm not convinced we're any closer to an answer.

The GLFL Power Index evaluates cumulative points scored, overall winning percentage and winning percentage against the league median. Rankings reflect season-long performance rather than subjective weekly assessments.
