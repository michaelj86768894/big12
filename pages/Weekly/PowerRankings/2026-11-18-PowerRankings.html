GLFL WEEK 10 POWER RANKINGS: Milwaukee Holds the Throne, Tucson Makes History and Vegas Keeps Climbing

By Marcus Vance | GLFL National • November 18, 2026

The 8-game win streak is over. The scoring record is gone. And the league's most dangerous division just got even more complicated. Welcome to the Week 10 GLFL Power Rankings, where Milwaukee remains No. 1 despite losing, Tucson is rewriting history, and Vegas has officially become everybody's problem.

Week 10 delivered one of the most explosive scoring performances in league history, a new all-time margin-of-victory record, and a stunning end to Milwaukee's eight-game winning streak.

But these rankings aren't determined by one Sunday. They're driven by season-long production, winning percentage, and performance against the league median.

And that explains why the Mustangs still occupy the throne, even as the rest of the contenders close in.

1. Milwaukee Mustangs (8-2)

Power Score: 7,398.0 | Previous: 1 | Movement: —

The eight-game winning streak is over. The dream of a 13-1 season is gone. And for the first time since Week 1, Michael Bautch's Mustangs are coming off a loss.

So why are they still No. 1?

Because one loss doesn't erase nine weeks of dominance. Milwaukee remains 8-2, owns the league's highest Power Score, and has established the strongest overall résumé through ten weeks.

But the 167.80-158.85 loss to Northern Arizona exposed something worth watching. With Michael Wilson lost for the season, the Mustangs' offensive depth isn't quite what it was a month ago.

Milwaukee's lead over Tucson is now just 376.1 Power Score points. The cushion is shrinking, and the schedule isn't getting easier.

Vance's Verdict: Still the league's most complete body of work. But the rest of the GLFL finally has proof that Milwaukee can be beaten.

2. Tucson Sugar Skulls (5-5)

Power Score: 7,021.9 | Previous: 2 | Movement: —

I don't care that Tucson is 5-5. I don't care that the Sugar Skulls have struggled to turn their offensive production into wins.

When a team scores 289.40 points and establishes a new all-time GLFL record, you pay attention.

Patrick Mahomes scored 61.90. Jahmyr Gibbs delivered 55.20. Ja'Marr Chase contributed 43.10. Isaiah Likely added 30.70.

It was an offensive masterpiece, and it pushed Tucson's season scoring average to approximately 212.8 points per game. No GLFL franchise has ever finished a season averaging more than 200.

But the elephant in the room remains: How is a team this explosive only .500?

The Sugar Skulls have gone 1-4 over their last five games. They may have the league's most dangerous offense, but they haven't consistently converted it into victories.

And then there's the Jahmyr Gibbs saga. The rumored trade request, the reported desire to join Milwaukee, and GM Mason Bautch's comments about Gibbs deserving to be happy have all fueled speculation. Yet Gibbs remains in Tucson after the deadline and just delivered 55.20 points.

Maybe that's the best response to the rumors. Or maybe it's the most awkward 55-point performance in franchise history.

Vance's Verdict: Nobody has a higher ceiling. Nobody is more difficult to figure out. If Tucson makes the playoffs, good luck to whoever draws them.

3. Northern Arizona Wranglers (6-4)

Power Score: 6,367.0 | Previous: 4 | Movement: ▲ 1

Finally.

For weeks, Northern Arizona watched Tucson stumble and repeatedly failed to take full advantage. But when the opportunity came against the league's top-ranked team, Jason Richmond's Wranglers delivered.

A 167.80-158.85 victory over Milwaukee snapped the Mustangs' eight-game winning streak and gave Northern Arizona sole possession of first place in the Superior Division.

Christian McCaffrey's 53.30-point explosion was the difference. And with the rest of the division fighting for position, this victory couldn't have arrived at a better time.

Northern Arizona hasn't always looked convincing, but its 6-4 record and season-long production have earned it a place among the league's top three.

Vance's Verdict: You wanted a statement victory? Beating Milwaukee and ending its winning streak certainly qualifies. Now prove it wasn't a one-week event.

4. Cedar Rapids Titans (7-3)

Power Score: 5,940.0 | Previous: 3 | Movement: ▼ 1

This one is going to sting for a while.

Cedar Rapids had Miami on the ropes. Brock Purdy delivered 59.55 points, Nico Collins contributed 31.40, and the Titans were positioned for a massive victory over another contender.

Instead, a late injury designation left Ladd McConkey in the starting lineup with zero points, and Cedar Rapids lost 157.20-151.85.

A 5.35-point defeat with an empty receiver slot. Brutal.

The Titans remain 7-3, but with Milwaukee sitting at 8-2, this was a costly missed opportunity in the Erie Division race.

The good news? Cedar Rapids is still producing enough offense to compete with the best. The bad news? There isn't much room left for mistakes.

Vance's Verdict: A contender that just let a huge opportunity slip away. The talent is there. The execution needs to match it.

5. Vegas Knight Hawks (5-5)

Power Score: 5,898.9 | Previous: 7 | Movement: ▲ 2

If you're still treating Vegas like the team that started 0-4, you're making a serious mistake.

Dave Nguyen's Knight Hawks have won five of their last six games, and their latest victory was downright historic.

Vegas demolished Cleveland 246.35-102.65, establishing a new GLFL record with a 143.70-point victory margin.

Chuba Hubbard scored 44.20, Jaxon Smith-Njigba delivered 37.10, Drew Lock contributed 33.00, and Bhayshul Tuten added 25.30.

Remember when that Tucson trade looked like a desperate gamble? It now looks like the move that saved Vegas' season.

Smith-Njigba and Tuten have become major contributors, while the Knight Hawks have transformed into a genuine playoff threat.

Vegas is now only one game behind Northern Arizona in the Superior Division, tied with Tucson at 5-5.

Vance's Verdict: The most dangerous turnaround story in the league. Nobody wants to play Vegas right now.

6. Miami Inferno (8-2)

Power Score: 5,843.0 | Previous: 6 | Movement: —

Eight wins. Two losses. A winning season secured. And somehow, only sixth in the Power Rankings.

Welcome to the GLFL.

Richard Head's Inferno survived a disastrous afternoon from Drake Maye, who exited at halftime and finished with just 6.50 points.

Miami still managed to defeat Cedar Rapids 157.20-151.85, thanks largely to Parker Washington's 34.20 points and Deebo Samuel Sr.'s 28.90.

It wasn't pretty, but it counts exactly the same.

Miami has now secured its seventh winning season, its first since 2022, and continues one of the league's best turnaround stories after last year's 3-11 disaster.

The concern is whether Miami can maintain its offensive production if Maye's injury becomes a lingering issue.

Vance's Verdict: The record says elite. The Power Score says there are several teams with stronger overall production. Miami won't care as long as it keeps winning.

7. Cleveland Gladiators (6-4)

Power Score: 5,526.1 | Previous: 5 | Movement: ▼ 2

Well, that escalated quickly.

Cleveland entered Week 10 riding five consecutive victories. The Gladiators had transformed a 1-3 start into a 6-3 record and looked like one of the league's hottest teams.

Then Vegas happened.

A 246.35-102.65 loss. The largest defeat in GLFL history.

Puka Nacua scored 0.90. Jalen Coker managed 0.30. Tucker Kraft finished with 3.90.

Cleveland's entire offense simply disappeared.

Josh Thurmer has done an impressive job turning this season around, but Week 10 exposed how quickly things can unravel.

The Gladiators remain 6-4 and very much in the playoff conversation. But the momentum they worked so hard to build has taken a serious hit.

Vance's Verdict: One catastrophic performance doesn't erase five straight wins. But Cleveland needs an immediate response before this becomes something more concerning.

8. Boston Brawlers (5-5)

Power Score: 5,452.1 | Previous: 8 | Movement: —

The defending champions aren't dead yet.

Boston finally stopped the bleeding with a 162.70-140.40 victory over Philadelphia.

Trevor Lawrence delivered 57.30 points, Harold Fannin Jr. added 20.10, and the Brawlers found just enough production to get back to .500.

After starting 3-1, Boston had lost four of its previous five. This was a desperately needed victory.

Chris Cholka's team still hasn't rediscovered the consistency that carried it to the 2025 championship, but at 5-5, the postseason remains within reach.

Vance's Verdict: The championship pedigree is real. The current performance is inconsistent. Boston needs more than one good week to convince me it's truly back.

9. Chicago Slaughter (4-6)

Power Score: 5,103.9 | Previous: 11 | Movement: ▲ 2

Where has THIS Chicago team been all season?

The Slaughter exploded for 262.40 points against Los Angeles, the third-highest regular-season score in GLFL history.

Dak Prescott scored 65.85. George Pickens delivered 50.50. Emmett Johnson contributed 44.10, and Cam Skattebo added 35.20.

Chicago won by 110.20 points and jumped two positions in the rankings.

After beginning 0-4, Darrien Clay's team has gone 4-2.

It's a remarkable turnaround, but the Slaughter still face an uphill battle to reach the postseason.

Vance's Verdict: Chicago's ceiling is suddenly undeniable. The problem is that the Slaughter spent the first month digging a hole they may not have enough time to escape.

10. Los Angeles Wildcats (3-7)

Power Score: 4,531.5 | Previous: 10 | Movement: —

The Wildcats have become one of the league's most frustrating teams to evaluate.

There's talent here. Josh Allen, James Cook, and Brock Bowers give Los Angeles legitimate offensive weapons.

But the results simply haven't followed.

A 262.40-152.20 loss to Chicago drops Elizabeth Bautch's team to 3-7.

And with only four games remaining, LA is guaranteed to finish with fewer than nine victories for the first time since 2018.

That's an unfamiliar position for a franchise accustomed to winning.

Vance's Verdict: The talent says LA should be better. The standings say otherwise. Time is running out.

11. Philadelphia Soul (3-7)

Power Score: 4,524.0 | Previous: 9 | Movement: ▼ 2

Six straight losses.

Let that sink in.

Philadelphia started the season 3-1 and looked positioned to compete for a playoff berth.

Now the Soul are 3-7, having lost every game since Week 4.

The latest defeat, 162.70-140.40 against Boston, was another frustrating afternoon for Brian Simmons.

C.J. Stroud delivered 42.55 points, but the supporting cast couldn't produce enough to keep pace.

Philadelphia has fallen two spots and now sits just 7.5 Power Score points ahead of Los Angeles.

Vance's Verdict: The early-season promise feels like a distant memory. Philadelphia needs to rediscover something, anything, before the season completely unravels.

12. Brooklyn Bolts (0-10)

Power Score: 2,993.3 | Previous: 12 | Movement: —

There really isn't much left to say.

Brooklyn is 0-10. The Bolts are officially eliminated from playoff contention.

And their latest defeat came against a Tucson team that scored 289.40 points.

Joe Burrow delivered an incredible 68.25 points, yet Brooklyn still lost by 133.50.

That's the kind of season Cory Colvin is enduring.

The Bolts have now matched last year's ten-loss total, and with four games remaining, the possibility of an 0-14 season is becoming increasingly real.

Vance's Verdict: The playoffs are gone. The only question now is whether Brooklyn can avoid becoming the league's most infamous winless team.

The Power Rankings Tighten at the Top

Milwaukee remains No. 1, but the Mustangs' lead over Tucson has narrowed to 376.1 points.

Northern Arizona has climbed into third, while Cedar Rapids, Vegas and Miami are separated by only 97.0 points from fourth through sixth.

The biggest risers this week are Vegas and Chicago, each climbing two positions. Cleveland and Philadelphia suffer the largest drops, each falling two spots.

But perhaps the most fascinating development is the Superior Division.

Three teams from that division now occupy spots inside the top five: Tucson at No. 2, Northern Arizona at No. 3, and Vegas at No. 5.

And only one of them can win the division title.

Vance's Final Word

Here's what makes the 2026 season so fascinating:

Milwaukee has the best overall résumé. Tucson has the greatest offensive ceiling we've ever witnessed. Northern Arizona just defeated the league's top team. Vegas has transformed from a disaster into a legitimate threat. And Miami keeps winning regardless of what the rankings say.

With four weeks remaining, there isn't a single contender that should feel safe.

The Mustangs still hold the throne.

But the rest of the league is getting closer.
