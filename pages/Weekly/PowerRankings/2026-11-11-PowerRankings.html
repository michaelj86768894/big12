GLFL WEEK 9 POWER RANKINGS: Milwaukee Pulls Away, Cleveland Surges and Tucson's No. 2 Ranking Is Under Siege

By Marcus Vance | Senior GLFL Analyst • November 11, 2026

Milwaukee is separating itself from the field. Cleveland is charging toward the top. Cedar Rapids is back in the conversation. And Tucson somehow remains the second-ranked team in the Great Lakes Fantasy League despite losing five of its last six games. Welcome to Week 9, where the Power Index and the eye test are having a very public disagreement.

The Power Index measures season-long performance using points scored, winning percentage, and results against the league median. It rewards teams that have built a strong statistical foundation, even when their recent results tell a different story.

And no team illustrates that distinction better than Tucson. The Sugar Skulls are still sitting at No. 2. But with five weeks remaining and the Superior Division slipping away, that ranking is beginning to feel less like a statement of strength and more like a warning about how quickly fortunes can change.

1. Milwaukee Mustangs (8-1)

Power Index: 6,953.6 | Previous: 1 | —

There is Milwaukee, and then there is everybody else.

Michael Bautch's Mustangs have opened a staggering 1,030.5-point advantage over the next closest team in the Power Index. At 8-1, Milwaukee owns the league's best record, has clinched its 11th winning season in franchise history, and continues to find ways to win even when everything doesn't go according to plan.

Kenneth Walker III erupted for 44.30 points in Week 9, while CeeDee Lamb added 37.50 in a comfortable victory over Brooklyn. Milwaukee didn't even need a particularly strong performance from Jared Goff to secure its eighth win.

The concern? Injuries. Wide receiver Michael Wilson is now lost for the season with a neck injury, creating another challenge for a roster that has already been forced to navigate personnel issues.

But here's what separates Milwaukee from the rest of the league: the Mustangs aren't dependent on one player to carry them. They've demonstrated an ability to win in multiple ways, and their statistical advantage is becoming impossible to ignore.

Vance's Verdict: Milwaukee is the clear No. 1. The only legitimate debate right now is who deserves to be ranked second.

2. Tucson Sugar Skulls (4-5)

Power Index: 5,923.1 | Previous: 2 | —

I can already hear the complaints.

"Marcus, how can a team that's lost five of its last six games still be ranked second?"

It's a fair question. And the answer is that the Power Index measures an entire season, not just the last month and a half.

Tucson still owns one of the league's strongest scoring profiles. But let's not pretend everything is fine in the desert.

Mason Bautch's Sugar Skulls started 3-0 and looked virtually unstoppable. Since then, they've gone 1-5. Their latest defeat came against Northern Arizona, ending a seven-game winning streak in that rivalry and dropping Tucson below .500.

And then there's the Jahmyr Gibbs situation.

GLFN's Alex Ramirez has reported that Gibbs wants a trade to a contender, and Mason Bautch has publicly indicated that Milwaukee could be a potential destination.

No trade has been completed, but the speculation adds another layer of uncertainty to an already turbulent season.

Tucson's talent remains undeniable. Ja'Marr Chase scored 39.50 points in Week 9, Patrick Mahomes delivered 38.90, and Amon-Ra St. Brown added 22.10. Yet the Sugar Skulls still lost.

At some point, a team has to turn production into victories. Tucson hasn't done that consistently in nearly two months.

Vance's Verdict: The numbers say No. 2. The recent results say something very different. If Tucson doesn't start winning immediately, this ranking is going to become increasingly difficult to defend.

3. Cedar Rapids Titans (7-2)

Power Index: 5,681.7 | Previous: 7 | ▲ 4

Welcome back to the top three, Cedar Rapids.

After a disappointing Week 8 performance, Mitch Smith's Titans responded by hanging 222.60 points on the defending champion Boston Brawlers.

Brock Purdy scored 47.60, Bucky Irving added 34.50, and defensive standout Carson Schwesinger delivered 30.75. The Titans also got 22.80 from Jonah Coleman and 19.50 from Barrett Carter.

Perhaps most impressive? Cedar Rapids scored 222.60 despite getting absolutely nothing from starting wide receiver Ladd McConkey.

At 7-2, the Titans are tied with Miami for the second-best record in the league. They're also just 241.4 Power Index points behind Tucson, making the battle for second place much closer than the standings initially suggest.

Cedar Rapids has demonstrated that it belongs among the championship contenders. Now the challenge is maintaining that consistency through the final five weeks.

Vance's Verdict: The biggest upward mover of Week 9. Cedar Rapids looks far more like a top-three team than its previous No. 7 ranking suggested.

4. Northern Arizona Wranglers (5-4)

Power Index: 5,677.0 | Previous: 5 | ▲ 1

Jason Richmond's Wranglers finally took advantage of Tucson's collapse, and they did it in the most satisfying way possible: by beating Tucson themselves.

Northern Arizona's 212.30-point performance snapped a seven-game losing streak against the Sugar Skulls and pushed the Wranglers into first place in the Superior Division.

Jordan Love was spectacular with 59.90 fantasy points. Christian McCaffrey contributed 28.70, while Tee Higgins and KC Concepcion combined for another 46.00.

The victory was a statement, but it also raises an important question: can Northern Arizona finally sustain some momentum?

The Wranglers have spent much of the season alternating between impressive victories and frustrating defeats. At 5-4, they've now positioned themselves to control the division.

And look at that Power Index. Northern Arizona trails Cedar Rapids by just 4.7 points.

That's essentially a statistical dead heat for third place.

Vance's Verdict: The Superior Division is there for the taking. Northern Arizona finally grabbed the opportunity. Now let's see if it can hold on.

5. Cleveland Gladiators (6-3)

Power Index: 5,413.7 | Previous: 8 | ▲ 3

If we're ranking teams strictly by how they're playing right now, Cleveland might deserve to be even higher.

Five consecutive victories. A 6-3 record after starting 1-3. And a 223.05-point explosion against Los Angeles in Week 9.

Josh Thurmer has completely transformed the trajectory of this season.

Bryce Young scored 47.10 points, Puka Nacua added 46.50, and Matthew Golden delivered 39.40. That's 133.00 fantasy points from just three players.

Cleveland's offensive ceiling is suddenly among the highest in the league.

The Gladiators also snapped an eight-game losing streak against Los Angeles, adding a little historical satisfaction to an already dominant performance.

At No. 5, Cleveland has officially entered the Power Index's upper tier. And given its current form, I wouldn't be surprised if the Gladiators climb even higher.

Vance's Verdict: Five straight wins. The week's highest score. A three-place jump. Cleveland isn't a surprise contender anymore. It's a contender, period.

6. Miami Inferno (7-2)

Power Index: 5,377.5 | Previous: 3 | ▼ 3

Before anybody in Miami starts throwing things at their screen, let's establish something: the Inferno are 7-2.

Seven wins. Two losses. First place in the Michigan Division.

That's an excellent season.

But the Power Index doesn't simply reward records. Miami's 155.60-point performance against Vegas was enough to secure a victory, but it wasn't enough to keep pace with the massive scoring performances delivered by Cleveland, Cedar Rapids, and Northern Arizona.

Drake Maye led the way with 37.25 points, while Luther Burden III contributed 24.20. Miami escaped with a 6.60-point win over a Vegas team that left a 32.50-point performance sitting on its bench.

Richard Head's Inferno deserve credit for finding ways to win. But the Power Index is beginning to question whether their statistical profile matches their impressive record.

Vance's Verdict: A three-place drop doesn't mean Miami is suddenly a bad team. It means the competition around them just delivered much stronger performances.

7. Vegas Knight Hawks (4-5)

Power Index: 4,968.0 | Previous: 4 | ▼ 3

Vegas finally lost.

After opening the season 0-4, the Knight Hawks had rattled off four consecutive victories and transformed themselves into one of the league's most compelling stories.

That streak ended in Week 9 with a narrow 155.60-149.00 defeat against Miami.

The frustrating part is that Vegas had a potential winning performance sitting on its bench. Kayshon Boutte scored 32.50 points but was projected for just 5.50, making the decision to leave him out of the lineup entirely understandable.

Jaxon Smith-Njigba and Trey McBride, the major acquisitions from Tucson, combined for just 28.50 points after previously delivering some spectacular performances.

The loss drops Vegas to 4-5, one game behind Northern Arizona in the Superior Division.

But don't forget what Dave Nguyen has accomplished. This team looked finished after four weeks. Now it's a legitimate playoff contender.

Vance's Verdict: The three-place decline hurts, but the bigger picture remains encouraging. Vegas is still dangerous, and the Superior Division is far from settled.

8. Boston Brawlers (4-5)

Power Index: 4,779.4 | Previous: 6 | ▼ 2

The defending champions are officially in trouble.

Boston has lost four of its last five games after starting the season 3-1, and its latest defeat came in ugly fashion.

Cedar Rapids hung 222.60 points on the Brawlers, who managed just 146.90 in response.

The loss also marked Boston's 100th franchise defeat, an unfortunate milestone for an organization that entered the year celebrating its first championship.

Jayden Daniels scored 32.30 and Myles Garrett added 20.50, but the Brawlers simply couldn't generate enough production across the lineup.

At 4-5, Boston remains among the eight teams ESPN currently gives at least a 51% chance of reaching the postseason.

But there's a difference between having a chance and playing like a playoff team.

Vance's Verdict: Chris Cholka needs answers, and he needs them quickly. The championship defense is approaching a crossroads.

9. Philadelphia Soul (3-6)

Power Index: 4,263.8 | Previous: 10 | ▲ 1

How does a team move up in the rankings while losing its fifth consecutive game?

Welcome to the Power Index.

Philadelphia's 179.60-point performance against Chicago was competitive enough to improve its relative statistical standing, even though the Soul lost 183.85-179.60.

C.J. Stroud was spectacular, scoring 65.20 fantasy points. Bijan Robinson added 35.70. The problem was the rest of the lineup.

And then there's the lineup decision that may haunt Brian Simmons for the remainder of the season.

Nik Bonitto scored 15.00 points on Philadelphia's bench, while starting defensive player Trent McDuffie recorded zero. Had Bonitto started, Philadelphia would have won by 10.75.

Instead, the Soul are 3-6 after opening the season 3-1.

Vance's Verdict: The Power Index gives Philadelphia a small promotion. The standings offer no such consolation. Five straight losses is five straight losses.

10. Los Angeles Wildcats (3-6)

Power Index: 4,241.3 | Previous: 9 | ▼ 1

Los Angeles had an opportunity to build momentum after upsetting Tucson in Week 8.

Instead, Cleveland delivered a 95.90-point beatdown.

The Wildcats managed just 127.15 points, with Josh Jacobs contributing only 0.50 from the running back position.

Josh Allen scored 32.20 and Brock Bowers added 27.30, but those efforts were nowhere near enough to keep pace with Cleveland's explosive offense.

Elizabeth Bautch recently became just the second GM in league history to reach 100 career victories, joining Milwaukee's Michael Bautch.

It's a remarkable career achievement, but the Wildcats' current season is becoming increasingly difficult to salvage.

Vance's Verdict: The talent is there in spots. The consistency isn't. At 3-6, Los Angeles needs a major turnaround.

11. Chicago Slaughter (3-6)

Power Index: 4,159.4 | Previous: 11 | —

Chicago won. Philadelphia lost. And somehow, neither team looks particularly comfortable heading into the final five weeks.

The Slaughter edged Philadelphia 183.85-179.60, improving to 3-6 behind strong performances from Dak Prescott, Christian Watson, and defensive standout Arvell Reese.

Prescott delivered 37.85, Watson added 35.10, and Reese contributed 32.50.

It was a solid performance for Darrien Clay's team, and Chicago deserves credit for taking advantage of Philadelphia's lineup mistake.

But the Slaughter remain 11th in the Power Index and three games below .500. One victory doesn't erase the struggles that preceded it.

Vance's Verdict: Chicago is showing some fight. Whether that fight translates into a legitimate late-season playoff push remains to be seen.

12. Brooklyn Bolts (0-9)

Power Index: 2,681.5 | Previous: 12 | —

Nine games. Nine losses. Zero victories.

Brooklyn's season has officially entered historically uncomfortable territory.

The Bolts fell 182.05-145.35 against Milwaukee, extending the worst start in franchise history.

Joe Burrow scored 54.25 fantasy points, which should have given Brooklyn a fighting chance.

Instead, the Bolts received minimal contributions from several other positions and lost by 36.70.

The Power Index gap between Brooklyn and Chicago is now 1,477.9 points. That's an enormous separation between the bottom two teams.

Cory Colvin has already suggested that his focus may be shifting toward the future if the league continues its transition toward a keeper format.

At this point, that may be the most reasonable approach.

Vance's Verdict: Brooklyn isn't simply last. It's in a category of its own. The only remaining question is when, or whether, the first victory finally arrives.

Vance's Week 9 Takeaways

Milwaukee Has Created Its Own Tier

A 1,030.5-point advantage over second place is extraordinary. The Mustangs aren't simply leading the Power Index. They're dominating it.

Second Through Sixth Is a Dogfight

Tucson sits at 5,923.1, but Cedar Rapids, Northern Arizona, Cleveland, and Miami are all within 545.6 points of the Sugar Skulls. The competition behind Milwaukee is much closer than the ranking numbers suggest.

Cleveland Is the Team Nobody Wants to Face

Five consecutive victories and a 223-point Week 9 performance. Cleveland's recent form is as impressive as anyone's.

Tucson's Ranking Is Living on Borrowed Time

The Sugar Skulls still have the season-long numbers to justify No. 2. But at 4-5, with five losses in six games and questions surrounding Jahmyr Gibbs, that statistical foundation won't protect them forever.

The Playoff Bubble Is Getting Crowded

ESPN currently projects eight teams with at least a 51% postseason probability. Only seven qualify. Boston, Tucson, and Vegas are among the teams that should be feeling the pressure.

Vance's Final Word

Nine weeks into the season, the GLFL has one clear statistical powerhouse and a crowded group of teams fighting to establish themselves behind it.

Milwaukee has separated. Cedar Rapids has rebounded. Cleveland has surged. Northern Arizona has seized its opportunity. Miami continues to win. Vegas remains dangerous.

And Tucson?

Tucson is still ranked second.

But here's the uncomfortable truth for Mason Bautch: power rankings don't award playoff spots.

Victories do.

And right now, Tucson needs those a whole lot more than it needs another impressive Power Index score.
