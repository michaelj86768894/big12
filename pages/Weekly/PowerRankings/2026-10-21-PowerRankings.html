GLFL WEEK 6 POWER RANKINGS: Milwaukee Reclaims No. 1, Tucson Survives the Vegas Disaster

By Marcus Vance | GLFL National • October 21, 2026

Milwaukee is back on top. Tucson just lost by nearly 60 points to a team it traded three players to, yet remains No. 2. Cedar Rapids is climbing, Miami is cooling, and Vegas has suddenly become the league's most interesting 2-4 team. Welcome to the Week 6 GLFL Power Rankings, where the numbers have their say whether the general managers like it or not.

Before we get into the rankings, let's address the obvious.

Yes, Tucson got embarrassed.

Yes, Vegas scored 216.20 points.

And yes, the Sugar Skulls are still ranked second.

That's not a typo.

The GLFL Power Index evaluates the full season, not just the most recent Sunday. It rewards production, winning percentage and performance against the league's weekly scoring median.

A disastrous week matters. But so do the five weeks that came before it.

That distinction is especially important in a season where Milwaukee and Tucson are still averaging more than 200 points per game, something no GLFL team has ever accomplished over a full season.

Milwaukee's fifth consecutive victory has returned the Mustangs to the top of the rankings, while Cedar Rapids has reclaimed third place following its bounce-back win over Chicago.

At the bottom, Brooklyn remains winless, Chicago has reached 100 regular-season losses, and Vegas and Los Angeles are making it increasingly difficult to dismiss their playoff hopes.

Six weeks are complete.

The standings are taking shape.

And the arguments are only getting louder.

1. Milwaukee Mustangs (5-1) ▲ 1

Power Index: 4894.93 | Week 6: W vs. Miami, 169.40-138.65

Back where they believe they belong.

Milwaukee reclaimed the top spot in the Power Rankings with a convincing 30.75-point victory over Miami, extending its winning streak to five games.

The Mustangs have now won every game since their opening-week defeat, and their ability to win in different ways is becoming one of the most compelling stories of the season.

They've produced historic offensive explosions. They've survived narrow finishes. And now they've handled one of the league's hottest teams without needing a 200-point performance.

Kenneth Walker III led Milwaukee with 35.90 points in Week 6, while Michael Mayer contributed 24.00 at tight end and CeeDee Lamb added 20.00.

The performance wasn't spectacular by Milwaukee's standards.

It didn't need to be.

Miami entered the matchup having scored 464.45 points over its previous two games. Against Milwaukee, the Inferno managed just 138.65.

That result matters.

Milwaukee also remains one of only two teams averaging more than 200 points per game through six weeks.

No franchise has ever sustained that pace for an entire season. Los Angeles set the previous single-season benchmark at 191.0 in 2025.

General manager Michael Bautch has reportedly been exploring tight end upgrades, including discussions involving Chicago's George Kittle.

One proposed deal involving Kittle and Christian Watson was rejected by Chicago GM Darrien Clay before Week 6.

For now, Milwaukee appears perfectly capable of winning with its current roster.

But a team sitting at 5-1 and still looking to improve should make the rest of the league uncomfortable.

Vance's Verdict: Milwaukee has the best record in the league, the highest Power Index and five consecutive victories. The Mustangs don't need to make a case for No. 1. They've already made it.

2. Tucson Sugar Skulls (4-2) ▼ 1

Power Index: 4770.40 | Week 6: L vs. Vegas, 157.60-216.20

Mason.

We need to talk.

A week ago, Tucson was sitting atop these rankings after scoring 254.70 points against defending champion Boston.

The Sugar Skulls were 4-1, leading the league in offensive production and looking every bit like a championship favorite.

Then Mason Bautch completed another blockbuster trade.

Then Tucson played the team it traded with.

Then Tucson lost by 58.60 points.

You couldn't have written a more uncomfortable opening chapter for this particular roster experiment.

Tucson sent Jaxon Smith-Njigba, Trey McBride and Bhayshul Tuten to Vegas for Derrick Henry, Ja'Marr Chase and Juwan Johnson.

Smith-Njigba immediately scored 33.40 points for the Knight Hawks. McBride added 17.80.

Meanwhile, Henry managed just 8.00 points in his Tucson debut, and Chase was unavailable because of his bye week.

The Sugar Skulls also received zero points from Brian Thomas Jr. and only 6.70 from Ty Johnson.

Isaiah Likely's 28.90-point performance at tight end was encouraging, particularly given Tucson's recent movement at the position.

But it wasn't nearly enough.

Here's the part some people won't like.

Tucson is still No. 2.

Why?

Because the Power Index isn't a weekly popularity contest.

Tucson has scored at an extraordinary pace through six games, remains 4-2 and owns a Power Index of 4770.40.

That puts the Sugar Skulls comfortably ahead of third-place Cedar Rapids.

Mason's aggressive trades may eventually prove brilliant. They may also create chemistry and roster-balance problems that weren't present before.

We don't know yet.

What we do know is that Tucson's next few games will be watched very closely.

Vance's Verdict: Tucson still has championship-level talent. But when you trade away three players and they help beat you by nearly 60 points days later, the questions are justified. The Sugar Skulls remain No. 2 on the strength of their full-season resume. Their general manager, however, has given the newsroom plenty to discuss.

3. Cedar Rapids Titans (5-1) ▲ 1

Power Index: 4417.72 | Week 6: W vs. Chicago, 168.20-144.20

The Titans are back on track.

After watching their undefeated season disappear in Week 5, Cedar Rapids responded with a steady 168.20-144.20 victory over Chicago.

It wasn't the most impressive performance of the week.

But it was exactly what the Titans needed.

Brock Purdy scored 35.00 points, DeVonta Smith contributed 23.50 and Tommy Tremble added 22.60.

RJ Harvey supplied 19.60, while Carson Schwesinger delivered 17.50 on defense.

The result improved Cedar Rapids to 5-1, matching Milwaukee for the league's best record.

GM Mitch Smith has built a roster capable of producing strong performances across multiple positions, and that depth remains a significant advantage.

There was also an interesting pregame subplot.

Chicago GM Darrien Clay reportedly rejected a trade proposal involving George Kittle partly because he expected Kittle to benefit from Purdy's performance.

Purdy scored 35.00 points for Cedar Rapids. Kittle and Mike Evans combined for 35.30 for Chicago.

Cedar Rapids still won by 24.

Sometimes a correct prediction about individual production doesn't translate into a correct prediction about the final score.

The Titans climb one position in the rankings, moving ahead of Miami.

They're still trailing Milwaukee and Tucson in Power Index, but their record and consistency keep them firmly in the championship conversation.

Vance's Verdict: Cedar Rapids handled its first setback exactly the way a contender should. No panic. No collapse. Just another win. At 5-1, the Titans are making a strong argument that they belong in the league's top tier.

4. Miami Inferno (4-2) ▼ 1

Power Index: 4251.52 | Week 6: L vs. Milwaukee, 138.65-169.40

Well, that was a reality check.

Miami entered Week 6 looking like an offensive juggernaut.

The Inferno had scored 220.80 points against Tucson and 243.65 against Northern Arizona in consecutive weeks.

That's 464.45 points in two games.

Then came Milwaukee.

And suddenly the Inferno couldn't find enough production to remain competitive.

Drake Maye continued his outstanding season with 45.15 points, but the rest of Miami's lineup struggled.

Jaylen Warren managed 14.80, Parker Washington scored 13.40 and Mark Andrews contributed just 2.40.

The defensive unit was particularly disappointing, producing a combined 19.00 points.

The result was Miami's lowest-scoring performance of the season's last three weeks and a 30.75-point defeat.

Richard Head's team falls to 4-2 and drops one spot in the rankings.

But let's not overreact.

Miami's previous two victories demonstrated what this offense can do when its supporting cast performs.

One disappointing Sunday doesn't erase that potential.

The bigger question is whether the Inferno can consistently produce enough around Maye to compete with Milwaukee, Tucson and Cedar Rapids.

There have also been reports of Miami's interest in Cleveland receiver Puka Nacua, with a potential package involving Deebo Samuel Sr. and Tyler Warren discussed.

No completed deal has been announced.

But it's clear that Miami's front office understands the value of adding another elite offensive weapon.

Vance's Verdict: Miami remains dangerous, but the Milwaukee game exposed the difference between having a spectacular quarterback and having a complete lineup. The Inferno have the talent to climb again. They just need more consistent help for Maye.

5. Boston Brawlers (4-2) —

Power Index: 4222.92 | Week 6: W vs. Brooklyn, 191.80-136.60

That's more like it.

The defending GLFL champions needed a response after their embarrassing loss to Tucson.

Brooklyn provided the opportunity.

Boston took full advantage.

Trevor Lawrence erupted for 54.40 points, Jonathan Taylor added 29.10 and Colston Loveland contributed 20.50.

Jamien Sherwood delivered another 20.75 on defense, helping the Brawlers cruise to a 191.80-136.60 victory.

The win improves Boston to 4-2 and extends its winning streak against Brooklyn to seven consecutive meetings.

For GM Chris Cholka, the performance was an important reminder that the Brawlers remain capable of championship-level production.

Boston's 191.80-point total was one of the stronger performances of Week 6, particularly after the team had been overwhelmed by Tucson the previous Sunday.

The challenge now is consistency.

The Brawlers have demonstrated the ability to produce excellent individual performances, but they haven't yet established the week-to-week dominance that Milwaukee and Tucson have shown.

Still, at 4-2 and with a Power Index of 4222.92, Boston remains firmly among the league's leading contenders.

And after winning the championship in 2025, this is a team that understands how to navigate a long season.

Vance's Verdict: Boston did what it needed to do against a winless opponent. The champions stay at No. 5, but the gap between Boston and Miami is just 28.60 Power Index points. That's close enough to make the next week very interesting.

6. Northern Arizona Wranglers (3-3) —

Power Index: 3998.93 | Week 6: L vs. Cleveland, 192.25-192.55

There are losses you forget.

And then there are losses that haunt you.

Northern Arizona's Week 6 defeat belongs in the second category.

The Wranglers scored 192.25 points against Cleveland.

Cleveland scored 192.55.

The difference was 0.30 points, making it the ninth-closest game in GLFL history, including playoffs.

That's the kind of margin that makes a general manager replay every lineup decision in his head.

Tyler Shough delivered 44.40 points, Christian McCaffrey added 36.70 and Hunter Henry contributed 20.30.

Dameon Pierce also scored 20.00.

It was an excellent offensive performance.

It simply wasn't quite enough.

The loss also carried historical significance: Northern Arizona reached 100 regular-season defeats in franchise history.

Jason Richmond's team has now lost two consecutive games and sits at 3-3.

Yet the Wranglers remain sixth in the Power Rankings.

Why?

Because scoring 192.25 points is still an impressive performance, even when the opponent scores 192.55.

The Power Index recognizes production beyond the final win-loss result.

Northern Arizona's 3998.93 score remains well ahead of seventh-place Philadelphia.

The Wranglers aren't winning enough right now, but they're still producing at a level that suggests better results are possible.

Vance's Verdict: Northern Arizona deserves criticism for its recent results, but not for its Week 6 offensive performance. Losing by 0.30 after scoring more than 192 points is brutal. The Wranglers remain a dangerous team despite their .500 record.

7. Philadelphia Soul (3-3) —

Power Index: 2812.88 | Week 6: L vs. Los Angeles, 135.20-210.90

Eleven.

That's how many consecutive games Philadelphia has now lost to Los Angeles.

Eleven.

The Soul entered Week 6 with a better record than the Wildcats, a chance to end a historic losing streak and an opportunity to strengthen their position in the playoff race.

Instead, they lost by 75.70 points.

Los Angeles scored 210.90. Philadelphia managed just 135.20.

Caleb Williams led the Soul with 34.55 points, but the rest of the offense struggled to keep pace.

Bijan Robinson scored just 8.30, Pat Freiermuth managed 4.10 and Garrett Wilson contributed 11.40.

Drake London added 14.20, while Chris Olave scored 17.30.

Those numbers weren't nearly enough.

Philadelphia drops to 3-3 and remains seventh in the Power Rankings.

But there's an important distinction between seventh place and sixth.

Northern Arizona's Power Index is 3998.93.

Philadelphia's is 2812.88.

That's a gap of more than 1,100 points.

The Soul are occupying the final playoff position in the standings conversation, but their underlying production is nowhere near the league's top six.

GM Brian Simmons needs to find answers quickly.

And maybe avoid scheduling Los Angeles for a while.

Vance's Verdict: Philadelphia is still 3-3, but the Power Index is sounding an alarm. The Soul have a winning-season opportunity in front of them, yet their scoring profile suggests they're much closer to the chasing pack than the championship contenders.

8. Cleveland Gladiators (3-3) —

Power Index: 2796.50 | Week 6: W vs. Northern Arizona, 192.55-192.25

Cleveland has won two consecutive games.

One delivered the franchise's 100th regular-season victory.

The other was decided by three-tenths of a point.

And somehow, the Gladiators are still ranked eighth.

Welcome to the Power Index.

Josh Thurmer's team has certainly become one of the league's most entertaining clubs.

After Puka Nacua's dramatic 47.70-point Monday-night performance against Cedar Rapids in Week 5, Cleveland followed with a 192.55-192.25 victory over Northern Arizona.

Bryce Young scored 37.65 points, Matthew Golden added 28.90 and Kyle Pitts Sr. contributed 27.00.

Jalen Coker delivered 19.70, while Ashton Jeanty scored 19.80.

The victory brought Cleveland back to 3-3 after a 1-3 start.

That's legitimate progress.

But the Gladiators' Power Index of 2796.50 still trails Philadelphia's 2812.88 by 16.38 points.

That's a remarkably narrow gap between seventh and eighth.

Cleveland has the recent momentum.

Philadelphia has the slight statistical edge.

If both teams continue on their current trajectories, that order may not last much longer.

There is also the ongoing question of Cleveland's trade strategy.

Nacua has reportedly drawn interest from Miami, and Thurmer has previously demonstrated a willingness to explore major roster moves.

Whether Cleveland continues building around its current core or reshapes the offense remains to be seen.

Vance's Verdict: Cleveland is the team I would least want to face among those ranked outside the top seven. The Gladiators have momentum, improving production and a habit of finding dramatic ways to win. Eighth place may not last long.

9. Vegas Knight Hawks (2-4) —

Power Index: 2544.37 | Week 6: W vs. Tucson, 216.20-157.60

Dave Nguyen, take a bow.

Vegas entered Week 6 at 1-4.

The Knight Hawks had just completed a blockbuster trade with Tucson.

And their next opponent was Tucson.

So naturally, Vegas scored 216.20 points and demolished the league's No. 1 team.

Sometimes fantasy football has a sense of humor.

Jaxon Smith-Njigba, acquired from the Sugar Skulls, scored 33.40 points.

Trey McBride, also acquired from Tucson, contributed 17.80.

Kirk Cousins delivered 40.65, D'Andre Swift added 28.00 and Chuba Hubbard scored 27.10.

The Knight Hawks won by 58.60 points.

Against the top-ranked team.

With players that team had just traded away.

You can practically hear Cole Maddox typing.

Vegas has now won two consecutive games after starting 0-4.

The Knight Hawks remain ninth in the Power Rankings because their season-long Power Index still trails Cleveland and Philadelphia.

But the gap is shrinking.

At 2544.37, Vegas sits 252.13 points behind Cleveland.

That's not an insurmountable difference, especially if the Knight Hawks continue producing at their Week 6 level.

Nguyen has changed the trajectory of his season.

The challenge now is proving the Tucson performance wasn't a one-week explosion.

Vance's Verdict: Vegas delivered the statement win of Week 6. The Knight Hawks are still 2-4, but they suddenly have a roster capable of producing serious numbers. If Smith-Njigba and McBride continue contributing, the rest of the league may need to reconsider what Vegas can become.

10. Los Angeles Wildcats (2-4) —

Power Index: 2474.26 | Week 6: W vs. Philadelphia, 210.90-135.20

Eleven straight victories over Philadelphia.

At this point, does Los Angeles even need a game plan for this matchup?

The Wildcats entered Week 6 at 1-4 and badly needed a victory.

Philadelphia entered at 3-2, looking to end a decade-long stretch of head-to-head frustration.

Instead, Los Angeles scored 210.90 points and won by 75.70.

Josh Allen led the way with 38.85 points, while James Cook III contributed 33.30.

Travis Hunter added 28.40 on defense, Carnell Tate scored 23.00 and Ryan Flournoy delivered 20.00.

Braelon Allen added another 20.40.

Cook and Tate, both acquired from Tucson, combined for 56.30 points.

That's an encouraging sign for GM Elizabeth Bautch, whose aggressive trade with the Sugar Skulls is beginning to produce tangible results.

Los Angeles improves to 2-4 and remains tenth in the rankings.

But the Wildcats' Power Index has climbed to 2474.26, just 70.11 points behind Vegas.

The Superior Division suddenly looks much more competitive than it did two weeks ago.

Tucson remains the clear statistical leader, but Vegas and Los Angeles have both delivered 200-point performances in Week 6.

If those offenses continue developing, the division race could become far more interesting.

Vance's Verdict: Los Angeles finally showed the offensive potential its roster has suggested all season. The Wildcats still have work to do, but a 210-point performance and an eleventh straight win over Philadelphia provide plenty of reasons for optimism.

11. Chicago Slaughter (1-5) —

Power Index: 2215.00 | Week 6: L vs. Cedar Rapids, 144.20-168.20

One hundred.

That's the number Chicago GM Darrien Clay probably wishes everyone would stop mentioning.

The Slaughter suffered their 100th regular-season defeat in franchise history with a 168.20-144.20 loss to Cedar Rapids.

Because Clay has been Chicago's only general manager since 2012, the milestone belongs to him as well.

Fourteen seasons.

One general manager.

One hundred regular-season losses.

The defeat was especially disappointing because Chicago had finally earned its first victory in Week 5 with a 202.65-point performance against Brooklyn.

Against Cedar Rapids, that offensive momentum disappeared.

Dak Prescott scored 29.60 points, George Kittle added 19.90 and George Pickens contributed 19.80.

But Cam Skattebo was held scoreless, Rhamondre Stevenson managed just 6.60 and Christian Watson added 12.40.

Chicago finished with 144.20 points.

The most interesting subplot came before kickoff, when Clay reportedly rejected a Milwaukee trade proposal involving Kittle and Watson.

Clay expected Kittle to benefit from a productive afternoon by opposing quarterback Brock Purdy.

Purdy scored 35.00 for Cedar Rapids.

Kittle and Mike Evans combined for 35.30 for Chicago.

The production relationship was nearly exact.

The final score wasn't particularly close.

Cedar Rapids won by 24.

Chicago remains eleventh in the Power Rankings, ahead of only winless Brooklyn.

Clay's team has enough individual talent to be competitive, but consistency remains a serious problem.

Vance's Verdict: Chicago's 100th loss is a historical milestone, not a complete evaluation of Clay's tenure. But the Slaughter's 1-5 start is becoming increasingly difficult to explain away. The talent is there. The results aren't.

12. Brooklyn Bolts (0-6) —

Power Index: 1760.40 | Week 6: L vs. Boston, 136.60-191.80

Six games.

Zero wins.

And the schedule isn't getting any easier.

Brooklyn remains the GLFL's only winless team after a 191.80-136.60 defeat to defending champion Boston.

The Bolts have now lost six consecutive games to begin the season, extending the worst start in franchise history.

Malik Nabers delivered another excellent performance with 37.00 points.

Tyler Huntley added 26.05 and Tetairoa McMillan contributed 17.60.

But Darren Waller was held scoreless, Kenny Gainwell managed just 5.40 and the defensive unit struggled to keep pace.

The result was another lopsided defeat.

For GM Cory Colvin, the question is becoming less about one particular lineup decision and more about the direction of the franchise.

Colvin previously acknowledged that he was already thinking about next season if Brooklyn's struggles continued.

Now the Bolts are 0-6.

And trade speculation is growing.

Tucson has reportedly expressed interest in Nabers, with Mason Bautch potentially willing to offer a four-for-one package.

No deal has been announced, and there's no indication Brooklyn has committed to a formal rebuild.

But Colvin faces difficult decisions.

Brooklyn's Power Index of 1760.40 remains comfortably last in the league.

The Bolts trail eleventh-place Chicago by 454.60 points.

That's a substantial gap.

And until Brooklyn begins winning games, there's little reason to expect its position to change.

Vance's Verdict: Brooklyn has talented players, but not enough consistent production across the roster. At 0-6, the Bolts are running out of time to turn the season around. The next few weeks may determine whether Colvin continues pursuing victories or begins prioritizing the future.

The Final Word: A New Leader, Familiar Questions

Milwaukee is back at No. 1.

Tucson remains No. 2 despite one of the most embarrassing defeats of the season.

Cedar Rapids is climbing.

Miami is searching for consistency.

Boston is responding.

And Northern Arizona is discovering that even 192 points might not be enough to win.

Below the top six, the picture is considerably less settled.

Philadelphia and Cleveland are separated by just 16.38 Power Index points.

Vegas and Los Angeles both delivered 200-point performances in Week 6 and are beginning to close the gap.

Chicago continues to struggle.

Brooklyn remains winless.

And somewhere in Tucson, Mason Bautch is probably looking at another trade proposal.

The most important thing to remember is that the Power Index rewards the entire body of work.

One upset doesn't erase five weeks of excellence.

One victory doesn't erase five weeks of struggles.

But every week changes the numbers.

And after six weeks, the league is beginning to separate into three distinct groups: championship contenders, playoff hopefuls and teams fighting to keep their seasons alive.

Milwaukee has earned the top spot.

Tucson has every reason to want it back.

And if Week 6 taught us anything, it's that nobody should get too comfortable.

Not even No. 1.
