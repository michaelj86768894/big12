GLFL POWER RANKINGS: One Week, Twelve Teams, Zero Reason to Stay Calm

By Marcus Vance | GLFL National • September 16, 2026

Boston begins the season where it finished the last one, while the GLFL Power Index separates performance from the standings.

One week.

That's all we've got.

One glorious, statistically irresponsible week of football.

That's not nearly enough information to know who will be playing in the Lakefront Bowl in December. It is, however, more than enough information to start arguing about it.

Welcome to the first GLFL Power Rankings of 2026.

Before anybody starts firing off angry messages to the League Office, understand what these rankings are — and what they aren't.

These are not the standings.

Winning matters. Losing matters. But fantasy football has never been kind enough to make record a perfect measurement of performance. You can score 190 points and lose. You can score 180 and win. Sometimes you're dominant. Sometimes you're lucky. Sometimes you leave Kenneth Walker III and 44.1 points on your bench.

Sorry, Milwaukee. We're going to be talking about that for a while.

HOW THE GLFL POWER INDEX WORKS

Power Score = PF × (2 + Win% + Median Win%)

Points scored form the foundation of the rankings, while actual winning percentage and performance against the weekly league median reward teams that consistently turn those points into successful weeks.

The question isn't simply, "Who has the best record?"

It's who has actually played the best football?

1. BOSTON BRAWLERS (1–0)

Week 1: W vs. Miami, 224.15–183.35

The champs stay the champs until somebody gives us a reason to think otherwise.

Boston didn't.

The Brawlers opened their Lakefront Bowl title defense by scoring 224.15 points, the highest total in the GLFL, behind 50.55 from Trevor Lawrence, 32.1 from Jonathan Taylor and another 31 from Zay Flowers.

It was also Boston's fifth consecutive victory over Miami. Chris Cholka is now 7–2 against the Inferno since taking over the Brawlers and 5–0 against current Miami GM Richard Head.

One week into the title defense, Boston remains exactly where everybody left them.

At the top.

2. TUCSON SUGAR SKULLS (1–0)

Week 1: W vs. Los Angeles, 222.45–180.55

Boston scored 224.15.

Tucson scored 222.45.

That's your difference between No. 1 and No. 2.

The Sugar Skulls got 48.6 from Jahmyr Gibbs, 35.5 from Patrick Mahomes and 33.8 from Isaiah Likely while extending their winning streak over Los Angeles to three games.

The funniest part?

GM Mason Bautch apparently watched his team score the second-most points in the league and decided he still needs Justin Jefferson, Bijan Robinson or Trey McBride.

Apparently 222 isn't enough.

3. CLEVELAND GLADIATORS (1–0)

Week 1: W vs. Chicago, 219.25–147.3

There are statement wins, and then there's whatever Cleveland just did to Chicago.

The Gladiators entered Week 1 having lost four consecutive meetings with the Slaughter dating back to 2023.

They left with a 71.95-point victory.

Bryce Young exploded for 58.05. Ashton Jeanty added 41.7. Jalen Coker scored 39.8.

We've spent plenty of time talking about Cleveland's young talent and what this roster could eventually become.

Scoring 219.25 is a good way to change the conversation from potential to production.

4. PHILADELPHIA SOUL (1–0)

Week 1: W vs. Brooklyn, 207.95–159.6

Oh look.

Philadelphia's talented again.

We've seen this movie before. Brian Simmons has assembled plenty of rosters capable of winning football games. The question has always been what happens when those expectations reach the postseason.

We're approximately three months away from answering that question.

For now?

Caleb Williams scored 60.95, Bijan Robinson added 40.3 and Chris Olave produced 35.2 as Philadelphia cruised past Brooklyn.

That's more than enough to earn the Soul a spot in the opening top four.

Derek Thompson is presumably preparing the parade route.

5. CEDAR RAPIDS TITANS (1–0)

Week 1: W vs. Milwaukee, 207–189

Four teams broke 200 points in Week 1.

Cedar Rapids was one of them.

Brock Purdy led the way with 44.15 while Bucky Irving, Nico Collins and Ladd McConkey all cleared 24 points.

And unlike the team directly below them in these rankings, the Titans actually put enough of those points into the starting lineup.

Mitch Smith leaves Week 1 at 1–0 with 207 points on the board.

There are worse ways to start a season.

6. MILWAUKEE MUSTANGS (0–1)

Week 1: L vs. Cedar Rapids, 207–189

Everybody remain calm.

Yes.

An 0–1 team is ranked ahead of a 1–0 team.

No, that isn't a typo.

Milwaukee scored 189 points, the sixth-highest total in the GLFL. That put the Mustangs in the top half of the league and earned them a win against the weekly median.

Unfortunately, fantasy football doesn't award victories against imaginary opponents.

Cedar Rapids scored 207.

Even worse, Kenneth Walker III scored 44.1 points on Milwaukee's bench. GM Michael Bautch rarely gives the league an obvious lineup decision to second-guess.

This one is going to follow him around for a while.

The Mustangs are 0–1 in the standings.

The Power Index doesn't think they're the sixth-worst team in the league.

Neither do I.

7. NORTHERN ARIZONA WRANGLERS (1–0)

Week 1: W vs. Vegas, 187.1–177.05

And here's the other side of Milwaukee.

Northern Arizona is 1–0.

Milwaukee is 0–1.

Milwaukee ranks higher.

Welcome to power rankings.

The Wranglers scored 187.1, seventh-best in the league and just below the weekly median. They happened to draw Vegas at 177.05 and walked away with the victory.

That's not criticism. Jason Richmond doesn't need to apologize for winning the game on his schedule.

But we're measuring performance here, not merely record.

The good news is considerably more important: Northern Arizona has now beaten Vegas three straight times, owns an 11–9 advantage in the all-time series and remains undefeated against Dave Nguyen.

Nguyen is now 0–5 all-time against Northern Arizona across two separate stints running Vegas.

The Wranglers will happily take the win.

8. MIAMI INFERNO (0–1)

Week 1: L vs. Boston, 224.15–183.35

Miami's reward for scoring a respectable 183.35 points?

Playing the highest-scoring team in the league.

Welcome to fantasy football.

Jacoby Brissett scored 39.25, Parker Washington added 23.3 and DeVaughn Vele contributed 22.9.

Against plenty of opponents, Miami is competitive.

Against Boston lately?

Not so much.

Richard Head falls to 0–5 against Chris Cholka, and the Inferno begin 2026 chasing the Brawlers once again.

9. LOS ANGELES WILDCATS (0–1)

Week 1: L vs. Tucson, 222.45–180.55

Imagine getting 58 points from Josh Allen and losing by nearly 42.

Los Angeles doesn't have to imagine it.

Amon-Ra St. Brown added 32.7, but Tucson's lineup simply had too much firepower.

T.J. Watt's 26.5 points sitting on the bench didn't help, although even that wasn't enough to flip the result.

The Wildcats have now lost three straight to Tucson and trail the Sugar Skulls 10–7 in the all-time series.

It's one loss.

It just happened to come against a division rival that doesn't need additional reasons to feel confident.

10. VEGAS KNIGHT HAWKS (0–1)

Week 1: L vs. Northern Arizona, 187.1–177.05

This one was winnable.

That's what should bother Vegas.

Derrick Henry scored 43.3. D'Andre Swift added 38.4.

But Ja'Marr Chase and Kayvshon Boutte combined for only 8.3, and the Knight Hawks fell by just 10.05 points in the closest game of Week 1.

The larger issue is becoming impossible to ignore.

Dave Nguyen has faced Northern Arizona five times as GM of Vegas.

He's lost all five.

At some point, it's not a weird statistic anymore.

It's a thing.

11. BROOKLYN BOLTS (0–1)

Week 1: L vs. Philadelphia, 207.95–159.6

Joe Burrow gave Brooklyn 35.7.

David Montgomery gave them 33.9.

Then things got thin.

Philadelphia cleared 200, Brooklyn couldn't crack 160 and Cory Colvin's Bolts begin the season near the bottom of the first Power Rankings.

There are 13 weeks to change that.

Week 1 didn't provide much reason to put them higher.

12. CHICAGO SLAUGHTER (0–1)

Week 1: L vs. Cleveland, 219.25–147.3

Somebody has to be 12th.

Scoring the fewest points in the league makes the decision considerably easier.

Chicago's 147.3 was the lowest total of Week 1, and Cleveland beat the Slaughter by a league-high 71.95 points.

Christian Watson's 37.7 points were also sitting on the bench.

Would Watson have changed the outcome?

No.

Would starting him have made the box score less embarrassing?

Absolutely.

Chicago is already the first team eliminated from GLFL Survivor.

Now they're No. 12 here.

Marcus Reed is going to have a wonderful week.

VANCE'S BOTTOM LINE

Before anyone screenshots these rankings in November:

It's Week 1.

We know almost nothing.

That's part of the fun.

Boston, Tucson and Cleveland looked terrifying.

Philadelphia and Cedar Rapids broke 200.

Milwaukee showed why record and performance aren't always the same thing.

Northern Arizona showed why winning the game in front of you still matters more than winning an argument about metrics.

And Chicago showed us that sometimes the standings, scoring totals, Power Index and eye test all agree.

We'll run this again after Week 2.

Some teams will rise.

Some will fall.

And somebody currently furious about being ranked eighth will probably be furious about being ranked ninth.

Welcome to the Power Rankings.
