GLFL WEEK 4 POWER RANKINGS: Milwaukee Takes the Throne, But the Only Perfect Team Sits Third

By Marcus Vance | Senior GLFL Analyst • October 8, 2026

Milwaukee has broken the league scoring record twice in three weeks. Cedar Rapids is the only undefeated team remaining. Tucson just pulled off a blockbuster trade with a division rival. And somehow, the most controversial number in this week's rankings might be a three.

Let's get something out of the way.

Cedar Rapids is 4-0.

Milwaukee is 3-1.

Milwaukee is ranked No. 1.

Cedar Rapids is ranked No. 3.

Everybody still with me?

Before the Titans' front office starts drafting an angry letter to the GLFL Network, allow me to explain.

Cedar Rapids has the best record in the league. Nobody disputes that. But these rankings measure more than wins. They measure scoring production, winning percentage and performance against the league's weekly median.

And while Cedar Rapids has been winning, Milwaukee has been doing things to scoreboards that probably violate several local ordinances.

The Mustangs have scored 743.45 points over their last three games, including two performances that established new regular-season scoring records.

Their latest? A ridiculous 278.60 points against Chicago. The highest single-team score in GLFL history, playoffs included.

So yes, Cedar Rapids is undefeated. Milwaukee is No. 1.

If these were the standings, the Titans would be first.

Fortunately, the GLFL already has standings.

These are the Power Rankings.

1. Milwaukee Mustangs (3-1) ▲ 2

Power Score: 3499.50 | Last Week: No. 3

What exactly are we supposed to do with this?

Milwaukee scored 259.70 in Week 2, breaking the GLFL regular-season record. Two weeks later, the Mustangs scored 278.60 against Chicago and broke it again.

The latest performance didn't just establish another regular-season record. It surpassed Los Angeles' 263-point playoff performance from 2025 to become the highest single-team score in league history.

CeeDee Lamb scored 52.3. Jared Goff added 45.6. Javonte Williams contributed 37.3, Kenneth Walker III scored 36.9, and T.J. Hockenson added 32.9.

Five players over 30 points.

Chicago scored 177.55 and lost by more than 100.

The Mustangs are averaging 247.82 points over their three-game winning streak. That's not sustainable forever, but nobody has figured out how to slow them down yet.

There is, however, an interesting development heading into Week 5.

Kenneth Walker III is on a bye. Saquon Barkley is doubtful. Jadarian Price has landed on injured reserve.

That leaves Javonte Williams and Samaje Perine as Milwaukee's available running back options under the current circumstances.

The team that just produced the greatest scoring performance in GLFL history suddenly has a legitimate depth concern at one of its most important positions.

And somewhere in Milwaukee, Michael Bautch is probably still thinking about leaving Kenneth Walker on the bench in Week 1.

The Mustangs could be 4-0.

Instead, they'll have to settle for being the highest-scoring team in league history and No. 1 in the Power Rankings.

Life is difficult sometimes.

2. Tucson Sugar Skulls (3-1) ▼ 1

Power Score: 3332.25 | Last Week: No. 1

Tucson finally lost.

Miami handed the Sugar Skulls their first defeat of 2026, winning 220.80-196.20 in Week 4.

And apparently Mason Bautch took that personally.

Within days, Tucson completed one of the biggest trades of the young season, acquiring Amon-Ra St. Brown, Trey McBride and Bhayshul Tuten from division rival Los Angeles.

The price was substantial: Brock Bowers, James Cook III and Carnell Tate.

Make no mistake, Tucson surrendered serious talent. Bowers and Cook were major contributors to the Sugar Skulls' early success, while Tate offered considerable upside.

But Bautch had been pursuing St. Brown, and now he has him.

The interesting part isn't simply the size of the transaction.

It's who Tucson made it with.

Los Angeles plays in the same Superior Division. The Wildcats are currently 1-3, but Tucson just handed them three players capable of changing that trajectory.

If LA turns its season around, this trade could become considerably more complicated than it looks today.

For now, Tucson remains one of the GLFL's strongest teams, and the Power Index still places the Sugar Skulls comfortably second.

But after three weeks at No. 1, the throne belongs to Milwaukee.

3. Cedar Rapids Titans (4-0) ▼ 1

Power Score: 3183.20 | Last Week: No. 2

Congratulations, Cedar Rapids.

You're the only undefeated team in the GLFL.

Your reward is dropping one spot.

I understand how that sounds.

The Titans defeated Los Angeles 189.85-151.95 to improve to 4-0, matching their best start since 2015.

Brock Purdy scored 36.8, Nico Collins delivered 35.8, and Emanuel Wilson contributed 33.5.

Cedar Rapids continues to find ways to win.

But here's the difference between the Titans and the two teams ahead of them:

Milwaukee and Tucson have simply produced more points.

Cedar Rapids already receives the maximum benefit available from its undefeated winning percentage. It cannot improve upon 4-0.

Milwaukee and Tucson are overcoming their one-loss records through superior scoring production.

That's how the formula works.

And here's something worth remembering: Cedar Rapids doesn't need to be No. 1 in these rankings to win the GLFL championship.

The Titans just need to keep winning.

So far, they're the only team that's done exactly that.

4. Northern Arizona Wranglers (3-1) —

Power Score: 2972.81 | Last Week: No. 4

Jason Richmond wanted respect.

He's getting it.

Northern Arizona defeated Brooklyn 198.05-155.25, improving to 3-1 and maintaining its position inside the top four.

The Wranglers have quietly become one of the more consistent teams in the league.

And Richmond apparently isn't satisfied.

Northern Arizona completed a trade with Cleveland this week, sending Matthew Golden and Kyle Pitts to the Gladiators in exchange for Rome Odunze and Hunter Henry.

It's an interesting move for a team that's already winning.

Richmond gets Odunze while changing his tight end situation, and Cleveland gets an opportunity to reshape its own struggling roster.

The transaction doesn't affect this week's Power Score, but it could influence how the Wranglers perform moving forward.

Northern Arizona holds fourth place by just 7.68 points over Boston.

That's practically a statistical dead heat.

The difference between fourth and fifth might come down to a single player next week.

5. Boston Brawlers (3-1) ▲ 2

Power Score: 2965.13 | Last Week: No. 7

Apparently Boston just needed to play Cleveland.

Which is a strange sentence considering the Brawlers entered Week 4 with a 3-11 all-time record against the Gladiators.

But the defending champions finally reversed that trend, winning 222.85-185.65.

Kyren Williams erupted for 47.2 points. Zay Flowers added 29.8, Trevor Lawrence contributed 29.35, and Jonathan Taylor scored 28.7.

It was exactly the response Boston needed after Philadelphia handed the Brawlers their first loss in Week 3.

And just like that, Boston is back inside the top five.

Chris Cholka's team isn't dominating every matchup, but the defending champions are 3-1 and remain firmly in the early championship conversation.

There's also something worth remembering about Boston.

Last season, the Brawlers didn't need to be the league's highest-scoring team every week.

They needed to be the team that won when it mattered.

That's generally how championships work.

6. Miami Inferno (3-1) ▲ 3

Power Score: 2850.94 | Last Week: No. 9

We need to start taking Miami seriously.

The Inferno finished 3-11 last season.

Four weeks into 2026, they're already 3-1.

And their latest victory wasn't exactly against a struggling opponent.

Miami scored 220.80 points and handed previously undefeated Tucson its first loss of the season.

Drake Maye led the way with 52.85 points, while Quinshon Judkins, Jaylen Warren and Deebo Samuel Sr. helped produce a balanced offensive performance.

It was Miami's first victory over Tucson since 2019.

Richard Head has now guided the Inferno to three wins in their first four games, matching their entire win total from last season.

That's a turnaround worth recognizing.

Miami climbs three positions this week, the largest upward movement in the rankings.

The Inferno aren't simply benefiting from an easy schedule.

They're producing points.

And after beating Tucson, nobody can reasonably claim they haven't beaten anybody.

7. Philadelphia Soul (3-1) ▲ 1

Power Score: 2077.49 | Last Week: No. 8

Philadelphia is 3-1.

The Soul have beaten Boston.

They've won six consecutive games against Vegas.

And somehow, they're still sitting seventh in the Power Rankings.

I can already hear Derek Thompson preparing a strongly worded defense of this team.

Philadelphia defeated Vegas 195.80-167.30 in Week 4, with C.J. Stroud scoring 45.55, Bijan Robinson adding 33.7 and Sam LaPorta contributing 27.4.

The Soul have now won three of their first four games, and Brian Simmons has a team that looks capable of competing in the Michigan Division.

The problem is the competition ahead of them.

Six teams have produced stronger cumulative Power Scores.

Philadelphia's 2077.49 is also substantially behind Miami's 2850.94.

That doesn't mean the Soul aren't good.

It means the top six have separated themselves from the field.

Philadelphia is winning.

Now it needs to start closing that statistical gap.

8. Los Angeles Wildcats (1-3) ▼ 3

Power Score: 1634.96 | Last Week: No. 5

Los Angeles is 1-3.

The Wildcats just lost 189.85-151.95 to Cedar Rapids.

And then Liz Bautch decided to rearrange a significant portion of her offense.

Los Angeles traded Amon-Ra St. Brown, Trey McBride and Bhayshul Tuten to Tucson for James Cook III, Brock Bowers and Carnell Tate.

It's the kind of transaction that can define a season.

And because Tucson is a Superior Division rival, it's also the kind of transaction that can haunt a franchise.

But let's examine the other side.

Los Angeles acquired an established running back in Cook, replaced McBride with another premier tight end in Bowers, and added a young receiver in Tate.

According to the team's evaluation of current-season production, the three incoming players had collectively averaged more points per game than the three players being sent to Tucson.

That's not a guarantee of future results.

Four weeks is a small sample, and St. Brown's proven production remains a significant loss.

But there's a legitimate argument that Los Angeles improved its overall positional balance.

Tucson may have won the headline.

Los Angeles may have won the math.

We won't know for a while.

What we do know is that the Wildcats are 1-3, have dropped three spots in the Power Rankings, and desperately need this move to translate into victories.

The trade doesn't change their Week 4 ranking.

But it might change everything that comes after it.

9. Cleveland Gladiators (1-3) ▲ 1

Power Score: 1618.31 | Last Week: No. 10

Cleveland has 99 regular-season victories.

I feel like I've been writing that sentence since July.

The Gladiators lost to Boston 222.85-185.65, extending their pursuit of regular-season victory No. 100 into a fifth week.

Bryce Young scored 46.75. Puka Nacua contributed 33.7. Ashton Jeanty added 24.6.

Cleveland wasn't terrible.

Boston was simply better.

Josh Thurmer has responded by making a move, acquiring Matthew Golden and Kyle Pitts from Northern Arizona in exchange for Rome Odunze and Hunter Henry.

The Gladiators are clearly looking for answers.

Whether this is the right one remains to be seen.

Cleveland actually climbs one spot this week, passing Vegas in the Power Index.

That's progress.

Unfortunately, the standings still say 1-3.

And the franchise record book still says 99.

Somebody please win a game so we can retire this storyline.

10. Vegas Knight Hawks (0-4) ▼ 4

Power Score: 1470.90 | Last Week: No. 6

Last week, I ranked an 0-3 team sixth.

People had questions.

Understandable questions.

Vegas had just scored 239.20 points in a loss to Tucson, the 10th-highest regular-season score in GLFL history.

The underlying production justified the ranking.

Then Week 4 happened.

Philadelphia defeated Vegas 195.80-167.30, sending the Knight Hawks to 0-4.

Kirk Cousins scored 48.25 and Chuba Hubbard added 34.9.

But the receiving corps struggled badly.

Ja'Marr Chase, Mack Hollins and Kalif Raymond combined for just 19.6 points.

The result?

Vegas drops four spots to No. 10.

And before anybody asks, no, I didn't manually correct last week's ranking.

The formula simply responded to another week of results.

That's what it's supposed to do.

Vegas still has enough talent to be dangerous.

But at 0-4, dangerous isn't particularly useful unless somebody actually gets beaten.

11. Chicago Slaughter (0-4) —

Power Score: 1350.60 | Last Week: No. 11

I almost feel bad for Chicago.

Almost.

The Slaughter scored 177.55 points against Milwaukee.

That's a perfectly respectable performance.

Unfortunately, Milwaukee scored 278.60.

That's a 101.05-point loss.

Chicago has now lost four consecutive games to open the season, matching its 0-4 start from 2021.

And Darrien Clay is clearly looking for answers.

After earlier reports connected Chicago to potential star acquisitions, the Slaughter made an aggressive free-agent move this week, spending $5 to acquire Tyreek Hill.

The catch?

Hill hadn't yet officially signed with an NFL team at the time of Chicago's acquisition.

That's either an extremely clever investment or an unusually expensive way to buy uncertainty.

Perhaps both.

If Hill returns and produces, Clay may look like a genius.

If he doesn't, Chicago will have spent valuable free-agent dollars on a player who couldn't help an 0-4 team.

Either way, the Slaughter aren't standing still.

At this point, that's probably a good thing.

12. Brooklyn Bolts (0-4) —

Power Score: 1201.20 | Last Week: No. 12

Cory Colvin forgot to set his lineup.

That's the headline.

Brooklyn left an injured Rico Dowdle in its starting lineup for Thursday night's game.

He scored zero.

Now, before we spend the next three paragraphs criticizing Colvin, there's an important detail.

Brooklyn lost to Northern Arizona 198.05-155.25.

No available bench substitution would have erased the 42.80-point deficit.

So the lineup mistake didn't actually cost Brooklyn the game.

Which somehow makes the rest of the performance more concerning.

Joe Burrow scored 51.0.

Tetairoa McMillan scored 53.2.

Two players combined for 104.2 points.

Brooklyn's entire lineup finished with 155.25.

That's an extraordinary amount of production concentrated in two players with very little support elsewhere.

The Bolts are now 0-4, remain last in the Power Rankings, and have scored the fewest cumulative Power Index points of any team in the league.

At least next week's lineup should be easy to remember.

Set it.

The View From the Top

Four weeks into the season, the GLFL has a fascinating situation developing.

Cedar Rapids is undefeated.

Milwaukee is No. 1.

Tucson just made a blockbuster trade.

Miami has already matched last season's win total.

Boston is climbing again.

And Northern Arizona is beginning to look like a legitimate contender.

The league's top six teams have started separating themselves from the rest of the field.

But the standings and the Power Rankings aren't telling exactly the same story.

That's not a problem.

That's the point.

The standings tell us who's winning.

The Power Rankings tell us who's producing.

And right now, nobody is producing quite like Milwaukee.

Cedar Rapids has the perfect record.

Milwaukee has the throne.

We'll see who still has what after Week 5.
