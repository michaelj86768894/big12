GLFL WEEK 7 RECAP: Milwaukee Ends Tucson Curse, Vegas Keeps Rolling and Brooklyn's Nightmare Hits Seven

By Ethan Calloway | GLFL National • October 27, 2026

Milwaukee finally ended five consecutive losses to Tucson, beating the No. 2 Sugar Skulls by nearly 60 points in the week's marquee showdown. Vegas demolished Cedar Rapids behind another monster performance from its former Tucson stars. Chicago rallied Monday night to stun defending champion Boston. And Brooklyn entered Sunday night leading Cleveland, only to suffer another agonizing defeat and fall to 0-7. Seven weeks into the season, the GLFL has a clear leader and no shortage of chaos behind it.

The Great Lakes Fantasy League entered Week 7 with a question.

Could Milwaukee finally beat Tucson?

Seven days later, the answer is emphatic.

Yes.

The Mustangs didn't merely snap their five-game losing streak against the Sugar Skulls. They won by 59.20 points, extended their overall winning streak to six games and became the only 6-1 team in the league.

Meanwhile, the teams chasing Milwaukee encountered problems of their own.

Cedar Rapids was overwhelmed by a resurgent Vegas offense. Boston watched a Monday-night comeback turn a projected victory into defeat. And Tucson's once-dominant season is suddenly headed in the wrong direction.

But perhaps no team experienced a more painful week than Brooklyn.

The Bolts had a legitimate opportunity to earn their first victory, carried a four-point lead into Sunday night and watched it disappear in a matchup between a wide receiver and a defensive player.

The result?

Another loss.

Another week of frustration.

And an 0-7 record.

Here's how one of the season's most dramatic weeks unfolded.

MILWAUKEE 202.40, TUCSON 143.20

Father Finally Gets the Better of Son as Milwaukee Takes Control

Five consecutive losses.

Eight defeats in ten meetings since Mason Bautch took over Tucson.

A playoff elimination in 2024.

Milwaukee GM Michael Bautch had plenty of reasons to circle this matchup on the calendar.

And this time, he got the last laugh.

The No. 1 Mustangs demolished the No. 2 Sugar Skulls 202.40-143.20 in the most anticipated matchup of Week 7.

The 59.20-point victory ended Tucson's five-game winning streak in the series and gave Milwaukee its sixth consecutive win of the season.

More importantly, it established the Mustangs as the league's only 6-1 team.

For the first time in several meetings, the father-son rivalry belonged to Michael.

Milwaukee Delivers Across the Lineup

Jared Goff led the Mustangs with 39.05 points, while Javonte Williams added 29.50 and CeeDee Lamb contributed 29.40.

Alex Singleton delivered an outstanding 27.25 points on defense, while Christian Ellis added 15.75.

T.J. Hockenson scored 15.50 at tight end, and Michael Wilson contributed 14.60.

Milwaukee's offensive production wasn't built around a single extraordinary performance.

It came from contributions throughout the roster.

And that's becoming the defining characteristic of this team.

The Mustangs have now scored more than 200 points in another victory, continuing a season-long pursuit of offensive history.

They entered Week 7 as one of only two teams averaging more than 200 points per game.

Against the other member of that exclusive club, Milwaukee delivered another 200-point performance.

Tucson's Troubles Continue

For Tucson, the result was another frustrating chapter in what has become an unexpectedly difficult stretch.

The Sugar Skulls have now lost three of their last four games.

After opening the season as one of the league's most dominant teams, Mason Bautch's club has fallen to 4-3.

And the roster changes that dominated the headlines over the previous two weeks are becoming increasingly difficult to ignore.

Amon-Ra St. Brown delivered 32.20 points against Milwaukee, while Aaron Rodgers contributed 27.20 and Jahmyr Gibbs added 24.30.

Ja'Marr Chase, acquired from Vegas, scored 21.20 in his return from a bye week.

But Derrick Henry managed just 7.90 points, Germie Bernard scored 6.70 and Isaiah Likely contributed only 4.20.

Tucson's three defensive starters combined for just 19.50 points.

That's not enough against a Milwaukee offense capable of scoring 200 or more.

Henry has now scored 15.90 points across his first two games with Tucson.

Meanwhile, the players Tucson sent to Vegas continue to produce extraordinary results.

The timing could hardly be worse for Mason.

Tucson still leads the all-time series against Milwaukee 13-8, and Mason remains 8-3 against his father since taking over the franchise.

But the immediate question is no longer whether Tucson can beat Milwaukee.

It's whether Tucson can regain the consistency that made it the league's top-ranked team just two weeks ago.

Calloway's Take: This was a statement victory for Milwaukee and a warning sign for Tucson. The Mustangs ended their most frustrating rivalry streak while reinforcing their claim as the GLFL's best team. Tucson has too much talent to dismiss, but three losses in four games and two consecutive blowout defeats demand answers.

VEGAS 204.35, CEDAR RAPIDS 131.35

Vegas Has Found Something Special, and Tucson's Former Stars Are Leading the Way

Somebody might want to check on Mason Bautch.

Because the Vegas Knight Hawks are absolutely loving that trade.

One week after demolishing Tucson, Vegas followed with another statement victory, defeating previously 5-1 Cedar Rapids 204.35-131.35.

The Knight Hawks have now won three consecutive games after opening the season 0-4.

And their newest offensive weapons are at the center of the turnaround.

Smith-Njigba and McBride Deliver Again

Jaxon Smith-Njigba scored 39.30 points against Cedar Rapids.

Trey McBride added 31.10.

Combined?

70.40 points.

Seventy.

Point.

Four.

From two players Tucson traded away just days before facing Vegas in Week 6.

Smith-Njigba and McBride combined for 51.20 points in their first game with the Knight Hawks.

They followed that with 70.40 in Week 7.

That's 121.60 points from the duo in two games.

Meanwhile, Derrick Henry has managed just 15.90 points in two games for Tucson.

No one is declaring a long-term winner of the blockbuster trade after two weeks.

But the early returns are becoming impossible to ignore.

Vegas also received 40.35 points from Kirk Cousins, 29.00 from Blake Cashman and 24.00 from D'Andre Swift.

The result was another offensive explosion and a 73.00-point victory over a championship contender.

Cedar Rapids Hits a Wall

The Titans entered Week 7 tied with Milwaukee for the league's best record.

They left with their second defeat in three games.

Brock Purdy scored 33.90 points, and Bucky Irving contributed 34.00.

But the receiving corps was disastrous.

Jaylin Noel scored 1.70, Nico Collins managed 2.60 and Wan'Dale Robinson added just 4.70.

That's 9.00 combined points from three starting receivers.

Even strong performances from Purdy and Irving couldn't overcome that production gap.

Cedar Rapids falls to 5-2.

Vegas improves to 3-4.

And the Knight Hawks' historical dominance over the Titans continues.

Vegas now leads the all-time series 15-6, with Cedar Rapids having earned just one victory in the matchup since 2018.

This was also the 50th career GLFL game for both Cedar Rapids GM Mitch Smith and Vegas GM Dave Nguyen.

Nguyen celebrated with a victory.

Smith had considerably less to celebrate.

Calloway's Take: Vegas is the hottest team outside Milwaukee right now. Three straight wins, back-to-back 200-point performances and a receiving-tight end combination producing ridiculous numbers. Dave Nguyen has transformed the conversation around his team in less than a month. The Knight Hawks are suddenly a legitimate threat to the teams above them.

CHICAGO 182.30, BOSTON 176.95

Monday Night Magic: Dak and Pickens Stun the Defending Champions

Boston had every reason to feel comfortable.

The defending champions entered Week 7 at 4-2, coming off a convincing victory over Brooklyn.

Chicago was 1-5 and had just recorded the franchise's 100th regular-season loss.

The Brawlers appeared positioned to continue their winning ways.

Then Monday night happened.

Dak Prescott and George Pickens combined for 63.10 points, powering a dramatic Chicago comeback and a 182.30-176.95 upset.

Prescott delivered 44.40 points.

Pickens added 18.70.

And just like that, the Slaughter had their second victory of the season.

The 5.35-point victory was one of Week 7's most dramatic finishes.

Chicago's Offensive Balance Makes the Difference

Prescott and Pickens supplied the Monday-night heroics, but Chicago received important contributions throughout the lineup.

Rhamondre Stevenson scored 25.50 points, while Blake Corum contributed 24.10.

George Kittle added 19.80 and Christian Watson delivered 15.20.

The Slaughter finished with 182.30 points, comfortably exceeding their 173.8-point projection.

For GM Darrien Clay, it was a welcome reversal after the disappointment of Week 6.

Chicago improves to 2-5.

And the victory came against the defending GLFL champions.

Boston's Lineup Decision Looms Large

The Brawlers received 36.30 points from Deshaun Watson, 32.70 from Zay Flowers and 24.10 from Kyren Williams.

Colston Loveland added 23.90, while Jonathan Taylor contributed 23.00.

Those performances should have been enough to secure a victory.

But Boston received zero points from Adonai Mitchell.

And that lineup decision proved costly.

Marvin Harrison Jr. scored 8.60 points on Boston's bench.

Had GM Chris Cholka started Harrison instead of Mitchell, Boston would have finished with 185.55 points.

That would have been enough to defeat Chicago's 182.30.

Instead, the Brawlers fall to 4-3.

The defending champions have now lost two of their last three games.

And a single lineup decision is likely to linger in Cholka's mind throughout the week.

Calloway's Take: Chicago deserves credit for a terrific Monday-night comeback. Prescott was outstanding, Pickens delivered when needed, and the Slaughter earned a victory over the defending champions. But Boston will have a difficult time forgetting the 8.60 points left on its bench. In a 5.35-point game, that decision changed everything.

CLEVELAND 149.45, BROOKLYN 145.80

Brooklyn Was Four Points Away From Hope. Then Sunday Night Happened.

At some point, you have to wonder what Brooklyn did to upset the fantasy football universe.

Because this is getting ridiculous.

The Bolts entered Week 7 at 0-6, searching desperately for their first victory of the season.

They were projected to lose.

Their pregame win probability hovered around 30 percent.

But as Sunday's games unfolded, something unexpected happened.

Brooklyn stayed competitive.

Its chances climbed toward 50 percent by midafternoon.

And by the time Sunday night's game arrived, the Bolts held a 140.70-136.70 lead over Cleveland.

Four points.

Two players remaining.

Brooklyn had wide receiver Xavier Worthy.

Cleveland had defensive player Nick Bolton.

Surely a wide receiver could hold his own against a linebacker.

Right?

Wrong.

The Sunday-Night Collapse

Worthy managed just 5.10 points.

Bolton scored 12.75.

Cleveland erased Brooklyn's lead and escaped with a 149.45-145.80 victory.

The final margin was 3.65 points.

And Brooklyn fell to 0-7.

The Bolts had entered the game with a chance to break their winless streak against a Cleveland team they had beaten in five of the previous six meetings.

Instead, the Gladiators delivered another dramatic finish.

Cleveland has now won three consecutive games, improving from 1-3 to 4-3.

Josh Thurmer's team has made a habit of surviving tense finishes.

Week 5 brought a dramatic comeback against Cedar Rapids.

Week 6 brought a 0.30-point escape against Northern Arizona.

And Week 7 brought another narrow victory, this time by 3.65 points.

The Gladiators may not always dominate.

But lately, they keep finding ways to win.

Another Great Quarterback Performance Goes to Waste

Brooklyn received 52.80 points from Joe Burrow, the highest individual score in the matchup.

Malik Nabers added 22.10, Rico Dowdle contributed 19.60 and Bam Knight scored 11.30.

But Xavier Worthy's 5.10-point performance became the defining number of the evening.

Cleveland received 28.40 points from Kyler Murray, 24.80 from Puka Nacua and 23.60 from Ashton Jeanty.

Tucker Kraft added 16.80, while Fred Warner and Bolton combined for 27.00 defensive points.

The result was another narrow Cleveland victory and another heartbreaking Brooklyn defeat.

Cory Colvin's team remains the league's only winless franchise.

And after coming this close, 0-7 probably feels considerably worse than 0-6 did a week ago.

Calloway's Take: Brooklyn had a legitimate chance to win. The Bolts led entering Sunday night and had a wide receiver remaining against Cleveland's defensive player. But Bolton outscored Worthy by 7.65 points, turning a four-point Brooklyn lead into another loss. You couldn't invent a more frustrating way to remain winless.

NORTHERN ARIZONA 225.25, LOS ANGELES 171.75

Wranglers Dominate as Liz Bautch's Historic Victory Must Wait

Northern Arizona had two reasons to be motivated entering Week 7.

The Wranglers were coming off a devastating 0.30-point defeat to Cleveland.

And they were facing a Los Angeles franchise that had historically dominated the matchup.

Jason Richmond's team responded with its strongest performance of the season.

Northern Arizona demolished Los Angeles 225.25-171.75, improving to 4-3 and securing its second victory over the Wildcats this season.

The 53.50-point margin left little doubt about the outcome.

Wranglers Deliver Across the Board

Jordan Love scored 34.70 points, while Tee Higgins contributed 32.80 and KC Concepcion added 30.20.

Christian McCaffrey delivered 27.00, Ollie Gordon II scored 24.30 and Hunter Henry added 19.10.

The defensive unit also contributed 43.25 points, led by Nakobe Dean's 17.75 and Jack Campbell's 14.25.

It was the kind of complete performance Northern Arizona had been searching for.

The Wranglers entered the season having lost nine of their previous ten meetings against Los Angeles.

But after winning in Week 3, they have now defeated the Wildcats twice in 2026.

The historical series still favors Los Angeles, 12-7.

The current season belongs to Northern Arizona.

Liz's 100th Victory Remains on Hold

For Los Angeles GM Elizabeth Bautch, the defeat carried an additional disappointment.

Liz entered Week 7 seeking her 100th career GLFL victory.

A win would have made her just the second general manager in league history to reach the milestone, joining Milwaukee's Michael Bautch.

Instead, the Wildcats fell to 2-5.

Matthew Stafford scored 37.45 points, while Josh Jacobs contributed 26.10 and Braelon Allen added 25.40.

Brock Bowers scored 16.30, but the Wildcats couldn't match Northern Arizona's offensive depth.

Liz's pursuit of career victory No. 100 will continue into Week 8.

Meanwhile, Northern Arizona has moved back above .500 and reestablished itself as a serious playoff contender.

Calloway's Take: Northern Arizona's 225.25-point performance was the highest score of Week 7. The Wranglers responded to a heartbreaking loss with a complete offensive performance and swept their season series against a historically difficult opponent. Liz Bautch's milestone will have to wait, but Jason Richmond's team deserves the headlines this week.

MIAMI 199.20, PHILADELPHIA 177.10

Inferno Rebounds and Completes the Season Sweep

Miami needed a response.

After scoring just 138.65 points against Milwaukee in Week 6, the Inferno entered a divisional rematch against Philadelphia looking to rediscover its offensive rhythm.

Richard Head's team delivered.

Miami defeated Philadelphia 199.20-177.10, improving to 5-2 and completing a two-game season sweep of the Soul.

The victory was especially important in the Michigan Division standings.

Miami now sits at 5-2, ahead of Boston at 4-3 and Philadelphia at 3-4.

Maye Continues His Outstanding Season

Drake Maye once again led the Inferno with 50.65 points.

Quinshon Judkins added 30.40, Luther Burden III contributed 27.70 and Jaylen Warren scored 24.70.

DeVaughn Vele delivered another 20.40, while Patrick Queen added 18.25 on defense.

The result was a much more balanced performance than Miami produced against Milwaukee.

Maye remains one of the league's most dangerous quarterbacks, and the supporting cast delivered enough production to push Miami close to 200 points.

Philadelphia received an extraordinary 43.50 points from Tony Pollard, while C.J. Stroud contributed 31.05.

Bijan Robinson added 24.20 and Drake London scored 23.60.

But Garrett Wilson managed just 5.60, Sam LaPorta scored 3.80 and Nik Bonitto contributed only 1.50 on defense.

Those shortfalls proved costly.

Philadelphia has now lost two consecutive games and falls below .500 at 3-4.

Miami, meanwhile, has won five of its first seven games after finishing 3-11 in 2025.

That's a remarkable turnaround for a franchise that struggled throughout last season.

The Inferno are firmly back in the championship conversation.

And Richard Head celebrated his 50th career GLFL game with an important divisional victory.

Calloway's Take: Miami's Week 6 disappointment looks increasingly like a temporary setback. The Inferno have now scored at least 199 points in three of their last four games, and Drake Maye continues to produce at an elite level. Philadelphia has enough talent to compete, but inconsistent production is becoming a serious concern.

The GLFL Standings After Week 7

Milwaukee now stands alone atop the league at 6-1.

Cedar Rapids and Miami follow at 5-2, while Tucson, Boston, Northern Arizona and Cleveland are all 4-3.

The playoff race remains tightly contested, particularly around the seventh and eighth positions.

Looking Ahead: Milwaukee Alone at the Top

Seven weeks into the 2026 GLFL season, the championship picture has changed dramatically.

Milwaukee is 6-1.

The Mustangs have won six consecutive games, ended their losing streak against Tucson and continue producing at a historic offensive pace.

Cedar Rapids has lost two of its last three games.

Tucson has lost three of four.

Boston has lost two of three.

Miami has rebounded from its Week 6 defeat and remains one of the league's strongest contenders.

Northern Arizona just delivered the week's highest score.

Cleveland keeps finding ways to win.

And Vegas may be the most dangerous team nobody expected to be talking about after an 0-4 start.

The Knight Hawks have now beaten Tucson and Cedar Rapids in consecutive weeks, scoring more than 200 points in both games.

Meanwhile, Brooklyn continues searching for its first victory.

The Bolts have now suffered seven consecutive defeats to begin the season, and their latest loss may have been the most painful yet.

But the biggest story remains Milwaukee.

For years, Tucson has been one of the Mustangs' most frustrating opponents.

This week, Michael Bautch finally ended his son's winning streak.

And he did it in convincing fashion.

Milwaukee is the league's No. 1 team.

Milwaukee has the league's best record.

And Milwaukee has the league's longest active winning streak.

Seven weeks down.

Seven weeks of drama.

And for the moment, everyone else is chasing the Mustangs.
