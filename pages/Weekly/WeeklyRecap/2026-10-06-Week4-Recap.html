GLFL WEEK 4 RECAP: Records Fall, Tucson Falls and Somehow Cleveland Is Still Waiting

By Ethan Calloway | GLFL National • October 6, 2026

Milwaukee broke the league scoring record for the second time in three weeks. Miami handed Tucson its first loss. Cedar Rapids became the last undefeated team standing. Three teams fell to 0-4. And Cleveland's pursuit of regular-season win No. 100 somehow survived another week.

Four weeks into the season, only one perfect team remains.

It isn't Tucson.

It's Cedar Rapids.

Meanwhile, Milwaukee apparently looked at the scoring record it set two weeks ago and decided it had become outdated.

The Mustangs dropped 278.60 points on Chicago, Miami stunned Tucson, Boston finally solved its Cleveland problem and Northern Arizona and Philadelphia quietly joined a growing collection of 3-1 contenders.

At the other end of the standings, Vegas, Chicago and Brooklyn are still searching for their first win.

Week 4 gave us a little bit of everything.

Milwaukee 278.60, Chicago 177.55 — The Record Lasted Two Weeks

Two weeks.

That's how long Milwaukee's previous scoring record survived.

In Week 2, the Mustangs scored 259.70 points to break the GLFL's previous regular-season record of 255.70 set by Tucson in 2025.

In Week 4, Milwaukee broke its own record.

278.60 points.

Not only is it the highest regular-season score in GLFL history, it is now the highest single-team score in any game in league history, regular season or playoffs, passing the 263.00 scored by Los Angeles in the first round of the 2025 playoffs.

And this wasn't one player dragging everybody else across the finish line.

CeeDee Lamb scored 52.3. Jared Goff added 45.6. Javonte Williams scored 37.3. Kenneth Walker III had 36.9. T.J. Hockenson added another 32.9.

Five Milwaukee starters scored more than 30 points.

Chicago wasn't even particularly bad.

The Slaughter scored 177.55, with Lamar Jackson contributing 35.1 and a solid defensive effort helping them nearly reach 180.

It didn't matter.

The combined 456.15 points made Milwaukee-Chicago the second-highest scoring regular-season matchup in league history, trailing only the 482.85 scored by Tucson and Vegas one week earlier.

Including the playoffs, Milwaukee-Chicago ranks as the fifth-highest scoring matchup in GLFL history.

And perhaps the scariest part is what Milwaukee has done since losing in Week 1.

Week Milwaukee Score Result
Week 2 259.70 Win
Week 3 205.15 Win
Week 4 278.60 Win

That's 743.45 points in three weeks.

And somewhere in Milwaukee, GM Michael Bautch probably still thinks about Week 1.

That's when Kenneth Walker III was left on the bench in the Mustangs' only loss of the season. The mistake proved costly then.

Three consecutive wins and two league scoring records later, Milwaukee is 3-1.

It could have been 4-0.

That one might sting for a while.

Cedar Rapids 189.85, Los Angeles 151.95 — And Then There Was One

Tucson entered Week 4 undefeated.

So did Cedar Rapids.

Only one made it out.

The Titans defeated Los Angeles 189.85-151.95 and improved to 4-0 for the first time since 2015.

By the end of the weekend, they were also the only undefeated team remaining in the GLFL.

Brock Purdy scored 36.8. Emanuel Wilson added 33.5. Nico Collins delivered 35.8. Tyler Allgeier and Jaylin Noel each contributed 14.9.

It was another balanced performance from a team that continues to win without needing one absurd individual performance to carry it.

The victory also meant something historically.

Los Angeles entered the game with an 11-3 all-time advantage over Cedar Rapids. The Titans had beaten the Wildcats only three times in league history, all during a three-game winning streak from 2016 through 2018.

Now they have a fourth.

More importantly, they have four wins in four games.

The Week 4 historical benchmark we highlighted before kickoff showed teams reaching 4-0 making the fantasy postseason 91.5 percent of the time.

Those weren't GLFL-specific odds, and nothing is guaranteed.

But Cedar Rapids has officially moved beyond being a nice early-season story.

The Titans are the last perfect team in the league.

Miami 220.80, Tucson 196.20 — The Sugar Skulls Finally Blink

Someone finally beat Tucson.

And Miami didn't sneak past them.

The Inferno scored 220.80 points and handed the Sugar Skulls their first loss of 2026, improving to 3-1 in the process.

Drake Maye led Miami with 52.85 points. Quinshon Judkins added 28.6, Jaylen Warren scored 23.6 and Deebo Samuel Sr. contributed 21.0.

Seven Miami offensive starters reached at least 15 points.

That's the kind of lineup-wide production it took to knock off the No. 1 team in the Power Rankings.

Tucson still scored 196.20.

Patrick Mahomes had 33.25, Carnell Tate scored 27.5, Brock Bowers added 25.6 and Ernest Jones IV led the defense with 24.0.

But Rashee Rice finished with zero, and the Sugar Skulls couldn't keep pace with Miami's 220-point afternoon.

The result snapped a long drought for the Inferno.

Miami's previous victory over Tucson came in 2019, before Mason Bautch took over the franchise. Tucson still owns the all-time series 10-6, but Richard Head finally has a win over the Mason Bautch version of the Sugar Skulls.

One week after collecting the franchise's 100th overall victory, Miami just added one of its most impressive wins in recent memory.

Boston 222.85, Cleveland 185.65 — Win No. 100 Will Have to Wait. Again.

Week 1.

99 regular-season wins.

Week 2.

Still 99.

Week 3.

Still 99.

Week 4.

Still. 99.

Cleveland's increasingly cursed pursuit of regular-season victory No. 100 will continue for at least one more week after Boston defeated the Gladiators 222.85-185.65.

And of all teams, Boston was a particularly unlikely one to stop them.

The defending champions entered Week 4 just 3-11 all-time against Cleveland, with their only victories coming in 2015, 2021 and 2024.

Make it four.

Kyren Williams erupted for 47.2 points, Zay Flowers scored 29.8, Trevor Lawrence added 29.35 and Jonathan Taylor contributed 28.7 as Boston rebounded emphatically from its Week 3 loss to Philadelphia.

Cleveland didn't play poorly.

Bryce Young scored 46.75. Puka Nacua had 33.7. Ashton Jeanty added 24.6. The Gladiators finished with 185.65 points.

They just happened to run into a 222-point Boston team.

The Brawlers move to 3-1.

Cleveland falls to 1-3.

And next week, for what feels like the 46th consecutive time, Cleveland will attempt to win regular-season game No. 100.

Philadelphia 195.80, Vegas 167.30 — The Hole Is Now 0-4

Last week, Vegas scored 239.20 points and lost.

This week, the Knight Hawks didn't come particularly close to repeating that performance.

Philadelphia defeated Vegas 195.80-167.30, moving the Soul to 3-1 while sending the Knight Hawks to 0-4.

Kirk Cousins gave Vegas 48.25 points. Chuba Hubbard added 34.9 and Derrick Henry scored 22.9.

Then came the problem.

Ja'Marr Chase: 6.7.

Mack Hollins: 10.3.

Kalif Raymond: 2.6.

That's 19.6 combined points from three starting wide receivers.

Philadelphia got 45.55 from C.J. Stroud, 33.7 from Bijan Robinson, 27.4 from Sam LaPorta and 24.6 from Chris Olave.

The Soul have now won six consecutive games against Vegas. The Knight Hawks' last victory in the series remains 2022, and Philadelphia extends its all-time advantage to 13-9.

Vegas was perhaps the league's most fascinating 0-3 team entering Week 4.

Now it's just 0-4.

Northern Arizona 198.05, Brooklyn 155.25 — Set Your Lineups, People

We need to begin with the obvious.

Brooklyn GM Cory Colvin forgot to set his lineup.

Rico Dowdle, already injured, missed Thursday night but remained in Brooklyn's starting lineup.

He scored zero.

That is not ideal roster management.

There is, however, some good news for Colvin:

It didn't cost Brooklyn the game.

Northern Arizona won 198.05-155.25, a margin of 42.80 points. There wasn't a bench substitution available that would have erased that deficit.

So Brooklyn can file this one under "embarrassing, but ultimately irrelevant."

Which is remarkable considering two Bolts nearly carried the entire team themselves.

Joe Burrow scored 51.0.

Tetairoa McMillan scored 53.2.

That's 104.2 points from two players.

Brooklyn's entire team scored 155.25.

Northern Arizona was considerably more balanced. Tyler Shough scored 34.8, Tee Higgins had 34.7, Byron Young contributed 21.5 and Ollie Gordon II scored 21.0.

Jason Richmond is now 3-0 against Brooklyn since taking over the Wranglers, and Northern Arizona improves to 3-1.

Brooklyn?

0-4.

Maybe set the lineup this week.

Week 4 Players of the Week

Offensive Player of the Week: Tetairoa McMillan, Brooklyn Bolts — 53.2 Points

There won't be much celebrating in Brooklyn after an 0-4 start, but Tetairoa McMillan did everything he possibly could.

McMillan's 53.2 points were the highest individual offensive total of Week 4, narrowly edging Miami quarterback Drake Maye's 52.85 and Milwaukee wide receiver CeeDee Lamb's 52.3.

It wasn't enough to save Brooklyn, but it earns McMillan Week 4 Offensive Player of the Week.

Defensive Player of the Week: Ernest Jones IV, Tucson Sugar Skulls — 24.0 Points

Tucson's undefeated season ended, but Ernest Jones IV delivered the week's top defensive performance with 24.0 points.

Like McMillan, Jones earns the individual honor despite his team taking a loss.

Tucson gets the bad-luck award this week.

The Sugar Skulls scored the fifth-most points in the league and would have beaten seven of the other 11 teams.

Instead, they drew Miami.

Cedar Rapids provides the inverse.

The Titans produced the seventh-highest score of Week 4 and would have gone 5-6 against the league, but 189.85 was more than enough to beat Los Angeles and preserve the league's only undefeated record.

That's fantasy football.

After Four Weeks

Cedar Rapids is 4-0.

Milwaukee, Boston, Miami, Northern Arizona, Philadelphia and Tucson are all 3-1.

And suddenly, there is a very large group forming directly behind the Titans.

At the bottom, the situation is becoming considerably more urgent.

Vegas is 0-4.

Chicago is 0-4.

Brooklyn is 0-4.

Before Week 4, the historical fantasy benchmark we examined put the playoff rate for an 0-4 team at just 11.7 percent.

Again, that's not GLFL-specific data.

But nobody wants to test it.

Meanwhile, the league's scoring record has changed hands twice in three weeks, Tucson is no longer perfect, Cedar Rapids somehow stands alone, and Cleveland remains frozen at 99 regular-season victories.

We're four weeks in.

It's probably safe to stop saying "it's still early".
