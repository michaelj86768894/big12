GLFL WEEK 10 RECAP: Tucson Shatters Scoring Record, Vegas Makes History and Milwaukee's Streak Comes Crashing Down

By Ethan Calloway | GLFL National • November 17, 2026

Records shattered. Winning streaks snapped. Playoff dreams ended. And somewhere in Tucson, a supposedly disgruntled running back just helped deliver the greatest scoring performance in GLFL history. Week 10 wasn't simply entertaining. It was historic.

Three teams surpassed 240 points, two games landed among the largest blowouts the league has ever witnessed, and the team that looked practically unbeatable a week ago finally came back to earth. Meanwhile, the Superior Division has turned into an absolute heavyweight fight, with Tucson, Northern Arizona and Vegas refusing to give each other an inch.

With four weeks remaining in the regular season, the race for seven playoff spots is only getting more intense.

Tucson 289.40, Brooklyn 155.90: The Greatest Offensive Performance in GLFL History

There are great fantasy performances. There are legendary fantasy performances. And then there is whatever Mason Bautch's Tucson Sugar Skulls just did to the Brooklyn Bolts.

289.40 points.

Read that number again.

Tucson has officially established a new GLFL record for the most points scored by a team in a single game, regular season or playoffs, surpassing Milwaukee's 278.60-point performance earlier this season.

And the Sugar Skulls didn't need one miracle performance to get there. They got contributions from practically everywhere.

Patrick Mahomes delivered 61.90 points. Ja'Marr Chase exploded for 43.10. Amon-Ra St. Brown added 27.80, while Isaiah Likely contributed a remarkable 30.70 from the tight end position. Derrick Henry chipped in 21.00.

But the most entertaining storyline belonged to Jahmyr Gibbs.

The running back who has spent recent weeks at the center of trade-request rumors, including speculation about a preferred destination in Milwaukee, delivered 55.20 points for the very franchise he reportedly wanted to leave.

Following a quiet trade deadline, Gibbs remains in Tucson. And if there was any concern about his commitment on the field, he certainly didn't show it Sunday.

Maybe Mason Bautch convinced him to stay. Maybe Gibbs is auditioning for Milwaukee. Whatever the explanation, the Sugar Skulls aren't complaining.

Brooklyn, meanwhile, actually received a spectacular 68.25-point performance from Joe Burrow. In almost any other matchup, that would have been the headline. Against Tucson? It barely registered.

The combined 445.30 points ranks as the third-highest-scoring regular-season matchup in GLFL history and sixth-highest when playoff games are included. Tucson's 133.50-point victory margin also ranks third all-time, including the postseason.

Tucson improves to 5-5 and stays firmly in the Superior Division race. Brooklyn falls to 0-10, matching last season's ten-loss total with four games still remaining.

More importantly, the Bolts are now mathematically eliminated from playoff contention. The first team officially out of the 2026 race.

For Cory Colvin, the questions are no longer about salvaging this season. They're about how to avoid making 2026 the worst campaign in franchise history.

Vegas 246.35, Cleveland 102.65: A New Standard for Destruction

Just when it seemed Tucson had monopolized the history books, Dave Nguyen and the Vegas Knight Hawks decided to write a chapter of their own.

Vegas demolished Cleveland by 143.70 points, establishing the largest margin of victory in GLFL history, regular season or playoffs.

The previous record was 142.20 points, set in 2015 when Chicago defeated Cleveland.

Yes, Cleveland was on the receiving end of both. Some franchises have championship traditions. Apparently, the Gladiators have a very specific relationship with the wrong side of the record book.

Chuba Hubbard led the Vegas assault with 44.20 points, Jaxon Smith-Njigba added 37.10, Drew Lock delivered 33.00, and Bhayshul Tuten contributed 25.30. Jordan Tyson and Josh Downs added 23.00 and 18.30, respectively.

It was a complete performance from a franchise that suddenly looks nothing like the team that started 0-4.

Remember the early-season trade that brought Smith-Njigba, Trey McBride and Tuten to Vegas from Tucson? At the time, it looked like a desperate attempt to rescue a season already slipping away.

Now the Knight Hawks are 5-5, having won five of their last six games, and they're fighting for the Superior Division crown.

Even more remarkably, Vegas now owns the first- and fourth-largest victory margins in GLFL history this season alone. Its 132.20-point destruction of Chicago in Week 8, briefly the second-largest margin ever, has already been pushed down the list.

For Cleveland, this was an astonishing collapse. Josh Thurmer's Gladiators entered the week riding a five-game winning streak after recovering from a 1-3 start.

Bryce Young managed 18.10 points, Puka Nacua was held to 0.90, Jalen Coker delivered just 0.30, and Tucker Kraft finished with 3.90. Cleveland's entire lineup combined for 102.65.

The Gladiators fall to 6-4, while Vegas reaches .500 for the first time since its opening-week loss set that disastrous start in motion.

Vegas isn't simply back in the playoff race. It's becoming one of the teams nobody wants to face.

Chicago 262.40, Los Angeles 152.20: Where Has THIS Team Been?

Darrien Clay, what in the world got into your team?

Chicago entered Week 10 at 3-6, facing a Los Angeles squad that desperately needed a win of its own. What followed was an offensive explosion that ranks among the greatest performances in league history.

The Slaughter scored 262.40 points, the third-highest single-game total in regular-season history and fourth-highest including playoffs.

Dak Prescott erupted for 65.85 points. George Pickens delivered 50.50. Emmett Johnson piled on 44.10, while Cam Skattebo added another 35.20.

That's 195.65 points from just four players. Four!

Chicago's 110.20-point victory margin ranks sixth among regular-season games and seventh including the playoffs.

It's an almost absurd turnaround for a franchise that needed until Week 5 to record its first victory. After opening 0-4, Chicago has gone 4-2 and suddenly has some life in the playoff conversation.

The frustrating question for Clay is obvious: Where was this production when Chicago desperately needed it in September?

For Los Angeles, Josh Allen's 35.30 points and James Cook's 26.70 weren't nearly enough. Brock Bowers was held to 5.80, and the Wildcats never seriously threatened Chicago's lead.

LA falls to 3-7, and the franchise is guaranteed to finish with fewer than nine wins for the first time since 2018.

Chicago improves to 4-6. The postseason remains an uphill battle, but after a performance like this, nobody can accuse the Slaughter of going quietly.

Northern Arizona 167.80, Milwaukee 158.85: The Streak Is Over

Eight consecutive victories. The league's best record. The No. 1 spot in the power rankings. And a chance to chase the greatest regular season in GLFL history.

Northern Arizona just brought Milwaukee's incredible run to a screeching halt.

The Wranglers defeated the Mustangs 167.80-158.85, ending Milwaukee's eight-game winning streak and delivering a massive result in the Superior Division race.

Christian McCaffrey was the difference-maker, exploding for 53.30 points. Ollie Gordon II added 19.50, Tee Higgins contributed 16.60, and Nakobe Dean finished with 13.75.

Northern Arizona didn't need a record-breaking performance. It needed to capitalize on a rare Milwaukee off week.

And that's exactly what Jason Richmond's team did.

Jared Goff produced a respectable 37.45 points, CeeDee Lamb added 24.20, and Justin Jefferson delivered 21.40.

But Milwaukee's depth was exposed. Javonte Williams managed 11.70, Jadarian Price finished with 12.40, and Tutu Atwell contributed just 5.10. The Mustangs also continued dealing with the fallout from Michael Wilson's season-ending neck injury.

The result is particularly significant because Milwaukee had dominated this matchup recently, winning six of the previous seven meetings.

The Mustangs fall to 8-2 and remain the team to beat in the Erie Division, but their pursuit of a 13-1 season is officially over.

The best Milwaukee can now do is finish 12-2, which would tie the best regular-season record in GLFL history.

And there's another consequence.

With Milwaukee's season scoring average dropping below the 200-point threshold, Tucson is now the league's only team averaging more than 200 points per game.

Northern Arizona improves to 6-4 and takes sole possession of first place in the Superior Division. After weeks of missed opportunities, the Wranglers finally delivered when it mattered.

Miami 157.20, Cedar Rapids 151.85: Survival of the Luckiest?

Sometimes you win because your roster dominates. Sometimes you win because you outmanage your opponent. And sometimes you win because the fantasy football gods decide it's your week.

Miami's 157.20-151.85 victory over Cedar Rapids might belong in that third category.

The Inferno lost quarterback Drake Maye at halftime, leaving their star signal-caller with just 6.50 fantasy points.

For a team chasing its eighth victory, that should have been disastrous.

Instead, Deebo Samuel Sr. delivered 28.90 points, Parker Washington exploded for 34.20, and Patrick Queen contributed 18.25. Miami somehow found enough production to survive.

But the real heartbreak belonged to Cedar Rapids.

Brock Purdy put up 59.55 points. Nico Collins added 31.40. The Titans were absolutely positioned to steal this game.

Then came the lineup disaster.

Ladd McConkey was ruled out, but by the time Cedar Rapids learned he wouldn't play, its other wide receivers had already played. The Titans were left with an empty starting receiver slot.

Zero points. A 5.35-point loss.

That's the kind of result that keeps a general manager awake at night.

Mitch Smith's Titans fall to 7-3, a costly defeat in their pursuit of Milwaukee atop the Erie Division.

Miami improves to 8-2 and has now secured a winning season for the seventh time in franchise history, the fourth-most winning seasons among GLFL teams.

It's also Miami's first winning season since 2022, a remarkable turnaround after finishing 3-11 just a year ago.

Richard Head probably won't care how pretty the victory looked. At this point in the season, survival counts just as much as dominance.

Boston 162.70, Philadelphia 140.40: The Brawlers Stop the Bleeding

Boston needed this one.

After opening the season 3-1, Chris Cholka's defending champions had stumbled through a difficult stretch, losing four of their previous five games.

A 162.70-140.40 victory over Philadelphia finally gave the Brawlers something positive to build on.

Trevor Lawrence led the way with 57.30 points, while Harold Fannin Jr. contributed 20.10. Jonathan Taylor added 17.30, and Zay Flowers finished with 14.40.

It wasn't spectacular. It wasn't particularly convincing. But for a team fighting to defend its championship, it was absolutely necessary.

Philadelphia received 42.55 points from C.J. Stroud, 21.70 from Bijan Robinson, and 20.90 from Drake London.

But the Soul couldn't overcome disappointing performances from Sam LaPorta, Chris Olave, and their defensive players.

Boston improves to 5-5, keeping itself squarely in the playoff race. Philadelphia drops to 3-7 after beginning the season 3-1.

That's six consecutive losses for Brian Simmons' Soul, a collapse that has turned a promising September into a miserable November.

The Brawlers aren't playing championship-caliber football yet. But at least they're still playing meaningful football.

Northern Arizona holds a one-game lead, but Tucson just produced the greatest offensive game in league history and Vegas is arguably the hottest team in the division.

The Knight Hawks have gone from 0-4 to 5-5. Tucson has gone from 4-1 to 5-5. And Northern Arizona, despite its own inconsistency, now controls first place.

The irony? Tucson's blockbuster trade with Vegas may have helped create the very division rival now threatening its playoff positioning.

Mason Bautch has a record-setting offense. Dave Nguyen has a team nobody wants to play. Jason Richmond has the division lead.

Something has to give.

The 200-Point Watch: Tucson Stands Alone

Earlier this season, Tucson, Milwaukee and Miami were all threatening to accomplish something no GLFL franchise had ever done: finish a season averaging more than 200 points per game.

After ten weeks, only one team remains above that historic threshold.

Tucson: 212.8 points per game.

Milwaukee's disappointing Week 10 performance has knocked the Mustangs below 200, leaving Tucson alone in pursuit of the unprecedented.

It's a remarkable statistical contradiction. The Sugar Skulls are just 5-5, yet they're producing offense at a pace the league has never seen sustained over a full season.

That's fantasy football in a nutshell. You can score more points than anybody and still spend November fighting for your playoff life.

Playoff Picture: Four Weeks to Decide Everything

Week 10 didn't just produce records. It changed the shape of the postseason race.

Milwaukee and Miami sit atop the league at 8-2, with Cedar Rapids close behind at 7-3. Northern Arizona and Cleveland are 6-4, while Tucson, Vegas and Boston are all 5-5.

That's eight teams at .500 or better competing for seven playoff berths, with Chicago at 4-6 still trying to force its way into the conversation.

Brooklyn, at 0-10, is officially eliminated. Los Angeles and Philadelphia, both 3-7, face increasingly difficult paths.

The biggest development might be Milwaukee's first loss since Week 1. The Mustangs still control their own destiny, but the aura of invincibility has disappeared.

And with Tucson and Vegas both capable of producing 240-plus-point explosions, nobody should feel particularly comfortable about drawing either team in the postseason.

Five Things We Learned in Week 10

  1. Tucson's ceiling is unlike anything we've seen. A 289.40-point performance isn't just a statement. It's a warning to every potential playoff opponent.
  2. Vegas is officially dangerous. Five wins in six games, a historic blowout, and a blockbuster trade that continues to pay dividends. Dave Nguyen has turned this season around.
  3. Milwaukee is beatable. The eight-game streak is over, and the chase for a 13-1 season has ended. The Mustangs remain elite, but the rest of the league has been reminded they're human.
  4. Chicago refuses to disappear. A 262.40-point eruption has given the Slaughter renewed hope, even if the playoff path remains difficult.
  5. Every lineup decision matters now. Cedar Rapids losing by 5.35 with an empty wide receiver slot is a brutal reminder that one roster decision can change an entire playoff race.

Calloway's Final Word

Ten weeks into the 2026 GLFL season, I thought we had a pretty good idea of who the contenders were.

Then Tucson scored 289.40. Vegas won by 143.70. Chicago dropped 262.40. Milwaukee lost. And suddenly, everything feels wide open again.

The trade deadline is behind us. The rosters are set. There are no more blockbuster deals coming to rescue a struggling franchise or strengthen a contender.

It's about the teams these general managers have built, the lineups they choose, and whether their stars show up when it matters most.

Four weeks remain. Seven playoff spots are available. One team is already out.

And after what we just witnessed?

I'm not predicting a damn thing.
