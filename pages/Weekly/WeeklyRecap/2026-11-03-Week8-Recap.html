GLFL WEEK 8 RECAP: Vegas Makes History, Liz Bautch Joins the 100-Win Club and Tucson's Collapse Continues

By Ethan Calloway | GLFL National • November 3, 2026

Eight weeks into the 2026 GLFL season, the league is beginning to look nothing like it did a month ago. Vegas has transformed an 0-4 disaster into a four-game winning streak, Cleveland has done the same after a 1-3 start, and the once-dominant Tucson Sugar Skulls have lost four of their last five. Meanwhile, Los Angeles GM Elizabeth Bautch has joined one of the most exclusive clubs in league history, Milwaukee continues to find ways to win, and Brooklyn is still searching for its first victory after another heartbreaking finish.

VEGAS 241.45, CHICAGO 109.25

From 0-4 to 4-4: Knight Hawks Deliver Historic Beatdown

Somebody needs to explain what happened to the Vegas Knight Hawks.

Four weeks ago, Dave Nguyen's team was winless, struggling to find answers and seemingly headed toward another disappointing season.

Today? Vegas might be the most dangerous team in the Superior Division.

The Knight Hawks demolished Chicago 241.45-109.25, winning their fourth consecutive game and delivering one of the most lopsided victories in GLFL history.

The 132.20-point margin ranks second all-time across both regular-season and playoff games. Only Chicago's 143.20-point victory over Cleveland in 2015 was larger.

And the irony of Chicago being on the receiving end of this one shouldn't be lost on anybody.

Kirk Cousins led the charge with 51.65 fantasy points, while Bhayshul Tuten erupted for 34.90 and Chuba Hubbard contributed 30.90.

Jaxon Smith-Njigba added 29.80, Trey McBride scored 24.20, and newly acquired Josh Downs chipped in 13.30 in his Vegas debut.

The trade acquisitions have become the defining story of this turnaround. Smith-Njigba and McBride have given Nguyen's offense a completely different identity, and Downs provides another option in the receiving corps.

Chicago, meanwhile, endured a nightmare. Lamar Jackson produced zero fantasy points, George Pickens managed just 8.80, and DK Metcalf contributed only 2.90.

The Slaughter fell to 2-6, one week after their encouraging upset of Boston.

Vegas, though, has bigger things on its mind.

At 4-4, the Knight Hawks are now tied with Tucson and Northern Arizona for the Superior Division lead.

A month ago, that sentence would have sounded ridiculous.

Today, it might be the most important development in the division.

LOS ANGELES 201.20, TUCSON 198.70

Liz Makes History as Tucson's Nightmare Gets Worse

Elizabeth Bautch waited another week for career victory No. 100.

The Los Angeles Wildcats made sure it was worth the wait.

Los Angeles edged Tucson 201.20-198.70 in a dramatic 2.50-point victory, giving Bautch her 100th career GLFL victory, including regular-season and playoff games.

She becomes just the second general manager in league history to reach the milestone, joining Milwaukee's Michael Bautch, who leads all GMs with 121 career victories.

For a franchise that entered the weekend just 2-5, the victory carried enormous significance beyond the standings.

Josh Allen delivered 47.45 fantasy points, Brock Bowers added 33.20, Josh Jacobs scored 28.20, and James Cook III contributed 25.10.

The Wildcats finished with 201.20 despite getting just 5.90 from Marvin Mims Jr. and 5.75 from Travis Hunter.

Tucson had plenty of firepower of its own.

Ja'Marr Chase erupted for 39.60, Geno Smith scored 38.90, and Jahmyr Gibbs added 33.80. But the Sugar Skulls received just 5.30 from tight end AJ Barner and couldn't quite overcome the Wildcats.

The loss is devastating for Mason Bautch's team.

Tucson opened the season 3-0 and looked like the clear favorite to win the Superior Division. Since then, the Sugar Skulls have gone 1-4, dropping three consecutive games.

And the questions surrounding the blockbuster trade with Vegas aren't going away.

Tucson sent Jaxon Smith-Njigba and Trey McBride to the Knight Hawks in a deal that brought Derrick Henry and Ja'Marr Chase to Arizona. Chase has certainly produced, but Vegas has won four straight while Tucson continues to slide.

Henry contributed just 16.00 fantasy points this week, while Smith-Njigba and McBride combined for 54.00 for Vegas.

Earlier rumors that Jahmyr Gibbs wanted out of Tucson only add to the atmosphere surrounding the franchise, though no trade request has been independently confirmed.

One thing is certain: the team that looked untouchable in September now finds itself in a three-way division tie.

As for Los Angeles, Bautch has her milestone, the Wildcats are 3-5, and their playoff hopes are still alive.

MILWAUKEE 178.35, BOSTON 171.40

Mustangs Get Their Revenge, Keep Rolling at 7-1

It wasn't the offensive explosion Milwaukee fans have grown accustomed to this season.

But it was a win over the defending champions.

And for Michael Bautch, that might have been enough.

Milwaukee defeated Boston 178.35-171.40, avenging last season's divisional playoff loss to the Brawlers and improving to a league-best 7-1.

Michael Wilson delivered a team-high 32.80 fantasy points, while Alex Singleton contributed 20.50 from the defensive position.

Jared Goff finished with 20.80, T.J. Hockenson added 18.80, and Justin Jefferson contributed 17.30.

The Mustangs didn't dominate, but they did enough to secure their seventh consecutive victory after a Week 1 loss.

Boston nearly pulled off the upset behind Trevor Lawrence's 37.50 and Adonai Mitchell's 33.20.

Harold Fannin Jr. also delivered 29.40, but the Brawlers were hurt badly by a zero from Zay Flowers and just 8.70 from Jonathan Taylor.

Boston's recent trade with Cleveland brought Cooper Kupp and Brenton Strange into the organization, but neither appeared in the Brawlers' Week 8 starting lineup.

The defending champions are now 4-4 after opening the season 3-1.

That's three losses in their last four games, an uncomfortable stretch for a franchise trying to defend its first championship.

Milwaukee, meanwhile, keeps winning even when the offense isn't firing on all cylinders.

The Mustangs remain the league's standard at the halfway point.

CLEVELAND 184.85, PHILADELPHIA 117.90

Gladiators Win Fourth Straight as Philadelphia's Season Unravels

Cleveland and Philadelphia are heading in completely opposite directions.

The Gladiators cruised to a 184.85-117.90 victory, extending their winning streak to four games and improving to 5-3 after starting the season 1-3.

Kyler Murray led Cleveland with 37.00 fantasy points, while Puka Nacua added 29.00 and Matthew Golden contributed 24.90.

Tucker Kraft chipped in 21.30, and Eric Wilson delivered 17.50 from the defensive position.

Cleveland didn't need a historic performance. The Gladiators simply received steady production throughout their lineup while Philadelphia struggled to generate offense.

The Soul were led by Caleb Williams with 31.45, but their running backs combined for just 12.90 points.

Drake London managed only 4.80, while Philadelphia's three defensive starters totaled just 20.75.

After opening the season 3-1, Philadelphia has now lost four consecutive games and fallen to 3-5.

What once looked like a promising playoff campaign is rapidly becoming a fight for survival.

Cleveland, meanwhile, is becoming one of the league's most compelling turnaround stories.

Josh Thurmer has been aggressive in the trade market, recently acquiring Kyle Monangai and Dallas Goedert from Boston to address roster needs.

Neither newcomer started in Week 8, but the Gladiators already look dramatically different from the team that stumbled through September.

Four consecutive victories have Cleveland firmly back in the playoff conversation.

CEDAR RAPIDS 197.90, NORTHERN ARIZONA 173.20

Titans Bounce Back, Wranglers Waste Another Opportunity

If Northern Arizona wanted to capitalize on Tucson's recent struggles, Week 8 presented another opportunity.

Instead, the Wranglers watched Cedar Rapids pull away for a 197.90-173.20 victory.

The Titans improved to 6-2 behind a monster performance from Tyler Allgeier, who erupted for 43.10 fantasy points.

Sam Darnold added 33.40, Bucky Irving contributed 27.00, and Dalton Kincaid delivered 22.60.

Cedar Rapids even overcame an empty wide receiver slot, receiving zero points from one of its three starting WR positions.

That's how strong the rest of the lineup was.

Northern Arizona had its own standout performances.

Tee Higgins exploded for 46.50, Jordan Love scored 42.40, and Dane Belton contributed 17.25.

But the Wranglers received just 7.70 from KC Concepcion, 8.40 from Elic Ayomanor, and 7.00 from Hunter Henry.

The loss drops Jason Richmond's team to 4-4.

Like Boston, Northern Arizona opened the season 3-1 and has gone just 1-3 since.

The frustrating part?

Tucson has practically opened the door for somebody to take control of the Superior Division, and the Wranglers haven't been able to walk through it.

Instead, Northern Arizona is now tied with Tucson and a surging Vegas team.

Cedar Rapids, meanwhile, rebounds from last week's ugly loss to Vegas and remains one of the strongest teams in the Erie Division.

MIAMI 171.40, BROOKLYN 169.40

Burrow Scores 74.10, Brooklyn Still Can't Find a Win

At some point, you have to wonder what Brooklyn needs to do to win a football game.

Joe Burrow scored an astonishing 74.10 fantasy points.

The Bolts still lost.

Miami escaped with a 171.40-169.40 victory, handing Brooklyn its eighth consecutive defeat to begin the season.

The 0-8 start is now the worst in Bolts franchise history.

Burrow's extraordinary performance gave Cory Colvin's team a legitimate opportunity to finally break through.

Rico Dowdle added 17.60, Stefon Diggs contributed 15.80, Tetairoa McMillan scored 15.80, and Quentin Lake provided 14.75.

But Brooklyn couldn't find enough production elsewhere.

Zach Charbonnet managed just 6.90, Xavier Worthy scored 5.30, and tight end Tyler Higbee contributed only 3.40.

Miami, meanwhile, got 37.45 from Drake Maye, 28.30 from Jaylen Warren, 22.60 from Luther Burden III, and 22.75 from defensive standout Patrick Queen.

The Inferno didn't deliver their most impressive performance of the season, but Richard Head's team found a way to survive.

Miami improves to 6-2 and remains in control of the Michigan Division.

For Brooklyn, the frustration continues.

The Bolts nearly defeated Cleveland in Week 7, losing by just 3.65 points. Now they've fallen to Miami by only 2.00.

That's two consecutive weeks in which Brooklyn was within a single possession of its first victory.

Instead, the record reads 0-8.

And with the season more than halfway complete, the question is no longer simply when Brooklyn will win.

It's whether the Bolts can avoid going through an entire regular season without one.

FIVE THINGS WE LEARNED IN WEEK 8

1. Vegas Is No Longer Just a Fun Comeback Story

Four consecutive victories, a historic 132.20-point blowout, and a rapidly improving roster have turned Vegas into a legitimate Superior Division contender. Dave Nguyen has gone from trying to save his season to potentially winning the division.

2. Tucson Has a Serious Problem

The Sugar Skulls were 3-0 and seemingly in complete control. Now they're 4-4, have lost three straight, and share the division lead with two teams that were chasing them from behind. The trade with Vegas will continue to be scrutinized as long as the Knight Hawks keep winning and Tucson keeps losing.

3. Cleveland's Turnaround Deserves More Attention

The Gladiators are 4-0 over the last four weeks after beginning the season 1-3. Josh Thurmer's team has quietly become one of the hottest in the league, and Cleveland is now above .500 with six regular-season games remaining.

4. The Defending Champions Are Vulnerable

Boston is 1-3 over its last four games. The Brawlers remain dangerous, but the team that captured last season's championship has struggled to establish consistency in 2026.

5. Brooklyn's Misery Has Reached Historic Levels

An 0-8 start is officially the worst in franchise history. Two consecutive narrow defeats have made the situation even more painful, and not even a 74.10-point performance from Joe Burrow was enough to change the outcome.

THE FINAL WORD

At the halfway point of the 2026 GLFL season, Milwaukee stands alone at 7-1, Cedar Rapids and Miami are chasing at 6-2, and Cleveland has suddenly entered the conversation at 5-3.

But the biggest story might be what's happening in the Superior Division.

Tucson, Northern Arizona and Vegas are all 4-4.

One team started 3-0 and has fallen apart.

Another started 3-1 and can't capitalize.

And the third started 0-4 and hasn't lost since.

If the first eight weeks have taught us anything, it's that the GLFL season is far from decided.

And for the rest of the league, the scariest words might be these:

Vegas is coming.
