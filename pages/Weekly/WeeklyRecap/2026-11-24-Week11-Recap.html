GLFL WEEK 11 RECAP: BROOKLYN FINALLY WINS, THREE TEAMS PUNCH THEIR PLAYOFF TICKETS AND THE MUSTANGS STUMBLE AGAIN

By Ethan Calloway | GLFL National • November 24, 2026

The longest losing streak of the 2026 season is over. The league's former runaway favorite has lost two consecutive games. A franchise that has spent much of its existence searching for sustained success has finally found it. And a team that looked dead in the water six weeks into the season is suddenly threatening to crash the playoff party.

Week 11 didn't rewrite the scoring record books like Week 10 did, but it may have been even more consequential.

Miami, Cedar Rapids, and Milwaukee officially secured their postseason invitations, while the battle for the remaining four playoff spots became considerably more interesting.

And somewhere in Brooklyn, a fan base finally has something to celebrate.

BROOKLYN FINALLY BREAKS THROUGH

Brooklyn Bolts (1-10) 201.10, Philadelphia Soul (3-8) 148.95

SOUND THE ALARMS. STOP THE PRESSES. THE BROOKLYN BOLTS HAVE WON A FOOTBALL GAME.

After ten consecutive losses, weeks of criticism, and an increasingly uncomfortable comparison to the infamous 2013 Boston Brawlers, Cory Colvin's Brooklyn Bolts finally found their way into the win column.

And they didn't just sneak out a victory, either.

Brooklyn dropped 201.10 points on Philadelphia, winning by 52.15 and officially putting an end to the GLFL's 0-14 Watch.

The 2013 Boston Brawlers can rest easy knowing their distinction as the only team in GLFL history to finish 0-14 remains intact.

Joe Burrow led the Bolts with 41.90 points, but unlike so many previous weeks, he actually received substantial help from the rest of his roster.

DJ Moore delivered 29.10 points, Tetairoa McMillan added 24.10, Malik Nabers contributed 22.50, and David Montgomery chipped in another 21.40.

Brooklyn's three starting wide receivers combined for 75.70 points, finally delivering the kind of production that made this roster look promising on paper.

For Philadelphia, however, this was another painful chapter in a season that has completely unraveled.

C.J. Stroud scored 40.40 points and Chris Olave added 27.70, but the Soul couldn't generate enough production elsewhere. Garrett Wilson and Emeka Egbuka combined for just 13.70 points.

Philadelphia has now lost seven consecutive games after opening the season 3-1. Its playoff probability has fallen to just 1%, according to ESPN.

Brooklyn remains mathematically eliminated, but for one week, none of that matters.

The Bolts are winners again.

Game MVP: Joe Burrow, BKN - 41.90 points

Calloway's Take: Imagine spending ten weeks waiting for your first victory and then finishing as the third-highest scoring team in the league. Brooklyn didn't just avoid history. It made Philadelphia the punchline instead.

CEDAR RAPIDS MAKES FRANCHISE HISTORY

Cedar Rapids Titans (8-3) 201.30, Tucson Sugar Skulls (5-6) 185.45

For the first time in franchise history, the Cedar Rapids Titans have secured back-to-back winning seasons.

Let that sink in for a moment.

A franchise that entered 2026 with just four winning seasons in its history has now secured its fifth, clinched its sixth postseason appearance, and reclaimed first place in the Erie Division.

And Mitch Smith's Titans accomplished all of it by defeating a Tucson team that had historically dominated this matchup.

Cedar Rapids entered Week 11 with a 4-11 all-time record against Tucson, having not defeated the Sugar Skulls since 2022.

That changed Monday night.

Brock Purdy delivered another outstanding performance with 45.75 points, while Jonah Coleman exploded for 42.90 and Bucky Irving contributed 27.00.

The Titans' quarterback and running back combination accounted for 115.65 points, providing the foundation for a 201.30-point performance.

Ladd McConkey also bounced back from last week's zero-point outing with 25.70 points, giving Cedar Rapids another reliable offensive weapon.

For Tucson, it was another frustrating defeat despite an impressive showing from its biggest stars.

Patrick Mahomes scored 54.20 points, Jahmyr Gibbs added 35.80, and Derrick Henry contributed 25.10. Together, those three players produced 115.10 points.

But with the game still within reach Monday night, Ja'Marr Chase delivered just 8.10 points against a projection of 30.70.

Chase's 22.60-point shortfall proved particularly costly in a game Tucson lost by just 15.85.

Had Chase simply reached his projection, with everything else remaining the same, Tucson would have won.

Instead, the Sugar Skulls are back below .500 just one week after setting the GLFL's all-time single-game scoring record.

Tucson's season continues to be one of the league's greatest contradictions: an extraordinarily talented, record-setting roster that cannot consistently translate its production into victories.

Meanwhile, Cedar Rapids is no longer just a feel-good story.

The Titans are legitimate championship contenders.

Game MVP: Brock Purdy, CR - 45.75 points

Calloway's Take: Cedar Rapids has already accomplished something no previous Titans team could. Now comes an even bigger opportunity: a Week 12 showdown with Milwaukee for control of the Erie Division. Clear your schedule.

TROUBLE IN MILWAUKEE?

Cleveland Gladiators (7-4) 152.05, Milwaukee Mustangs (8-3) 145.85

Two weeks ago, the Milwaukee Mustangs were 8-1, riding an eight-game winning streak and threatening to make a run at the greatest regular-season record in GLFL history.

Today, they aren't even leading their own division.

Cleveland handed Milwaukee its second consecutive defeat in Week 11, escaping with a 152.05-145.85 victory that further tightened the Erie Division race.

It wasn't pretty, but Josh Thurmer's Gladiators desperately needed a response after suffering the largest defeat in GLFL history against Vegas last week.

Bryce Young led Cleveland with 33.50 points, Ashton Jeanty added 29.90, Calvin Ridley scored 21.80, and Justice Hill contributed 21.60.

Cleveland's balanced production proved just enough to overcome a Milwaukee lineup that continues to struggle to find consistency outside of its biggest stars.

CeeDee Lamb delivered 39.20 points for the Mustangs, J.J. McCarthy scored 30.45, and Javonte Williams added 29.10.

Those three players combined for 98.75 of Milwaukee's 145.85 points.

The rest of the lineup?

Just 47.10 points across seven starting positions.

Makai Lemon scored 1.30, Dalton Schultz managed 3.10, and Christian Elliss occupied a defensive starting spot despite being on a bye.

In a game decided by 6.20 points, that empty defensive position is particularly difficult to overlook.

Milwaukee has now scored 158.85 and 145.85 points in consecutive weeks, averaging just 152.35 points during its two-game losing streak.

For a team that spent much of the season flirting with a 200-point scoring average, that's a concerning decline.

There is some good news for Michael Bautch's Mustangs: Milwaukee officially clinched its 11th postseason appearance in franchise history.

But with Cedar Rapids now holding first place in the Erie Division and Cleveland sitting just one game behind both teams, the Mustangs suddenly have much more to worry about than playoff qualification.

Game MVP: Bryce Young, CLE - 33.50 points

Calloway's Take: Is it time to panic in Milwaukee? Not yet. But the Mustangs have gone from chasing history to chasing Cedar Rapids in just two weeks. Week 12 will tell us a lot about this team's championship credentials.

CHICAGO IS NOT DEAD YET

Chicago Slaughter (5-6) 221.75, Northern Arizona Wranglers (6-5) 168.65

Remember when the Chicago Slaughter were 1-5?

Remember when their season appeared to be over before Halloween?

Apparently, nobody told Darrien Clay.

Chicago continued its remarkable late-season resurgence with a commanding 221.75-168.65 victory over Northern Arizona, securing its fourth win in five games and posting the highest team score of Week 11.

One week after scoring 262.40 points against Los Angeles, the Slaughter delivered another offensive explosion.

Dak Prescott was absolutely sensational, scoring 71.15 points to lead all players in the GLFL this week.

Emmett Johnson added 33.30, George Pickens contributed 28.80, and George Kittle delivered 20.40.

Prescott and Pickens alone accounted for 99.95 points, continuing to demonstrate just how dangerous Chicago's Dallas connection can be.

Chicago has now scored 484.15 points over its last two games, an average of 242.08 points per contest.

Northern Arizona received solid performances from Tyler Shough (33.50), Breece Hall (26.00), and Nakobe Dean (19.00), but the Wranglers simply couldn't keep pace.

Chicago has now won seven of its last eight meetings against Northern Arizona and taken a 9-8 lead in the all-time series.

More importantly, ESPN now gives the Slaughter a 42% chance of reaching the postseason.

That's a remarkable turnaround for a team that looked destined to spend November discussing next season.

But the road ahead is brutal.

Nobody said this comeback would be easy.

Game MVP: Dak Prescott, CHI - 71.15 points

MIAMI KEEPS FINDING A WAY

Miami Inferno (9-2) 143.00, Los Angeles Wildcats (3-8) 138.95

It wasn't dominant. It wasn't particularly impressive. It certainly wasn't comfortable.

But it was another Miami victory.

The Inferno escaped Week 11 with a narrow 4.05-point win over Los Angeles, ending a four-game losing streak against the Wildcats and securing their ninth postseason appearance in franchise history.

Miami now stands alone atop the GLFL standings at 9-2.

With Drake Maye out of the starting lineup following last week's injury, Cam Ward stepped in and delivered a crucial 40.70-point performance.

Parker Washington continued his productive season with 24.30 points, while Devaughn Vele added an unexpected 19.60.

Miami needed every bit of that production.

Chase Brown and Jaylen Warren combined for just 15.50 points, while Tyler Warren managed only 4.20 at tight end.

For Los Angeles, Josh Allen scored 31.20 points, James Cook III added 23.20, and Braelon Allen contributed 16.40.

But Oronde Gadsden's zero-point performance at tight end proved costly in the closest matchup of the week.

The loss guarantees Los Angeles will finish without a winning record for the first time since 2017, bringing an extraordinary run of eight consecutive winning seasons to an end.

ESPN now gives the Wildcats just a 1% chance of reaching the playoffs.

Meanwhile, Miami continues to demonstrate that winning doesn't always require putting up spectacular numbers.

The Inferno have scored just 455.80 points over their last three games, averaging 151.93 points per contest.

They've won all three.

Game MVP: Cam Ward, MIA - 40.70 points

Calloway's Take: Miami went 3-11 last season. Now Richard Head's team is 9-2 and alone atop the standings. The Inferno haven't been overwhelming lately, but their ability to survive difficult weeks is becoming a defining characteristic of this team.

THE DEFENDING CHAMPIONS ARE STILL HERE

Boston Brawlers (6-5) 161.80, Vegas Knight Hawks (5-6) 154.55

In a matchup with enormous postseason implications, the Boston Brawlers reminded everyone that they aren't ready to surrender their championship just yet.

Chris Cholka's Brawlers edged Vegas by 7.25 points, moving above .500 and creating valuable separation from one of their direct competitors in the playoff race.

Jayden Daniels led Boston with 34.95 points, while Zay Flowers contributed 21.40 and Khalil Shakir added 18.30.

Boston's defensive unit also delivered when it mattered, combining for 45.25 points behind Jamien Sherwood, Alex Highsmith, and Jack Gibbens.

That defensive production helped compensate for a disappointing 7.70-point performance from Jonathan Taylor.

Vegas received another strong outing from Chuba Hubbard, who scored 34.00 points, while Antonio Williams added 26.20 and Trey McBride contributed 16.80.

Unfortunately, Daniel Jones managed just 10.50 points at quarterback, and Jordyn Tyson added only 3.00.

One week after dismantling Cleveland by a league-record 143.70 points, the Knight Hawks managed just 154.55 in defeat.

That's a decline of 91.80 points in a single week.

Boston has now won three consecutive meetings against Vegas, narrowing the Knight Hawks' all-time series advantage to 8-7.

With three games remaining, Boston sits at 6-5 while Vegas joins Tucson and Chicago at 5-6.

That one-game difference could prove enormous when the final playoff field is determined.

Game MVP: Jayden Daniels, BOS - 34.95 points

Calloway's Take: Boston didn't need a championship-caliber scoring performance this week. It needed a championship-caliber result. The defending champions got exactly that.

FIVE TAKEAWAYS FROM WEEK 11

1. Chicago Is Becoming Everyone's Problem

Four wins in five games. Nearly 500 points over the last two weeks. A 42% chance of making the playoffs. Chicago has gone from an afterthought to one of the most dangerous teams in the league. Nobody should want to see this roster sneak into the postseason.

2. Milwaukee's Margin for Error Has Disappeared

The Mustangs have secured a playoff spot, but their grip on the Erie Division has slipped away. Two consecutive losses and declining offensive production have created legitimate questions about a team that looked nearly unstoppable earlier this season.

3. Cedar Rapids Deserves More Respect

Back-to-back winning seasons for the first time ever. Eight victories. A playoff berth. First place in the Erie Division. At some point, we need to stop talking about Cedar Rapids as a surprising team and start talking about the Titans as a championship contender.

4. Tucson Is the League's Biggest Mystery

How does a team with Patrick Mahomes, Jahmyr Gibbs, Derrick Henry, Amon-Ra St. Brown, and Ja'Marr Chase find itself at 5-6? The Sugar Skulls continue to produce impressive scoring totals, but their inability to consistently win games has put their postseason hopes in jeopardy.

5. Miami Doesn't Care How It Wins

The Inferno were the second-lowest scoring team in Week 11 and still emerged with the league's best record. Richard Head's team has won high-scoring shootouts and low-scoring battles alike. At 9-2, Miami has earned its place atop the standings.

THE FINAL WORD

Week 11 was a reminder that fantasy football seasons aren't decided in September or October.

Chicago looked finished at 1-5. Milwaukee looked untouchable at 8-1. Tucson looked like a championship favorite after assembling one of the most talented rosters the league has seen.

Today, Chicago is fighting for a playoff spot, Milwaukee is fighting to reclaim its division, and Tucson is fighting just to get back to .500.

Cedar Rapids is making franchise history. Miami is quietly stacking victories. Boston is keeping its championship defense alive.

And Brooklyn?

Brooklyn finally won.

The 0-14 Watch is over. The playoff race is far from it.

Three weeks remain. Four playoff spots are available. And next week, two of the league's best teams will collide with the Erie Division lead on the line.

Welcome to the business end of the GLFL season.
