GLFL WEEK 1 RECAP: The Champs Haven't Gone Anywhere

By Ethan Calloway | GLFL National/League Correspondent • September 15, 2026

Boston opened its title defense by dropping the highest score in the league. Tucson and Cleveland weren't far behind. Milwaukee left a win sitting on its bench. And somewhere in Chicago, the GLFL Survivor competition already has its first casualty.

One week.

Six games.

And absolutely no patience whatsoever for reasonable conclusions.

Welcome back to the Great Lakes Football League.

The first week of the GLFL era delivered three 200-point performances, a defending champion that looked very much like a defending champion, a pair of long-running head-to-head trends that only became more lopsided and at least one General Manager who will probably spend the next several days staring at his bench and wondering what could have been.

That's the beauty of Week 1.

Nobody has been eliminated from the actual championship race.

But we're absolutely going to overreact anyway.

🏆 BOSTON 224.15, MIAMI 183.35 — THE CHAMPS PICK UP WHERE THEY LEFT OFF

Apparently Boston didn't get the memo that championship hangovers are supposed to be a thing.

The defending champion Brawlers opened 2026 with the highest score in the entire GLFL, beating Miami 224.15–183.35 and immediately reminding everyone why the Lakefront Bowl road still runs through Boston.

Trevor Lawrence erupted for 50.55 points, Jonathan Taylor added 32.1, Zay Flowers scored 31.0 and Dallas Goedert contributed 26.7 as Boston received production throughout the lineup.

Miami wasn't bad.

That's what makes this worse.

Jacoby Brissett scored 39.25, Parker Washington added 23.3 and DeVaughn Vele scored 22.9. The Inferno finished with 183.35 points — a perfectly respectable Week 1 total.

Boston simply didn't care.

And at this point, the matchup is becoming something bigger than a winning streak.

Since Chris Cholka took over the Brawlers in 2022, he is now 7–2 against Miami. Former Inferno GM Levetzow managed a 2–2 split against Cholka.

Current Miami GM Richard Head?

0–5.

Five meetings. Five Boston victories.

Calloway's Take: It is one game, and nobody should be planning a championship parade in September. But when the defending champion immediately produces the highest score in the league, the burden of proof remains with everybody trying to catch them.

🔥 TUCSON 222.45, LOS ANGELES 180.55 — STILL LOADED, STILL DANGEROUS

Boston had the highest score of Week 1.

Tucson missed it by 1.70 points.

So much for easing into the season.

The Sugar Skulls opened 2026 with a 222.45–180.55 victory over Los Angeles behind a monster 48.6-point performance from Jahmyr Gibbs.

Isaiah Likely added 33.8, Jaxon Smith-Njigba scored 31.2 and Patrick Mahomes contributed 35.5.

Los Angeles received a massive 58-point performance from Josh Allen and another 32.7 from Amon-Ra St. Brown, but the Wildcats simply couldn't match Tucson's depth.

And the rivalry continues moving in one direction.

Tucson has now defeated Los Angeles three consecutive times and owns a 10–7 advantage in the all-time series.

There was one additional source of pain for Los Angeles.

T.J. Watt scored 26.5 points from the Wildcats' bench.

It wouldn't have erased a 41.9-point deficit by itself, but it certainly didn't make the box score any prettier.

Calloway's Take: Tucson spent the offseason acting like a 12–2 season followed by a championship loss was a personal insult. Week 1 did nothing to suggest Mason Bautch has gotten over it.

⚔️ CLEVELAND 219.25, CHICAGO 147.3 — FOUR STRAIGHT LOSSES, THEN THIS

Cleveland didn't just end its losing streak against Chicago.

The Gladiators put it through a wall.

After losing four consecutive meetings to the Slaughter dating back to 2023, Cleveland opened the season with a 219.25–147.3 demolition.

The margin: 71.95 points.

Bryce Young led the way with an absurd 58.05 points, Ashton Jeanty added 41.7 and Jalen Coker scored 39.8.

Chicago received 44.2 from Lamar Jackson and 22.9 from Mike Evans.

After that?

Things got unpleasant.

Jaylen Waddle scored 1.2. Jamien Sherwood scored 4.5. Chicago's three defensive players combined for just 21.5 points.

And because Week 1 apparently wasn't painful enough, Christian Watson's 37.7 points were sitting on the Chicago bench.

Watson wouldn't have rescued Chicago from a 71.95-point beating.

But that's probably not going to stop Marcus Reed from mentioning it.

Calloway's Take: Cleveland entered 2026 as one of those teams everybody agreed had talent. Talent is nice. Scoring 219.25 and finally beating a team that had owned you for three years is considerably nicer.

🗡️ CEDAR RAPIDS 207, MILWAUKEE 189 — THE WIN THAT SAT ON THE BENCH

Cedar Rapids deserves credit.

Let's establish that before Milwaukee fans start throwing things.

The Titans scored 207 points behind 44.15 from Brock Purdy, 25.3 from Bucky Irving, 25.2 from Nico Collins and 24.2 from Ladd McConkey.

That's a strong opening performance from Mitch Smith's team.

Milwaukee still should have won.

There is no delicate way to put this.

Kenneth Walker III scored 44.1 points from Michael Bautch's bench.

Milwaukee lost by 18.

Javonte Williams scored 28.2. Justin Jefferson scored 36.2. Jared Goff contributed 32.5.

But Saquon Barkley managed only 11.0, A.J. Brown scored 7.6 and Walker's 44.1 counted for absolutely nothing.

Put the right combination on the field and Milwaukee leaves Week 1 at 1–0.

Instead, the Mustangs are 0–1.

Fantasy football is a beautiful game.

Calloway's Take: Milwaukee can complain about the schedule — and, statistically, the Mustangs actually have a case. They were a top-half scoring team that lost. But when 44.1 points are sitting on your bench, maybe keep the schedule complaints to a reasonable volume.

🪽 PHILADELPHIA 207.95, BROOKLYN 159.6 — THE SOUL START WITH A STATEMENT

Philadelphia spent much of the offseason hearing the same thing.

Nice roster.

Now win something.

One regular-season game doesn't answer the larger question, but 207.95 points is a pretty good opening argument.

Caleb Williams exploded for 60.95 points, the biggest individual performance in this matchup, while Bijan Robinson scored 40.3 and Chris Olave added 35.2.

Brooklyn couldn't keep pace.

Joe Burrow scored 35.7 and David Montgomery added 33.9, but Jake Ferguson's 2.6 and a quiet defensive group left the Bolts without enough firepower to threaten Philadelphia.

The final was 207.95–159.6.

Brian Simmons has heard plenty about Philadelphia's inability to turn talented rosters into postseason success.

Week 1 isn't the postseason.

But 1–0 beats the alternative.

Calloway's Take: Derek Thompson has probably already ordered the championship banner. The rest of us will wait. But Philadelphia scoring more than 200 in the opener deserves attention.

🤠 NORTHERN ARIZONA 187.1, VEGAS 177.05 — NGUYEN'S WRANGLER PROBLEM CONTINUES

Different year.

Same problem for Dave Nguyen.

Northern Arizona defeated Vegas 187.1–177.05, extending the Wranglers' winning streak in the series to three games and pushing their all-time advantage to 11–9.

But the more interesting number belongs to Nguyen.

0–5.

Nguyen went 0–2 against Northern Arizona during his original stint running Vegas in 2012.

He returned to the Knight Hawks in 2025.

He's now 0–3 against the Wranglers since coming back.

Five career meetings with Northern Arizona.

Zero victories.

Jordan Love's 43.85 points led the Wranglers, while Breece Hall added 26.8. Vegas received 43.3 from Derrick Henry and 38.4 from D'Andre Swift, but Ja'Marr Chase's 4.2 and Kayvshon Boutte's 4.1 proved costly.

Vegas lost by just 10.05 points.

And Northern Arizona remains the opponent Nguyen simply cannot solve.

Calloway's Take: At 0–5, we're past coincidence. Dave Nguyen has a Northern Arizona problem.

🦆 THE LUCKY DUCK REPORT

Welcome to the newest scientific advancement from GLFL National.

And by "scientific," we mean we're going to publicly identify who benefited from the schedule and who got punched in the mouth by it.

The rules are simple.

A Lucky Duck is a team that scores in the bottom half of the league and still wins.

An Unlucky Loss belongs to a team that scores in the top half of the league and still loses.

If you're calling yourself unlucky and you're not on this list?

Relax.

You're not special.

🦆 WEEK 1 LUCKY DUCK: NORTHERN ARIZONA

Northern Arizona scored 187.1 points, seventh among the league's 12 teams.

Bottom half.

The Wranglers happened to draw Vegas, which scored 177.05.

Result?

1–0.

Quack.

💀 WEEK 1 UNLUCKY LOSS: MILWAUKEE

Milwaukee scored 189 points, sixth-best in the GLFL.

Top half.

Five teams scored fewer points and won or had a chance to win against a different opponent.

Milwaukee drew Cedar Rapids at 207.

That's legitimately unfortunate.

Again, though:

Kenneth Walker III. 44.1. Bench.

We cannot stress this enough.

☠️ GLFL SURVIVOR: AND THEN THERE WERE 11

Twelve teams entered Week 1 alive.

Eleven remain.

The rules of GLFL Survivor are mercilessly simple: the lowest-scoring team still alive is eliminated every week.

Once you're gone, you're gone.

Score 300 next week?

That's wonderful.

Enjoy it from the afterlife.

WEEK 1 ELIMINATION: ☠️ Chicago Slaughter — 147.3 points

Darrien Clay and the Slaughter become the first team eliminated from the inaugural GLFL Survivor competition.

Better luck in 2027.

🌎 THE PARALLEL UNIVERSE

Every General Manager believes the schedule is conspiring against him.

We're going to find out who actually has a case.

Each week, The Parallel Universe takes every team's actual fantasy scores and asks a simple question:

What would your record be if you had somebody else's schedule?

The scores don't change.

Only the opponents do.

When swapping into the schedule of the team that actually played you, the matchup is reversed — you face that team rather than yourself.

After only one week, the records obviously aren't dramatic yet.

Give it time.

By November, somebody is going to hate this table.

The Week 1 extremes are already obvious.

Boston's 224.15 would have beaten every opponent on every available Week 1 schedule.

Chicago's 147.3?

There wasn't a schedule in the multiverse that was saving the Slaughter.

That's evidence.

THE BOTTOM LINE

Week 1 didn't answer many questions.

It created better ones.

Boston wanted to prove the 2025 championship wasn't the end of its story.

The Brawlers responded with 224.15 points.

Tucson wanted to prove last year's championship loss hadn't changed its trajectory.

The Sugar Skulls scored 222.45.

Cleveland wanted to prove its young roster was ready to become something more than interesting.

The Gladiators scored 219.25 and ended a four-game losing streak against Chicago.

Philadelphia wanted its talent to finally start producing results.

The Soul cleared 200 and started 1–0.

Northern Arizona wanted another shot at proving this could finally be the year.

The Wranglers survived Vegas and kept Dave Nguyen winless against them.

Cedar Rapids wanted to establish itself as a contender.

The Titans scored 207 and walked out of Milwaukee with a win.

And Milwaukee?

Milwaukee would presumably like Kenneth Walker III's 44.1 points back.

Too late.

Week 1 is over.

Eleven teams remain alive in Survivor.

One Lucky Duck has already been identified.

The Parallel Universe is officially open for business.

And the defending champions are still standing exactly where everybody left them.

At the top.
