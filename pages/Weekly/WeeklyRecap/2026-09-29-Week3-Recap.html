GLFL WEEK 3 RECAP: History in Tucson, Trouble in Brooklyn and a New Contender in Cedar Rapids

By Ethan Calloway | GLFL National • September 29, 2026

Tucson and Vegas rewrote the record book, Philadelphia knocked off the defending champions, Cedar Rapids moved to 3-0 and three weeks of football have already created a fascinating divide in the GLFL playoff picture.

Three weeks.

That's usually when we start telling everyone not to overreact. I'm not going to do that.

Not after Tucson and Vegas combined for the highest-scoring regular-season game in league history. Not after Philadelphia dropped 227 on the defending champions. Not after Cedar Rapids reached 3-0 for the first time in more than a decade. And definitely not after the first serious look at the GLFL playoff model showed just how quickly the league is beginning to separate.

Week 3 gave us plenty to talk about.

Tucson 243.65, Vegas 239.20: An Instant Classic

For a little while Sunday, it looked like Vegas was finally going to get one.

The 0-2 Knight Hawks had pushed their win probability to 54 percent against the top-ranked team in the league. They were scoring. Tucson was under pressure. An upset was very much alive.

Then Tucson hit the accelerator.

Jahmyr Gibbs erupted for 52.4 points. Jaxon Smith-Njigba added 45.5. Brock Bowers scored 34.6. Patrick Mahomes contributed 32.4. James Cook III finished with 27.4.

Five Tucson starters scored at least 27 points.

And the crazy part is that Vegas didn't fold.

Kirk Cousins scored 46.7. Juwan Johnson had 33.8. Ja'Marr Chase added 30.8. Derrick Henry scored 25.9. Vegas finished with 239.20 points and put together one of the best offensive performances in GLFL regular-season history.

It still wasn't enough.

Tucson escaped 243.65-239.20 in a game that produced 482.85 combined points, the highest-scoring regular-season matchup in GLFL history.

The previous record was 443.30, set by Los Angeles and Northern Arizona in Week 7 of 2025. Tucson and Vegas didn't just break that record. They obliterated it by 39.55 points.

Across all GLFL games, including the postseason, Sunday's shootout ranks second all-time. The only matchup to produce more offense was Tucson's 485.20-point playoff battle with Boston in the first round of the 2024 postseason.

Individually, Tucson's 243.65 ranks as the fifth-highest regular-season team score in league history, while Vegas' 239.20 ranks 10th.

Vegas finally played like a team that deserved to win.

Tucson reminded them that deserving it and getting it are two entirely different things.

The Sugar Skulls are 3-0.

The Knight Hawks are somehow 0-3.

Philadelphia 227.00, Boston 167.05: The Champs Go Down

Boston knew it was in trouble before Sunday even arrived.

Bijan Robinson and Drake London opened the week by combining for 82.2 points, immediately putting the defending champions in a hole. Boston needed an answer.

It never really came.

Robinson finished with 47.3 points, London had 34.9 and Garrett Wilson added 32.7. Jeremiyah Love scored 26.9 and Chris Olave contributed another 26.7.

Philadelphia finished with 227.00 points and handed Boston its first loss of the season.

The result becomes even more interesting when you look at the history between these teams. Philadelphia beat Boston in Week 10 last season, snapping a six-game Boston winning streak in the series dating back to 2019.

Now the Soul have beaten the Brawlers twice in a row.

Once is an upset.

Twice starts becoming a matchup Boston may not enjoy seeing on the schedule anymore.

Philadelphia improves to 2-1. Boston falls to 2-1.

#FlySoulFly.

Cedar Rapids 189.20, Cleveland 162.65: The Titans Are 3-0

Go ahead and read that headline again.

Cedar Rapids is 3-0.

For the first time since 2015, the Titans have opened a season with three consecutive victories. Sam Darnold led the way with a massive 59.45-point performance, while Tyler Higbee added 26.2 and Wan'Dale Robinson scored 22.7.

After entering the season with plenty to prove, Cedar Rapids now sits atop the Erie Division at 3-0.

Cleveland, meanwhile, will leave Week 3 wondering what might have happened with a healthy lineup.

Starting running back De'Von Achane was projected for 25.6 points but left injured after scoring only 2.7. Wide receiver Jalen Coker, projected for 16.8, was also injured and finished with 4.8.

That's 42.4 projected points from two starters that turned into just 7.5.

Would Cleveland have won if both stayed healthy?

Maybe. Maybe not.

Cedar Rapids won the game that was actually played, and a 3-0 record doesn't need an apology.

But Cleveland's pursuit of its 100th regular-season victory will have to wait at least one more week.

Milwaukee 205.15, Chicago 170.20: The Mustangs Keep Rolling

One week after setting the GLFL regular-season scoring record with 259.70 points, Milwaukee apparently decided 200 would be enough this time.

It was.

Jared Goff scored 40.05, Michael Wilson added 32.9, Kenneth Walker III and Javonte Williams each scored 26.3 and CeeDee Lamb finished with 25.2.

Milwaukee did it despite losing Justin Jefferson early. Jefferson finished with only 6.2 points, but the rest of the Mustangs lineup provided more than enough offense in a 205.15-170.20 victory.

Here's one of the stranger numbers of the week: Chicago was projected to score 170.0.

Chicago scored 170.2.

The projection couldn't have been more accurate. The problem was Milwaukee scored 205.15.

The Mustangs improve to 2-1 and have now scored 464.85 points over the last two weeks.

Chicago falls to 0-3 for only the second time in franchise history. The other occurrence came in 2021, when the Slaughter opened 0-4.

And now for the good news, Chicago.

You get Milwaukee again next week.

Miami 211.70, Brooklyn 143.00: Turns Out the Quarterback Wasn't the Problem

Last week, Brooklyn GM Cory Colvin watched Jaxson Dart leave on the opening drive and finish with three points.

Colvin pointed out afterward that Joe Burrow was waiting on his bench, prompting Tucson GM Mason Bautch to offer the helpful observation that maybe Brooklyn deserved it for starting Dart in the first place.

Fine.

Brooklyn started Burrow this week.

He scored 46.4 points.

Brooklyn lost by 68.7.

Turns out the quarterback wasn't the problem.

Miami rolled to a 211.70-143.00 victory behind 53.4 points from Jacoby Brissett, 27.6 from Jaylen Warren and a massive 27.0-point defensive performance from Will Anderson Jr.

The victory moves Miami to 2-1 and gives the Inferno franchise its 100th all-time victory, including the regular season and playoffs.

Brooklyn falls to 0-3 for the first time since 2022, when the Bolts eventually opened 0-5.

There is still plenty of season left.

But history suggests this isn't a place Brooklyn wanted to revisit.

Northern Arizona 226.25, Los Angeles 184.70: The Wranglers Punch Back

Los Angeles entered Week 3 having won nine of its last 10 meetings with Northern Arizona.

The Wranglers apparently had enough of that statistic.

Northern Arizona erupted for 226.25 points behind 52.35 from its quarterback spot, 28.6 from Christian McCaffrey, 25.0 each from Tee Higgins and Matthew Golden, 25.15 from Jakobi Meyers and 22.75 from Nakobe Dean.

Los Angeles received 47.8 from Matthew Stafford and solid production throughout much of the lineup, but it wasn't enough to keep pace.

Northern Arizona improves to 2-1 with a 41.55-point victory over a franchise that has dominated this matchup historically.

Maybe one win doesn't erase a 5-12 all-time series deficit.

But if you're Jason Richmond, 226 points is a pretty good place to start.

Nobody is mathematically eliminated in September.

Let's make that clear before anyone starts throwing furniture. *cough* GM Clay *cough*

Tucson's 3-0 start has the Sugar Skulls sitting at 100 percent in the current model, while Milwaukee, Cedar Rapids and Northern Arizona are all above 93 percent.

But perhaps the strangest number belongs to Vegas.

The Knight Hawks are 0-3 and still carry a 46.05 percent playoff probability. That's actually higher than 2-1 Miami at 42.60 percent.

If you're looking for proof that record and underlying performance aren't always the same thing, the team that just scored 239.20 points and lost probably isn't a bad place to start.

At the other end, Chicago has fallen to 0.44 percent while Brooklyn sits at 0 percent in the current model.

Again: model probability is not mathematical elimination.

But 0-3 has consequences.

It's September.

Nothing has been won yet.

But the race is beginning to take shape.

GLFL Week 3 Players of the Week

Offensive Player of the Week: Sam Darnold, Cedar Rapids Titans — 59.45 Points

Cedar Rapids needed a big performance to reach 3-0 for the first time since 2015.

Sam Darnold gave them a massive one.

Darnold's 59.45 points led all players in Week 3 and helped push the Titans past Cleveland, 189.20-162.65.

There were plenty of worthy candidates in an outrageous offensive week — Jahmyr Gibbs scored 52.4 for Tucson, Northern Arizona received 52.35 from its quarterback spot and Miami got 53.4 from Jacoby Brissett — but nobody topped Darnold.

Defensive Player of the Week: Talanoa Hufanga, Cleveland Gladiators — 29.75 Points

Cleveland didn't get the victory, but Talanoa Hufanga turned in the best defensive fantasy performance of Week 3.

Hufanga's 29.75 points narrowly beat Miami's Will Anderson Jr., who scored 27.0 in the Inferno's win over Brooklyn.

Three Weeks Down

Tucson is 3-0 after surviving one of the greatest shootouts in league history.

Cedar Rapids is 3-0 for the first time since 2015.

Philadelphia just dropped 227 on the defending champions.

Milwaukee has scored 464.85 points in two weeks.

Northern Arizona just hung 226 on a team that had beaten it nine times in the previous 10 meetings.

Miami is 2-1.

Vegas scored 239 points and is 0-3.

Chicago and Brooklyn are staring up from the bottom at 0-3.

And somehow we're only three weeks into this thing.

See you in Week 4.
