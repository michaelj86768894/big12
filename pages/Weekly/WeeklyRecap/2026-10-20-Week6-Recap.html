GLFL WEEK 6 RECAP: Vegas Stuns No. 1 Tucson, Two Franchises Hit 100 Losses and Cleveland Survives by 0.30

By Ethan Calloway | GLFL National • October 20, 2026

In Week 6, two franchises reached 100 regular-season losses, one game was decided by three-tenths of a point, and the league's No. 1 team got demolished by players it had just traded away. Meanwhile, Milwaukee won its fifth straight, Los Angeles extended an astonishing head-to-head streak, and the defending champions sent Brooklyn deeper into an increasingly miserable season.

Sometimes the Great Lakes Fantasy League delivers a weekend of competitive football.

Sometimes it delivers absolute chaos.

Week 6 chose chaos.

The biggest shock came in the Superior Division, where Vegas dismantled Tucson just days after completing a six-player blockbuster with the Sugar Skulls. The Knight Hawks entered the matchup at 1-4, facing the league's top-ranked team, and proceeded to score a week-high 216.20 points.

Elsewhere, Cleveland and Northern Arizona produced one of the closest finishes in league history. Chicago's Darrien Clay reached a milestone he'd probably prefer to forget. And Los Angeles reminded Philadelphia that some rivalries simply refuse to change.

Six games. Six stories. And another week that will leave the GLFL newsroom with plenty to discuss.

VEGAS 216.20, TUCSON 157.60

The Trade Reunion Turns Into a Tucson Nightmare

Well, that escalated quickly.

Less than a week after trading three players to Vegas, Tucson GM Mason Bautch watched two of those players help the Knight Hawks dismantle his Sugar Skulls in a 216.20-157.60 blowout.

And just like that, the No. 1 team in the GLFL Power Rankings had been handed a 58.60-point defeat by a team that entered the week with one victory.

The timing could not have been more extraordinary.

Tucson had sent Jaxon Smith-Njigba, Trey McBride and Bhayshul Tuten to Vegas in exchange for Derrick Henry, Ja'Marr Chase and Juwan Johnson.

It was Mason's second blockbuster in roughly a week, following an earlier three-for-three deal with Los Angeles.

The Sugar Skulls were already 4-1 and coming off a historic 254.70-point performance against defending champion Boston.

Apparently, that wasn't enough to convince their general manager to leave the roster alone.

Now the first returns are in.

And Vegas is smiling.

Former Sugar Skulls Deliver

Smith-Njigba led the Vegas receiving corps with 33.40 points, while McBride added 17.80 at tight end.

Those two former Tucson players combined for 51.20 points against their old team.

Vegas also received 40.65 points from Kirk Cousins, 28.00 from D'Andre Swift, 27.10 from Chuba Hubbard and 22.75 from Jeffery Simmons.

The result was the Knight Hawks' highest-scoring performance of the season and their second consecutive victory.

For GM Dave Nguyen, it was the kind of statement win that can change the conversation surrounding a struggling franchise.

Vegas didn't merely upset Tucson.

It dominated.

Mason's New-Look Offense Stumbles

The Sugar Skulls' new additions did not enjoy the same immediate success.

Henry, acquired from Vegas in the blockbuster, scored just 8.00 points in his Tucson debut.

Chase, the other marquee acquisition, was unavailable because of his bye week.

Meanwhile, Tucson's starting tight end was Isaiah Likely, who delivered a strong 28.90 points. That performance provided some relief at a position Mason had repeatedly reshaped through his recent trades.

Patrick Mahomes scored 24.20 points, DeMario Douglas added 23.60 and Germie Bernard contributed 30.70.

But the Sugar Skulls received just 6.70 from Ty Johnson and nothing from Brian Thomas Jr.

It was a far cry from the balanced offensive explosion Tucson had produced against Boston.

And the trade implications are impossible to ignore.

Across two major deals, Tucson effectively exchanged James Cook III, Brock Bowers, Carnell Tate and Smith-Njigba for Amon-Ra St. Brown, Henry, Chase and Juwan Johnson.

Those moves may still pay off over the remainder of the season.

But in their first meeting after the Vegas trade, the players heading out of Tucson made a considerably stronger impression than the players coming in.

Tucson falls to 4-2. Vegas improves to 2-4.

And Marcus Vance now has a fascinating decision to make at the top of his next Power Rankings.

Calloway's Take: One game doesn't determine who won a blockbuster trade. But when the players you just dealt help beat you by nearly 60 points, you can expect the questions to get louder. Mason has built a roster with enormous upside. Week 6 was a painful reminder that upside doesn't always translate into immediate results.

CLEVELAND 192.55, NORTHERN ARIZONA 192.25

Three-Tenths of a Point. One Historic Finish.

There are close games.

There are heartbreaking games.

And then there is what happened between Cleveland and Northern Arizona.

The Gladiators escaped with a 192.55-192.25 victory, a margin of just 0.30 points.

That's the ninth-closest game in GLFL history, including both regular-season and playoff contests.

Three-tenths.

In a league where individual players can score 40, 50 or even 60 points in a single week, the difference between victory and defeat was less than one fantasy point.

For Cleveland GM Josh Thurmer, the result was another dramatic victory following the Gladiators' historic win over previously undefeated Cedar Rapids in Week 5.

For Northern Arizona GM Jason Richmond, it was an agonizing defeat with an additional historical consequence.

The Wranglers entered Week 6 sitting at 99 regular-season losses.

They left with 100.

Two Quarterbacks, One Razor-Thin Margin

Northern Arizona received 44.40 points from Tyler Shough and 36.70 from Christian McCaffrey.

Dameon Pierce added 20.00, while Hunter Henry contributed 20.30 at tight end.

Those performances helped the Wranglers produce 192.25 points, a total that would have beaten nine of the other ten teams in action this week.

Unfortunately for Northern Arizona, Cleveland wasn't one of them.

Bryce Young led the Gladiators with 37.65 points. Matthew Golden continued to justify his recent acquisition with 28.90, while Kyle Pitts Sr. added 27.00 and Jalen Coker scored 19.70.

Ashton Jeanty contributed 19.80, and Puka Nacua added 16.00 after his massive Week 5 performance.

Cleveland's defensive contributors supplied enough production to preserve the narrowest of advantages.

Quincy Williams, the hero of Cleveland's defensive unit a week earlier, added 11.50 points.

Every fraction mattered.

The victory improves Cleveland to 3-3, bringing the Gladiators back to .500 after an 1-3 start.

Northern Arizona also falls to 3-3, having lost two consecutive games.

And the Wranglers now own a place in GLFL history that nobody was hoping to claim.

Calloway's Take: Cleveland has won two straight games with entirely different kinds of drama. First came Puka Nacua's Monday-night rescue mission against Cedar Rapids. Then came a 0.30-point escape against Northern Arizona. The Gladiators are back to .500, but the Wranglers will be thinking about this one for a long time.

CEDAR RAPIDS 168.20, CHICAGO 144.20

Chicago Reaches 100 Losses, and a Rejected Trade Adds Another Twist

Darrien Clay has been Chicago's general manager since the GLFL began in 2012.

He's been there for the victories, the disappointments, the roster changes and everything in between.

Now he's been there for 100 regular-season losses.

Chicago fell 168.20-144.20 to Cedar Rapids in Week 6, dropping to 1-5 and becoming the latest franchise to reach the century mark in regular-season defeats.

Because Clay is the only GM in Slaughter history, the milestone belongs to him personally as well.

It was a particularly frustrating afternoon for Chicago, which had finally earned its first victory of the season in Week 5.

The Slaughter entered the matchup hoping to build momentum against a Cedar Rapids team coming off its first defeat.

Instead, the Titans bounced back.

The Trade That Didn't Happen

But the most intriguing subplot began before kickoff.

Milwaukee GM Michael Bautch reportedly approached Clay with a trade proposal that would have sent T.J. Hockenson, Makai Lemon and Saquon Barkley to Chicago in exchange for George Kittle and Christian Watson.

Clay declined.

According to details of the negotiations, one reason for the rejection was Clay's expectation that Kittle would benefit from a strong performance by San Francisco quarterback Brock Purdy, who was starting for Cedar Rapids.

The logic was understandable: if Purdy had a productive afternoon, Kittle could benefit from the same passing offense.

And the numbers were almost comically precise.

Purdy scored 35.00 points for Cedar Rapids.

Kittle delivered 19.90 for Chicago, while Mike Evans contributed 15.40.

Together, Kittle and Evans produced 35.30 points.

A difference of just 0.30 points from Purdy's total.

The anticipated relationship between quarterback production and Chicago's receiving production wasn't enough to change the outcome.

Cedar Rapids won by 24.00 points.

And Clay's 100th regular-season loss became official.

To be clear, the rejected trade wasn't the reason Chicago lost. Kittle was actually one of the Slaughter's more productive starters, and nobody can know how the proposed trade would have affected the result.

But as GLFL storylines go, the timing was remarkable.

Titans Regroup

Cedar Rapids received 35.00 points from Purdy, 23.50 from DeVonta Smith and 22.60 from Tommy Tremble.

RJ Harvey added 19.60, while Carson Schwesinger contributed 17.50 on defense.

The Titans didn't produce the kind of overwhelming performance that characterized their undefeated start, but they did enough to earn an important victory.

Chicago, meanwhile, received 29.60 points from Dak Prescott, 19.80 from George Pickens and 19.90 from Kittle.

Cam Skattebo was held scoreless, and Rhamondre Stevenson managed just 6.60.

Cedar Rapids improves to 5-1 and remains firmly in the championship race.

Chicago drops to 1-5, with another difficult chapter added to Clay's long tenure.

Calloway's Take: The rejected Milwaukee trade will generate plenty of conversation, but Chicago's bigger problem remains consistency. The Slaughter scored 202.65 points in Week 5 and just 144.20 in Week 6. Cedar Rapids didn't need a spectacular performance to win. It simply needed a steadier one.

MILWAUKEE 169.40, MIAMI 138.65

Five Straight Wins, and the Mustangs Keep Rolling

Milwaukee doesn't need to score 278 points every week.

Apparently, 169 will do just fine.

The Mustangs defeated Miami 169.40-138.65 to improve to 5-1, extending their winning streak to five games following an opening-week defeat.

The matchup had been billed as one of the premier showdowns of Week 6.

Both teams entered at 4-1. Both had demonstrated championship-level offensive potential. And Miami was coming off consecutive performances of 220.80 and 243.65 points.

The Inferno had scored 464.45 points over two games.

Against Milwaukee, they managed just 138.65.

That's a dramatic reversal for an offense that had looked nearly unstoppable.

Walker Leads the Way

Kenneth Walker III delivered 35.90 points to lead the Mustangs, while Michael Mayer contributed 24.00 from the tight end position.

CeeDee Lamb scored 20.00, Jameis Winston added 18.00 and Javonte Williams contributed 17.00.

The Mustangs also received 13.80 from Rashid Shaheed and 12.75 from Alex Singleton.

It wasn't Milwaukee's most explosive performance of the season, but it was balanced enough to secure a comfortable 30.75-point victory.

Miami received 45.15 points from Drake Maye, who continued his excellent season following a 69.20-point explosion in Week 5.

Luther Burden III added 19.50, Jerry Jeudy scored 14.90 and Jaylen Warren contributed 14.80.

But the Inferno received only 2.40 from Mark Andrews, while their three defensive starters combined for just 19.00 points.

The lack of supporting production proved costly.

The 200-Point Chase

Milwaukee's victory also helped sharpen one of the most remarkable statistical storylines of the season.

Through six weeks, Milwaukee and Tucson are the only two GLFL teams still averaging more than 200 points per game.

No team in league history has finished a season averaging 200 points per game.

The previous high-water mark was Los Angeles in 2025, when the Wildcats averaged 191.0.

Milwaukee and Tucson are now chasing a standard that has never been reached.

And with Tucson suffering a blowout defeat this week, the Mustangs may have gained an important advantage in the race for the league's most productive offense.

For GM Michael Bautch, the larger objective remains winning another championship.

But with five consecutive victories and a historic scoring pace, Milwaukee is building a compelling early-season resume.

Miami falls to 4-2 and will need to rediscover the offensive balance that fueled its previous two victories.

Calloway's Take: Milwaukee continues to find different ways to win. The Mustangs have produced historic offensive explosions, survived narrow finishes and now handled one of the league's hottest teams by more than 30 points. At 5-1, they're doing considerably more than simply keeping pace.

LOS ANGELES 210.90, PHILADELPHIA 135.20

Eleven Straight. Philadelphia Still Has No Answer for Los Angeles.

Different season.

Different rosters.

Same result.

Los Angeles extended its remarkable winning streak against Philadelphia to eleven consecutive games with a 210.90-135.20 demolition of the Soul.

The Wildcats now lead the all-time series 14-4.

Eleven straight victories against the same opponent is an extraordinary accomplishment in any fantasy league.

And this one wasn't particularly close.

Los Angeles won by 75.70 points, delivering its strongest offensive performance of the season and improving to 2-4.

For Philadelphia GM Brian Simmons, it was another frustrating chapter in a rivalry that has become increasingly one-sided.

LA's New Additions Make Their Mark

Josh Allen led the Wildcats with 38.85 points, while James Cook III contributed 33.30.

Travis Hunter delivered 28.40 on defense, Carnell Tate added 23.00 and Ryan Flournoy scored 20.00.

Braelon Allen contributed another 20.40.

Cook and Tate were both acquired from Tucson in the blockbuster trade that also brought Brock Bowers to Los Angeles.

Their combined 56.30 points provided another encouraging return on that deal.

Bowers added 14.00 at tight end.

The Wildcats' 210.90-point total was particularly significant because LA entered the week at 1-4 and badly needed a victory to remain within reach of the playoff race.

Philadelphia, meanwhile, struggled to find consistent production.

Caleb Williams scored 34.55 points, Chris Olave added 17.30 and Drake London contributed 14.20.

But Bijan Robinson managed just 8.30, while Pat Freiermuth scored only 4.10 at tight end.

The Soul fell to 3-3, dropping back to .500 after a promising start.

And the question that has followed Philadelphia through this rivalry remains unanswered.

What will it take to beat Los Angeles?

Eleven consecutive meetings have produced the same answer.

Not enough.

Calloway's Take: Los Angeles has been one of the league's most frustrating teams this season, but its talent has never been in question. The Wildcats showed what their offense can look like when multiple contributors deliver. Philadelphia, meanwhile, has to be wondering whether this particular matchup is ever going to change.

BOSTON 191.80, BROOKLYN 136.60

The Champions Respond. Brooklyn's Nightmare Continues.

Boston needed a response after being embarrassed by Tucson in Week 5.

Brooklyn needed a miracle.

Only one team got what it wanted.

The defending GLFL champions defeated Brooklyn 191.80-136.60, improving to 4-2 and handing the Bolts their sixth consecutive loss to open the season.

The Brawlers were led by a spectacular 54.40-point performance from Trevor Lawrence.

Jonathan Taylor added 29.10, Colston Loveland contributed 20.50 and Jamien Sherwood delivered 20.75 on defense.

Khalil Shakir scored 15.40, while Zay Flowers added 14.70.

It was the kind of balanced performance Boston needed after surrendering 254.70 points to Tucson the previous week.

The victory also extended Boston's winning streak against Brooklyn to seven consecutive meetings.

Brooklyn still leads the all-time series 11-8, but the recent history has belonged entirely to the Brawlers.

Another Wasted Performance for Brooklyn

The Bolts once again received an outstanding performance from one of their stars.

Malik Nabers scored 37.00 points, continuing to produce despite Brooklyn's increasingly difficult season.

Tyler Huntley added 26.05 and Tetairoa McMillan contributed 17.60.

But Darren Waller was held scoreless, Kenny Gainwell managed just 5.40 and the defensive unit provided limited support.

The result was another defeat, dropping Brooklyn to 0-6.

For GM Cory Colvin, the question is no longer simply how to turn the season around.

It's also how to approach a trade market that may offer opportunities to reshape the roster.

Tucson has reportedly expressed interest in Nabers, with Mason Bautch potentially willing to assemble a four-for-one package.

No completed deal has been announced.

But with Brooklyn still winless, speculation surrounding the franchise's future is unlikely to disappear.

Boston, meanwhile, has recovered from its championship rematch disappointment and remains firmly positioned among the league's contenders.

Calloway's Take: The Brawlers did exactly what a defending champion should do after a humiliating loss: respond convincingly. Brooklyn continues to get excellent individual performances without enough production across the roster. At 0-6, the Bolts are running out of room for another disappointing Sunday.

The Playoff Picture After Week 6

Milwaukee and Cedar Rapids have separated themselves from the rest of the league at 5-1, while Tucson, Miami and Boston remain close behind at 4-2.

Cleveland has climbed back to .500 with consecutive victories, while Vegas and Los Angeles both improved to 2-4 after impressive wins.

Looking Ahead

Six weeks into the season, the GLFL championship race is becoming more complicated by the day.

Milwaukee and Cedar Rapids sit atop the standings at 5-1, but their paths have been very different.

The Mustangs have won five consecutive games and continue chasing a historic scoring pace.

The Titans have recovered from their first defeat and remain among the league's strongest teams.

Tucson still possesses enormous talent, but its aggressive roster overhaul has introduced new questions after a stunning defeat.

Miami remains dangerous despite an uncharacteristically quiet offensive week.

Boston is back on track.

Cleveland is suddenly surging.

And Vegas just announced that the bottom half of the standings isn't necessarily where it intends to stay.

Meanwhile, the trade market remains active, with multiple general managers exploring significant roster changes.

If Week 6 taught us anything, it's that reputations, projections and Power Rankings offer no guarantees.

A 1-4 team can dismantle the league's No. 1 contender.

A 192-point performance can still end in heartbreak.

And a general manager can get exactly the production he anticipated from his players and still suffer a historic defeat.

That's the GLFL.

Six weeks down.

And the chaos is only getting started.
