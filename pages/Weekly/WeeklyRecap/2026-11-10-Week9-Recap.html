GLFL WEEK 9 RECAP: Milwaukee Keeps Rolling, Tucson Keeps Falling and the Playoff Race Turns Into a Traffic Jam

By Ethan Calloway | GLFL National • November 10, 2026

Nine weeks down. Five to go. And somehow, the Great Lakes Fantasy League has managed to turn what looked like a predictable playoff picture into complete chaos. Milwaukee keeps winning. Cleveland has forgotten how to lose. Tucson has forgotten how to win. Boston just reached a milestone nobody wants. And according to ESPN's latest projections, eight teams currently have at least a 51% chance of reaching the postseason. There's just one problem: only seven get in.

Week 9 featured two 220-point explosions, another devastating Brooklyn defeat, a narrow Miami escape, and a Tucson team whose early-season championship expectations are beginning to look increasingly distant. Let's get into it.

MKE-BKN | CLE-LA | CR-BOS | NAZ-TUC | MIA-LV | CHI-PHI | Playoff Race

Milwaukee Mustangs 182.05, Brooklyn Bolts 145.35

Eight Wins, One Loss, and Another Winning Season in Milwaukee

At some point, winning becomes less of an accomplishment and more of an organizational expectation. For Michael Bautch and the Milwaukee Mustangs, that point arrived a long time ago.

Milwaukee improved to a league-best 8-1 with a 36.70-point victory over Brooklyn, clinching the franchise's 11th winning season in 14 years of GLFL competition. Eleven winning seasons. Fourteen years. That's not a hot streak. That's sustained excellence.

Kenneth Walker III delivered 44.30 fantasy points, while CeeDee Lamb added 37.50 and Javonte Williams contributed 27.40. The Mustangs didn't even need much from quarterback Jared Goff, who finished with just 13.00 points.

But the victory came with a significant cost. Milwaukee wide receiver Michael Wilson has been lost for the remainder of the season with a neck injury. Wilson had contributed 15.00 points in the victory, and his absence creates another personnel challenge for a Mustangs roster that has already dealt with its share of injuries.

Milwaukee has the record, the scoring production, and the pedigree of a championship favorite. But maintaining that momentum through the final five weeks will require navigating an increasingly complicated injury situation.

Brooklyn, meanwhile, fell to an astonishing 0-9.

Joe Burrow scored 54.25 fantasy points, one of the week's biggest individual performances. It wasn't remotely enough. The Bolts received just 4.40 from tight end Greg Dulcich and 9.00 combined from defensive players Kyle Hamilton and Robert Spillane.

The season has officially become a weekly exercise in finding new ways to lose for GM Cory Colvin. Brooklyn has now extended the worst start in franchise history to nine consecutive defeats.

Calloway's Take: Milwaukee doesn't need to be perfect to win games anymore. That's what separates contenders from everyone else. Brooklyn, on the other hand, got 54 points from its quarterback and still lost by nearly 37. There's not much left to say.

Cleveland Gladiators 223.05, Los Angeles Wildcats 127.15

Cleveland's Fifth Straight Win Sends a Message to the Entire League

Remember when Cleveland was 1-3?

Neither do the Gladiators.

Josh Thurmer's team has now won five consecutive games, improving to 6-3 after dismantling Los Angeles by 95.90 points. Cleveland's 223.05 was the highest score of Week 9, and the victory ended an eight-game head-to-head losing streak against the Wildcats dating back to 2019, including playoff meetings.

Bryce Young led the way with 47.10 points, but Cleveland's receivers were the real story. Puka Nacua delivered 46.50, Matthew Golden erupted for 39.40, and Justice Hill added 20.70 from the backfield.

Cleveland entered the season with questions about its ability to compete with the league's established heavyweights. Those questions are getting harder to ask with every passing week.

The Gladiators have now transformed a 1-3 start into a 6-3 record, and suddenly they're not merely fighting to make the playoffs. They're looking like a team nobody wants to face once they get there.

For Los Angeles, the afternoon was disastrous. Josh Allen managed 32.20 points and Brock Bowers contributed 27.30, but running back Josh Jacobs finished with just 0.50. The Wildcats managed only 127.15 overall.

GM Elizabeth Bautch remains at 100 career victories after reaching the milestone in Week 8, but her Wildcats have fallen to 3-6 and are running out of opportunities to salvage their season.

Calloway's Take: Five straight wins and 223 points against a team that had owned them for years. Cleveland isn't sneaking up on anybody anymore. The Gladiators are a legitimate postseason threat.

Cedar Rapids Titans 222.60, Boston Brawlers 146.90

Titans Rebound, Boston Hits the Century Mark in Losses

Boston entered Week 9 hoping to stop its recent slide. Instead, the Brawlers became the latest victims of a Cedar Rapids offensive explosion.

The Titans improved to 7-2 with a 75.70-point victory, powered by Brock Purdy's 47.60 fantasy points, Bucky Irving's 34.50, and an outstanding 30.75-point defensive performance from Carson Schwesinger.

Cedar Rapids also received 22.80 from Jonah Coleman and 19.50 from Barrett Carter. Even with Ladd McConkey recording a zero, the Titans comfortably cleared 220 points.

That's the kind of depth that makes Mitch Smith's team dangerous. A starting receiver contributes nothing, and the offense still produces one of the week's biggest scores.

Boston's defeat carried an unfortunate historical distinction: the Brawlers suffered their 100th franchise loss.

For defending champion GM Chris Cholka, the milestone arrives during an increasingly frustrating stretch. Boston opened the season 3-1 but has gone just 1-4 since, dropping to 4-5 overall.

Jayden Daniels scored 32.30 and Myles Garrett contributed 20.50, but the Brawlers received only 6.80 from Zay Flowers and couldn't keep pace with Cedar Rapids.

A team that entered the year hoping to defend its championship is now fighting simply to remain above the playoff cut line.

Calloway's Take: Cedar Rapids just scored 222 with a zero in its starting lineup. Boston, meanwhile, is 1-4 over its last five games. The defending champions are running out of time to rediscover their identity.

Northern Arizona Wranglers 212.30, Tucson Sugar Skulls 195.65

Seven-Game Rivalry Streak Ends as Tucson's Nightmare Continues

The Tucson Sugar Skulls started this season 3-0. They looked like championship favorites. They had the league's most feared offense. They had control of the Superior Division.

Six weeks later, they're 4-5.

Northern Arizona delivered another crushing blow to Mason Bautch's increasingly troubled team, winning 212.30 to 195.65 and snapping Tucson's seven-game winning streak in the rivalry.

The victory was especially significant for GM Jason Richmond, whose Wranglers had repeatedly come up short against Tucson, including a defeat in the 2022 Great Lakes Bowl.

Jordan Love was magnificent, producing 59.90 fantasy points. Christian McCaffrey added 28.70, Tee Higgins contributed 24.20, and KC Concepcion finished with 21.80. Northern Arizona needed a major performance against a talented Tucson roster, and it got one.

Tucson certainly didn't go quietly. Patrick Mahomes scored 38.90, Ja'Marr Chase delivered 39.50, Amon-Ra St. Brown added 22.10, and Jahmyr Gibbs contributed 18.40.

But 195.65 wasn't enough.

And that's becoming the defining frustration of Tucson's season. The Sugar Skulls remain capable of producing excellent fantasy scores, but the victories have disappeared. After starting 3-0, Tucson has lost five of its last six games.

The loss also arrives amid growing speculation surrounding Gibbs' future in Tucson. GLFN's Alex Ramirez previously reported that the star running back wanted a trade to a contender, and Mason Bautch publicly suggested Milwaukee could be a potential destination.

No Gibbs trade has been confirmed, but the timing couldn't be more uncomfortable. Tucson is losing games, losing control of its division, and now dealing with questions about the future of one of its most important players.

Northern Arizona improves to 5-4 and moves into first place in the Superior Division. Tucson falls to 4-5, one game behind both the Wranglers and the surging Vegas Knight Hawks.

Calloway's Take: The question isn't whether Tucson has enough talent. It clearly does. The question is how a team that looked unbeatable in September has managed to lose five of six. And if Gibbs really wants out, this situation could get even messier.

Miami Inferno 155.60, Vegas Knight Hawks 149.00

Miami Survives, Vegas' Four-Game Winning Streak Ends

Sometimes the difference between winning and losing isn't a spectacular performance. Sometimes it's simply the decision you didn't make.

Miami escaped with a 6.60-point victory over Vegas, improving to 7-2 and maintaining control of the Michigan Division.

Drake Maye scored 37.25 fantasy points, Luther Burden III added 24.20, and Caleb Douglas contributed 17.00. It wasn't Miami's most explosive performance of the season, but it was enough.

Vegas, meanwhile, saw its remarkable four-game winning streak come to an end after climbing all the way back from an 0-4 start.

And the most painful part? The Knight Hawks had the points to win sitting on their bench.

Wide receiver Kayshon Boutte erupted for 32.50 fantasy points, but GM Dave Nguyen left him out of the starting lineup. Boutte had been projected for just 5.50 points, while Vegas' starting receivers all carried projections above 10.

In other words, the decision made perfect sense before kickoff. Afterward? Not so much.

Jaxon Smith-Njigba, one of the centerpieces of the blockbuster Tucson trade, managed just 14.10 points. Trey McBride added 14.40. D'Andre Swift struggled with 5.10.

Vegas had the opportunity to make it five consecutive victories and keep pressure on Northern Arizona in the Superior Division. Instead, the Knight Hawks fall to 4-5.

Still, there's reason for optimism. This is a team that started 0-4 and has completely transformed its season through aggressive roster moves. One disappointing week doesn't erase that progress.

Calloway's Take: You can't seriously blame Nguyen for benching a receiver projected for 5.5 points. But losing by 6.6 while watching 32.5 points sit on the bench? That's the kind of fantasy football pain that stays with you.

Chicago Slaughter 183.85, Philadelphia Soul 179.60

Chicago Steals Another Close One as Philadelphia's Slide Reaches Five

Philadelphia was 3-1.

Five weeks later, the Soul are 3-6.

Chicago edged Philadelphia by just 4.25 points, handing Brian Simmons' team its fifth consecutive defeat and turning what once looked like a promising season into a full-blown crisis.

The loss was particularly brutal because Philadelphia received an extraordinary 65.20-point performance from quarterback C.J. Stroud and another 35.70 from Bijan Robinson.

That's 100.90 points from two players. And the Soul still lost.

Chicago countered with 37.85 from Dak Prescott, 35.10 from Christian Watson, and a massive 32.50 from defensive player Arvell Reese.

But the story of this game might be sitting on Philadelphia's bench.

Defensive player Nik Bonitto scored 15.00 fantasy points, while starting defensive player Trent McDuffie finished with zero.

Had Simmons started Bonitto instead, Philadelphia would have won 194.60 to 183.85.

That's a 10.75-point victory turned into a 4.25-point defeat by one lineup decision.

For Chicago, the victory improves its record to 3-6 and gives Darrien Clay another reason to believe there's still something worth fighting for this season.

Philadelphia, however, is watching its playoff hopes disappear one painful Sunday at a time.

Calloway's Take: Imagine getting 65 points from your quarterback, another 35 from your running back, and losing because you left the wrong defensive player on the bench. That's Philadelphia's season in one game.

The Playoff Picture: Eight Contenders, Seven Tickets

With five weeks remaining in the regular season, the postseason race is beginning to take shape. But don't mistake that for clarity.

According to ESPN's current playoff projections, eight teams have at least a 51% chance of reaching the postseason: Milwaukee, Cedar Rapids, Miami, Cleveland, Northern Arizona, Tucson, Vegas, and Boston.

Only seven teams qualify.

That means at least one team currently considered more likely than not to make the playoffs will be left out, assuming those eight remain the leading contenders.

Five Things We Learned in Week 9

1. Milwaukee's Standard Is Different

Eleven winning seasons in 14 years. An 8-1 record. The league's best start. Michael Bautch has built an organization where winning isn't the exception. It's the expectation. The question now is whether the Mustangs can overcome their injury concerns and turn regular-season success into another championship.

2. Cleveland Might Be the League's Most Dangerous Team

Five consecutive victories after starting 1-3, capped by a 223-point explosion. The Gladiators have transformed from an early-season disappointment into a legitimate contender. Nobody should be looking forward to seeing Cleveland on their playoff schedule.

3. Tucson's Collapse Is No Longer a Slump

Five losses in six games. A seven-game rivalry winning streak snapped. Division leadership surrendered. Trade rumors surrounding Jahmyr Gibbs. This is no longer a minor rough patch. It's a season threatening to unravel.

4. The Defending Champions Are in Trouble

Boston's 100th franchise loss arrives during a 1-4 stretch that has pushed the Brawlers below .500. Last year's championship banner isn't going anywhere, but another postseason appearance is far from guaranteed.

5. Every Lineup Decision Matters Now

Philadelphia left a game-winning defensive performance on its bench. Vegas watched 32.5 points from Kayshon Boutte go unused in a 6.6-point defeat. With only five weeks remaining, those decisions could determine who plays in December and who watches from home.

Calloway's Final Word

The first half of the 2026 GLFL season was about establishing contenders. The second half is going to be about surviving.

Milwaukee looks like the team to beat. Cedar Rapids and Miami aren't far behind. Cleveland is charging. Northern Arizona is finally taking advantage of Tucson's mistakes. Vegas has reinvented itself. Boston is fighting to stay alive. And Tucson is desperately trying to remember how it became a championship favorite in the first place.

Eight teams. Seven playoff spots. Five weeks.

Somebody is going to be heartbroken.

And if the first nine weeks have taught us anything, it's that the GLFL rarely delivers heartbreak quietly.
