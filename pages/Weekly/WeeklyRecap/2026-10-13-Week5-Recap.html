GLFL WEEK 5 RECAP: Cleveland Hits 100, Tucson Gets Revenge and the Last Perfect Season Falls

By Ethan Calloway | GLFL National • October 13, 2026

For four weeks, Cedar Rapids couldn't lose. For four weeks, Cleveland couldn't reach 100 wins. On Monday night, Puka Nacua solved both problems. Elsewhere, Tucson turned its championship rematch into a 254-point statement, Miami continued rewriting expectations, Chicago and Vegas finally entered the win column, and Milwaukee discovered that sometimes a backup running back is all you need.

Five weeks into the 2026 GLFL season, there are no undefeated teams remaining.

There is, however, one franchise still searching for its first victory.

And there are three offenses threatening to rewrite the league's record book.

Week 5 delivered a little bit of everything.

Cleveland completed a dramatic Monday-night comeback to secure its 100th regular-season victory. Tucson avenged last season's championship defeat with one of the highest-scoring performances in league history. Miami continued its remarkable turnaround.

Chicago and Vegas finally won.

Brooklyn didn't.

And somewhere in the middle of all that, one general manager spent the week sending trade offers around the league while another openly contemplated next season.

Welcome to the GLFL.

Let's recap Week 5.

Cleveland 206.80, Cedar Rapids 188.55: One Hundred Ways to End a Perfect Season

Going into Monday night, Cedar Rapids was undefeated.

Cleveland was trailing by approximately 26 points.

And the Gladiators were running out of time to reach a milestone that had been waiting since week 1.

Then Puka Nacua happened.

With Cleveland needing a massive performance, Nacua delivered 47.70 fantasy points against Buffalo.

Cedar Rapids, meanwhile, was relying on Dalton Kincaid to help protect its lead.

Kincaid scored 3.70.

That's a 44-point difference between the two players.

By the time Monday night ended, Cleveland had completed an extraordinary comeback, defeating Cedar Rapids 206.80-188.55.

The victory was Cleveland's 100th regular-season win in franchise history.

It also ended Cedar Rapids' undefeated season.

For four weeks, the Titans were the only team nobody could beat.

For four weeks, the Gladiators couldn't find victory No. 100.

One Monday night changed both stories.

Cleveland also received 35.55 points from Kyler Murray, 27.00 from Quincy Williams and 25.30 from Matthew Golden.

Golden's performance was particularly notable. The receiver had arrived in Cleveland earlier in the week as part of a trade with Northern Arizona.

Cedar Rapids received 38.50 points from Bucky Irving, 31.05 from Brock Purdy and 19.25 from Carson Schwesinger.

But it wasn't enough.

The Titans fall to 4-1, ending the best opening winning streak in franchise history.

Cleveland improves to 2-3 and extends its all-time series advantage over Cedar Rapids to 13-8.

And here's where the story gets even better.

Earlier in the week, Cleveland general manager Josh Thurmer had been actively shopping players around the league.

One proposed deal would have sent Nacua to Tucson.

Had that trade been completed, the player who delivered Cleveland's historic victory wouldn't have been on the Gladiators' roster.

Sometimes the best trade is the one you don't make.

Tucson 254.70, Boston 176.90: Championship Revenge Served Cold

Mason Bautch waited an entire offseason for another shot at the Boston Brawlers.

He made it count.

Tucson demolished the defending champions 254.70-176.90, delivering one of the most impressive offensive performances in GLFL history.

The Sugar Skulls' 254.70 points rank fourth among regular-season performances and sixth among all games, including the playoffs.

The two teams combined for 431.60 points, the ninth-highest combined total in a regular-season matchup in league history.

But for Tucson, this was about considerably more than statistics.

Boston defeated the Sugar Skulls in last season's Lakefront Bowl.

Tucson entered Week 5 having won five of its previous seven meetings against Boston since Mason Bautch took over the franchise.

The two losses came in 2023 and last season's championship.

That second defeat had been difficult to forget.

This time, there was no fourth-quarter suspense.

Jahmyr Gibbs erupted for 48.70 points. Aaron Rodgers contributed 39.35. Jaxon Smith-Njigba added 28.30, and DeMario Douglas scored 26.50.

Trey McBride, acquired from Los Angeles earlier in the week, delivered 25.60 points in his Tucson debut.

Nate Landman added 23.75 on defense.

Boston received 49.25 points from Jayden Daniels, but the rest of the Brawlers couldn't keep pace with Tucson's relentless production.

The defeat also denied Boston its first-ever 4-1 start.

Tucson improves to 4-1. Boston falls to 3-2.

And just one week after suffering its first loss against Miami, Tucson responded by scoring 254.70 against the team that took away its championship.

Message received.

Miami 243.65, Northern Arizona 166.60: The Inferno Is Getting Hotter

At what point do we stop calling Miami a surprise?

The Inferno finished 3-11 last season.

Five weeks into 2026, they're 4-1.

And their latest performance suggests this turnaround is no accident.

Miami overwhelmed Northern Arizona 243.65-166.60, recording the seventh-highest regular-season single-team score in GLFL history.

Drake Maye was extraordinary.

The Miami quarterback scored 69.20 fantasy points, throwing for 374 yards and five touchdowns.

It was the kind of performance that deserves consideration among the league's most memorable individual outings.

Jaylen Warren added 41.80 points. Parker Washington contributed 33.40. Tyler Warren scored 26.40.

Those four players combined for 170.80 points.

That's more than Northern Arizona's entire starting lineup.

The Wranglers received 43.95 points from Jordan Love, but their offense couldn't match Miami's production.

Northern Arizona falls to 3-2 after entering the week tied with Miami at 3-1.

Richard Head's Inferno, meanwhile, have now beaten Tucson and Northern Arizona in consecutive weeks.

Two teams that entered those matchups with legitimate championship aspirations.

Miami didn't simply beat them.

The Inferno scored a combined 464.45 points across the two games.

The franchise that won just three games last season has already surpassed that total.

And with one of the league's most explosive offenses, Miami is rapidly becoming a serious championship contender.

Chicago 202.65, Brooklyn 143.00: Somebody Had to Win

Well, somebody finally did.

Chicago and Brooklyn entered Week 5 with identical 0-4 records.

One team was guaranteed to earn its first victory.

The other was guaranteed to fall to 0-5.

The Slaughter made sure there wasn't much suspense.

Chicago defeated Brooklyn 202.65-143.00, snapping the Bolts' six-game winning streak in the head-to-head series.

And Darrien Clay's receiving corps was the difference.

Christian Watson scored 35.30. George Pickens added 34.00. Mike Evans contributed 32.30.

Three receivers. A combined 101.60 points.

Dak Prescott also contributed 27.80, while Rhamondre Stevenson added 20.50.

It was Chicago's most important performance of the young season.

Not only did the Slaughter secure their first victory, they avoided the first 0-5 start in franchise history.

They also avoided their 100th regular-season defeat.

Chicago remains at 99 losses, as does general manager Darrien Clay, who has managed the franchise since its founding in 2012.

Brooklyn wasn't nearly as fortunate.

The Bolts fall to 0-5, matching their worst start in franchise history, previously established in 2022.

Joe Burrow scored 48.50 points, and Malik Nabers contributed 36.10.

But Brooklyn received almost nothing from several other positions.

Austin Ekeler scored 3.40. Kenyon Sadiq managed 0.30. D.J. Moore finished without a point.

The result was another disappointing loss for a franchise that now stands alone as the GLFL's only winless team.

And considering what Brooklyn's general manager said earlier in the week, the future may already be taking priority over the present.

Vegas 155.50, Los Angeles 153.05: A Blockbuster, a Heartbreaker and a First Win

Los Angeles made one of the biggest trades of the season.

Vegas made just enough plays to ruin the debut.

The Knight Hawks defeated the Wildcats 155.50-153.05, securing their first victory of 2026 by just 2.45 points.

Vegas entered Week 5 at 0-4, desperately searching for a way to turn individual production into victories.

Michael Penix Jr. scored 31.80 points. Ja'Marr Chase added 28.20. Derrick Henry contributed 25.00, and D'Andre Swift scored 21.30.

It wasn't a record-breaking performance.

It didn't need to be.

Dave Nguyen's Knight Hawks finally got the result they'd been chasing.

For Los Angeles, the defeat was particularly painful.

Earlier in the week, Liz Bautch completed a blockbuster trade with division rival Tucson, sending Amon-Ra St. Brown, Trey McBride and Bhayshul Tuten to the Sugar Skulls.

In return, Los Angeles acquired James Cook III, Brock Bowers and Carnell Tate.

And here's the remarkable part.

In their first week following the trade, the two three-player packages produced almost identical fantasy totals.

Six players. Two franchises. One blockbuster.

A difference of just 0.70 points.

And yet the outcomes couldn't have been more different.

Tucson won by 77.80.

Los Angeles lost by 2.45.

Cook and Bowers combined for 57.80 points in their Wildcats debut.

Josh Allen added 47.60.

But Romeo Doubs scored just 3.60, Roman Wilson finished with zero, and Tate managed only 1.60.

Los Angeles received strong performances from two of its three new acquisitions.

It simply didn't receive enough from the rest of the lineup.

The Wildcats fall to 1-4.

Vegas improves to 1-4.

And while it's far too early to declare a winner of the Tucson-Los Angeles trade, the first week offered a fascinating lesson.

Sometimes a blockbuster changes everything.

Sometimes it changes almost nothing.

And sometimes you lose by 2.45 points anyway.

Milwaukee 173.70, Philadelphia 169.90: The Samaje Perine Game

Last week, Milwaukee scored 278.60 points.

This week, the Mustangs scored 173.70.

Both games count as victories.

And considering the circumstances, this one might have been more satisfying than anyone expected.

Milwaukee entered Week 5 with serious concerns about its running back depth.

Kenneth Walker III was on a bye. Saquon Barkley was doubtful. Jadarian Price was on injured reserve.

That left Michael Bautch turning to Samaje Perine.

It wasn't exactly the lineup decision Milwaukee had envisioned entering the season.

But Perine delivered.

The veteran running back scored 23.55 fantasy points, finishing ninth among running backs for the week and second among Milwaukee's starters.

Jared Goff led the Mustangs with 50.65 points.

Justin Jefferson returned to the starting lineup after missing the previous week, contributing 21.80.

Javonte Williams added 18.20.

But Milwaukee needed nearly every point.

Philadelphia received 40.55 from C.J. Stroud, 27.40 from Garrett Wilson, 27.30 from Drake London and 22.80 from Sam LaPorta.

The Soul pushed the Mustangs to the finish.

Milwaukee escaped with a 173.70-169.90 victory, a margin of just 3.80 points.

The Mustangs improve to 4-1 and have now won four consecutive games since their opening-week defeat.

Philadelphia falls to 3-2.

Milwaukee didn't break any scoring records this week.

The Mustangs didn't overwhelm their opponent.

They simply found a way to win with a depleted running back group.

Sometimes that's what championship contenders do.

And sometimes the player you were worried about starting turns out to be the reason you're celebrating.

Samaje Perine, everybody.

A Trade Deadline Mentality in Week 5?

Cleveland general manager Josh Thurmer apparently had a busy week.

Before the Gladiators' dramatic victory over Cedar Rapids, Thurmer announced his intentions in the league's group chat.

"I'm sliding into inboxes today. So if you get a trade offer from me, go ahead and hit accept."

He wasn't kidding.

Cleveland completed a trade with Northern Arizona, acquiring Matthew Golden and Kyle Pitts in exchange for Rome Odunze and Hunter Henry.

But according to information obtained by GLFL Network, Thurmer was also pursuing considerably larger transactions.

One proposal sent to Tucson would have brought Trey McBride and Jahmyr Gibbs to Cleveland.

In exchange, the Gladiators offered Puka Nacua, Ashton Jeanty and Tucker Kraft.

A source familiar with the discussions indicated Tucson gave the proposal serious consideration.

Ultimately, no deal was completed.

And after Nacua's 47.70-point Monday-night performance delivered Cleveland's 100th regular-season victory, Thurmer may be particularly grateful that the receiver remained on his roster.

Another proposal reportedly involved Brooklyn.

Cleveland offered Ashton Jeanty and Cooper Kupp in exchange for D.J. Moore and Davante Adams.

There is no confirmation that Brooklyn seriously considered accepting the proposal.

In fact, Bolts general manager Cory Colvin offered a rather revealing response to the week's trade activity.

"No need to send me any, I'm going down with this ship. If this stays a keeper league and I'm already 0-4 I'm thinking more about next season than this one."

That statement came before Brooklyn suffered its fifth consecutive defeat.

Colvin stopped short of formally declaring a rebuilding effort.

But his comments suggest the franchise may already be weighing the value of future seasons against the increasingly difficult circumstances of 2026.

Five weeks into the season, Brooklyn is 0-5 and its projected playoff probability has fallen to zero.

The Bolts are the only winless franchise remaining.

And their general manager was already talking about next season before the latest loss.

It's not the kind of October conversation Brooklyn fans were hoping to have.

Three Teams, One Historic Scoring Pace

The GLFL has seen some extraordinary offenses over its first 14 seasons.

But five weeks into 2026, something unprecedented is developing.

Tucson, Milwaukee and Miami are all averaging more than 200 fantasy points per game.

If those averages hold, all three would establish scoring paces beyond the league's previous single-season benchmark.

No team has finished a GLFL season averaging more than 200 points per game.

This season, three franchises are simultaneously threatening to become the first.

Tucson leads the group, followed by Milwaukee and Miami.

The Sugar Skulls have now produced two of the most remarkable scoring performances of the young season.

Milwaukee has broken the single-team scoring record twice in three weeks.

And Miami has scored 220.80 and 243.65 in consecutive victories over Tucson and Northern Arizona.

It's still early.

Nine regular-season weeks remain.

But the possibility of three teams breaking the same historical scoring benchmark in a single season is becoming one of the defining stories of 2026.

Five weeks into the 14-week regular season, the GLFL has passed the one-third mark.

And the playoff projections are beginning to reveal a significant divide.

Tucson, Milwaukee, Miami, Cedar Rapids and Boston all have projected playoff probabilities above 90 percent.

At the opposite end of the standings, four franchises have fallen below 10 percent.

But the direction is unmistakable.

Five teams are establishing themselves as strong postseason favorites.

Four others are facing increasingly difficult paths to January.

And the remaining franchises are fighting for position in the middle of the standings.

There's still plenty of football left.

But the margin for error is getting smaller every week.
